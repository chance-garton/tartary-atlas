Tartary in the historical record

What did the people who went there write down?

Travellers, envoys, captives, monks and chroniclers described the lands that European maps called Tartary. This atlas collects their words one passage at a time. Every passage says how the author knew, and links to the page it comes from.

One passage from the record
BuildingsPontic-Caspian steppe1253

A house thirty feet wide, pulled by twenty-two oxen

Some Mongol houses were never taken down but rode on huge carts. Rubruck measured one cart himself and counted the oxen. The axle was as thick as a ship's mast and a driver stood in the doorway of the house. On the next pages he describes felt-covered chests on carts and a rich man's camp of one or two hundred carts.

I myself once measured the width between the wheel-tracks of a cart xx feet, and when the house was on the cart it projected beyond the wheels on either side v feet at least. I have myself counted to one cart xxii oxen drawing one house, eleven abreast across the width of the cart, and the other eleven before them.

Who Tartars (Mongols of the Golden Horde)

Saw itWilliam of Rubruck (translated by W. W. Rockhill, 1900)

Keep in mind. Rockhill thinks these carts were exceptionally large ones.
