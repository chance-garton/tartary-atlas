Map · Chinese

Hua yi tu (Map of China and the Barbarians), stone stele of 1136 at Xi’an: 20th-century rubbing at LOC

華夷圖

anonymous (Song), stele carved 1136

How we know

compilation Assembled from earlier sources. The author did not observe it directly.

retrospective compilation: Song map derived from Jia Dan's Tang-era Hainei Hua yi tu (801), with marginal texts on the foreign peoples of each direction

Research notes
  • Rubbing date uncertain (1933?); the stele shares its stone with the Yu ji tu on the reverse. Marginal texts require higher zoom.
  • Title 華夷圖 read at top centre; 北/東/西/南 orientation labels; Great Wall drawn along the top.

Why it matters

The earliest surviving Chinese map that frames the northern and western peoples as the Yi beyond the Wall; its margins summarize the states of the north (Liao, Jurchen) as of 1136, a Chinese counterpart to Western Tartary framing.
