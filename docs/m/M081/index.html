Map · Latin · European cartography

New and complete description of Russia, Muscovy and Tartary, London 1562: the sole surviving original impression, Wroclaw University Library

Nova absolutaque Russiae, Moscoviae et Tartariae descriptio

Anthony Jenkinson (author); Clement Adams (engraver, per literature); London 1562

How we know

mixed Part firsthand observation, part drawn from other sources.

Card wording

Mixed: firsthand for Jenkinson's Volga-Caspian-Bukhara route of 1558-1560 (W044); informant testimony for Colmack, Kirgessi, Molgomzaia, Kitaia and Cathay (the legends state that the information was obtained from merchants and Tartars).

Cautions
  • Only known impression; Ortelius (1570), De Jode (1578) and later maps derive from it. The Wroclaw copy is larger and more pictorial than the Ortelius plate; differences between them are themselves evidence of editorial reduction.
  • Found via Europeana search 'Russiae Moscoviae et Tartariae descriptio' (17 hits).

Why it matters

The unique surviving 1562 original, identified in Wroclaw in 1987: it lets the inventory compare Jenkinson's own sheet with the Ortelius 1570 reduction (next record) and the 1914 Russian reproduction (earlier record), resolving the transmission chain for the most influential 16th-century 'Tartaria' map.
