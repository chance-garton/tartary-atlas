Map · Latin · European cartography

World map (mappamundi) and portolan charts by Pietro Vesconte in a Sanudo manuscript, Vatican Pal. lat. 1362 pt. A

Land- und Seekarten (Portolane) [catalogue title]; mappamundi

Pietro Vesconte (cartographer), maps accompanying Marino Sanudo's Liber secretorum fidelium crucis

How we know

compilation Assembled from earlier sources. The author did not observe it directly.

Card wording

Compilation: portolan-chart coastlines combined with a circular mappamundi of Asia drawn from written sources (Sanudo's crusading treatise), with early Mongol-era information; no observation of Asia by the maker.

Cautions
  • Catalogue (DigiVatLib) attributes the maps to Pietro Vesconte; several Sanudo manuscripts (BL Add MS 27376, Paris, Vatican) carry variants of the same map set, so this copy is one witness among siblings rather than a unique original. Shelfmark on DigiVatLib must be written 'MSS_Pal.lat.1362.pt.A' (other forms return 404).
  • The BL copy Add MS 27376 could not be checked (BL digitised manuscripts offline since 2023).

Why it matters

The Vesconte-Sanudo mappamundi is the first Western world map made after the Mongol conquests with a portolan-style frame; it shows the Latin West's early 14th-century picture of Asia at the moment 'Tartari' entered European geography (Sanudo's text discusses Tartar alliances). A companion to the BL Add MS 27376 copy.
