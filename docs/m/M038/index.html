Hereford Mappa Mundi

Mappa Mundi (Hereford)

Richard of Haldingham and Lafford (named in the map's inscription); English workshop

How we know

compilation Assembled from earlier sources. The author did not observe it directly.

Card wording

Retrospective compilation of classical and patristic geography (Orosius, Isidore, Solinus, Pliny) and legends; no observation of Asia.

Cautions
  • Inscription names Richard de Haldingham e de Lafford; authorship, date and place of manufacture (Lincoln vs Hereford) remain debated in the literature. Derived from the same Orosian tradition as the Ebstorf and Psalter maps rather than from any travel report.
  • The official site's 'exploration' feature is thematic (myths, beasts, peoples, towns) with 500 px feature crops under /admin/resources/; there is no zoomable tile viewer exposed to a non-browser client.

Why it matters

The largest surviving medieval mappa mundi (c.1300): shows the pre-Tartary Latin picture of northern Asia (Scythia, Gog and Magog walled by Alexander, cannibal Essedones) that the 16th-century 'Tartaria' maps inherited; contemporary with the first Mongol-era reports but without Mongol content, which is itself evidence of transmission lag.
