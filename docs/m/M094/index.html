Map room / M094
Map · Arabic

Kitab surat al-ard (al-Masalik wa-l-mamalik) with 20 Balkhi-school maps: BnF Arabe 2214, copied 1445-1446

كتاب صورة الأرض / المسالك والممالك

Ibn Hawqal (fl. 943-988)

How we know

mixed Part firsthand observation, part drawn from other sources.

mixed: Ibn Hawqal revised Istakhri's text and maps after his own travels (943-973), including the Caspian, Khazar and Transoxiana chapters

Research notes
  • BnF record titles the work Kitab ajib al-shan fi ahwal al-buldan and Kitab hayat ashkal al-ard; the manuscript is a 1445 copy. Existing W039 (Ouseley translation) actually renders a Persian Istakhri, a known misattribution; this record does not resolve that but is the Ibn Hawqal witness.
  • Better Ibn Hawqal map copies (Topkapi A.3346, 1086; Istanbul) are not open online. Digitized from microfilm R 3061.

Why it matters

Ibn Hawqal's tenth-century regional maps are the Muslim base picture of the Khazar Sea, Khwarazm and the Turkic frontier that later Islamic and, through Idrisi, some European maps inherit; W039 in the existing inventory (Ouseley 1800 English) is a translation of a Persian abridgement, so this is the Arabic original with maps.
