The Selden Map of China (Bodleian MS. Selden supra 105): Chinese merchant map of East and Southeast Asian shipping routes, c.1619-1629

東西洋航海圖 (modern title)

anonymous Chinese (Fujian) merchant cartographer

How we know

firsthand The author saw the places or people described.

firsthand mercantile compilation of sea routes from Quanzhou with a China interior copied from Ming printed maps

Research notes
  • Date range disputed (1607-1629); unique object; the China interior copied from a Ming printed map (probably a Guang yu tu derivative).

Why it matters

Included as the best open example of non-official Chinese mapping of the early 17th century; its interest for the Tartary question is negative: it shows a maritime Chinese world in which the northern steppe is a blank margin, so it calibrates what Ming merchants did and did not map.
