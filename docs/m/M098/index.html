Kharidat al-aja’ib (The Pearl of Wonders): circular world map on fol. 6b, Library of Congress Arabic manuscript SM-3 (16th century)

خريدة العجائب وفريدة الغرائب

Ibn al-Wardi (d. 1457; older attribution to Zayn al-Din Ibn al-Wardi, d. 1349)

How we know

compilation Assembled from earlier sources. The author did not observe it directly.

Card wording

compilation: a fifteenth-century Mamluk-era digest of Ibn al-Wardi, al-Qazwini and earlier geographers; the map is the standard late-medieval Islamic schematic world map with Gog and Magog enclosure

Cautions
  • Authorship contested between the two Ibn al-Wardis (LOC lists both). LOC segment numbering skips two images (0027-0028), so folio-to-segment mapping is offset: fol. 6b = segment 0029. Egyptian copy per LOC note.
  • Labels read: بحيرة خوارزم, ماوراء النهر, بلاد المغرب, ارض الحبشة; المشرق (left) and المغرب (right) margins; south-up orientation. Gog and Magog enclosure in the lower-left (north-east) quadrant as in other Kharidat copies.

Why it matters

A widely copied late-medieval Islamic world map that places the Turks and Khwarazm on the northern rim beside the Gog and Magog wall; it shows the schematic frame through which Ottoman and Mamluk readers of the 15th-17th centuries pictured Inner Asia, in contrast to the Balkhi and Idrisi maps.

Places named
