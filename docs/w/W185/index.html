Written source · Russian · Russian and Siberian

Yarliks (charters) of the Golden Horde khans to the Russian clergy, in Russian translation of the 14th-15th century, with Grigoriev 1842 study of their authenticity

Ярлыки, сиречь жалованные грамоты, иже давали первые цари Ордынские митрополитам Киевским и всея Руси (О достоверности ярлыков, данных ханами Золотой Орды русскому духовенству)

Khans of the Golden Horde (Mengu-Timur, Uzbek, Taidula, Berdibek, Tulak); ed. V. V. Grigoriev

How we know

official or contemporary record An official document or a record made at the time.

Card wording

official record: khan charters of 1267-1379 preserved in 15th century Russian translation collection; authenticity and dating debated since Grigoriev

Cautions
  • Survive only in Russian translation; two redactions; Grigoriev 1842 argued for authenticity with reservations; later scholarship (Priselkov, Pliguzov) revised dating.

Why it matters

Direct administrative evidence of Horde sovereignty over Rus, in the form the Russian church preserved; the authenticity debate is itself relevant to chronology disputes. vostlit Golden Horde section also hosts Mengu-Timur, Tokhtamysh, Timur-Kutlug and Ulug-Muhammad yarliks and Venetian letters.
