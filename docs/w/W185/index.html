Golden Horde khans' charters to the Russian church

Russian copies of khans' charters (yarliks) to the Orthodox clergy, 1267 to 1379.

Full title Yarliks (charters) of the Golden Horde khans to the Russian clergy, in Russian translation of the 14th-15th century, with Grigoriev 1842 study of their authenticity

Khans of the Golden Horde (Mengu-Timur, Uzbek, Taidula, Berdibek, Tulak); ed. V. V. Grigoriev

Open the original book

Khans of the Golden Horde, among them Mengu-Timur, Uzbek, Taidula, Berdibek and Tulak, issued yarliks (charters) to the Russian Orthodox church. The originals are lost. The texts survive in Old Russian translation, in a collection copied in the fifteenth and sixteenth centuries, and were edited and defended as authentic by V. V. Grigoriev in 1842. The atlas does not hold the text: the corpus knows the work only from its catalogue card and a web text of the 1842 study.

How they knew
Official record
Who “Tartar” means here
Not recorded. The issuers are the khans of the Golden Horde, the state that Russian writers called Tatar.
Why read it
Direct administrative evidence of Horde rule over Rus, in the form the Russian church kept it, and a case study in how a dating dispute is argued.
Watch out
Only translations survive, in two redactions, and later scholars (Priselkov, Pliguzov) revised Grigoriev's dating. The atlas quotes nothing from this work.
