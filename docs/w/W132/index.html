Written source · Chinese · Chinese, Manchu, Mongolian, Tibetan (texts)

Fine Talk from the Jade Hall: Yuan miscellany preserving the Record of an Embassy to the West (Xi shi ji, 1263), the Record of an Embassy to the North (Bei shi ji) and the Journey to Lingbei (Lingbei jixing)

玉堂嘉話 (秋澗集 卷九十三至一百)

Wang Yun (1227-1304), Hanlin academician; preserving the travel records of Liu Yu (1259 embassy of Changde to Hülegü in Persia), Wugusun Zhongduan (1220-1221 Jin embassy to Chinggis Khan) and Zhang Dehui (1247-1248 journey to Möngke's court)

How we know

firsthand The author saw the places or people described.

Card wording

firsthand embassy narratives (Changde's 1259 journey to Persia; Wugusun's 1220 mission; Zhang Dehui's 1247 journey to Mongolia) transmitted through Wang Yun's notes

Cautions
  • Transmitted inside Wang Yun's collected works; the Bei shi ji here is the version behind Bretschneider's translation (W112); Lingbei jixing was translated into Russian by Palladius (1867; vostlit) and English by Bretschneider.
  • Bei shi ji chapter relates to W112 (category for that portion: edition of existing).

Why it matters

Chinese originals of Bretschneider's Bei shi ji (W112) and of the Xi shi ji (the only Chinese eyewitness of Hülegü's Persian campaign) and Zhang Dehui's route description from Beijing to the Mongol court: earliest Chinese itineraries across Mongolia with place and tribe names.
