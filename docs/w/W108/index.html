Priscus at the court of Attila

An eyewitness account of a Roman embassy to the camp of Attila the Hun in 448.

Full title Account of the Embassy to Attila

Priscus of Panium

Open the original book

Priscus of Panium was a Roman envoy's companion who went to the court of Attila, king of the Huns, in 448 and later wrote a history in Greek. Only fragments of it survive. The best-known passages describe the journey and Attila's residence, a camp built largely of timber, and a banquet held there. The card lists J. B. Bury's English selection as posted by Fordham University. The atlas does not hold the text of this edition: no copy was harvested into the corpus, so nothing from it was read for this guide.

How they knew
Eyewitness
Who “Tartar” means here
Not recorded, and the word is not expected here. Priscus writes about Huns in the fifth century, and the card warns that Huns are not automatically later Tatars.
Why read it
It is a rare eyewitness look at a steppe ruler's camp and court, useful for seeing what a Roman visitor thought worth describing.
Watch out
The narrative survives only in excerpts. The card also warns against treating the Huns as the same people as later Tatars.
