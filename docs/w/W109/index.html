Tang histories on the Uighur empire

Chinese court histories on the Uighur steppe empire of Mongolia in the eighth and ninth centuries.

Catalogue title Tang History excerpts concerning the Uighurs

Compilers of the Tang histories

The Tang histories were compiled in the tenth and eleventh centuries from earlier court records. They describe the Uighurs, a Turkic people whose steppe empire in Mongolia lasted from the 740s to 840. The card lists excerpts translated by Colin Mackerras, an English selection rather than the complete Old or New Tang History. The atlas does not hold the text of this edition: no copy was harvested into the corpus, so nothing from it was read for this guide.

Who “Tartar” means here
Not recorded. The Tang period is when the name Tatar first appears in Turkic and Chinese records for a group on the steppe, but the card does not say whether these excerpts mention it.
Why read it
It offers a Chinese neighbour's view of a steppe empire and its politics, several centuries before the Mongols.
Watch out
The histories were put together long after the events, and the catalogue entry is a selection. It should not be read as the whole Tang record.
