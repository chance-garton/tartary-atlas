The Old Manchu Chronicles: Grand Secretariat copy (Manchu facsimile with romanised transcription and Chinese translation)

內閣藏本滿文老檔 (Tongki fuka sindaha hergen i dangse)

Old Manchu Archives (Manwen laodang), Grand Secretariat copy; edited and translated by the First Historical Archives of China (Liaoning Nationalities Press, 2009, 20 vols)

How we know

official or contemporary record An official document or a record made at the time.

Card wording

official record: contemporary Manchu-language state annals and documents of Nurhaci and Hong Taiji (1607-1636), recopied with reformed script in 1775-1778

Cautions
  • The Grand Secretariat copy is a 1770s recopy in reformed script with editorial changes; the original old-script archive (Jiu Manzhou dang, Taipei 1969 facsimile) and the Tokyo 1955-63 Manbun roto edition are independent witnesses not located open here. Catalogue metadata on IA is minimal.
  • Harvard-Yenching holds a manuscript Man Han dang ce / Tongki fuka sindaha hergen-i dangse (CURIOSity record 990096065610203941) not yet checked for a scan.

Why it matters

The Manchu state's own earliest records: how the Jurchen leadership named itself, the Mongols and the Ming before adopting the name Manchu (1635) and Qing (1636); indispensable for the transition from 'Jurchen' to 'Manchu Tartars'.
