Nieuhof's manuscript journal of the Dutch embassy to China (1655 to 1657)

The handwritten journal and drawings behind Nieuhof's famous printed book on the Dutch embassy to the Qing court.

Full title Journal of some events on the voyage of the ambassadors Pieter de Goyer and Jacob Keyser to the mighty emperor of China and Tartary in the years 1655-1657 (autograph manuscript with 81 drawings)

Johan Nieuhof

Open the original book

Johan Nieuhof was steward to the Dutch East India Company embassy of Pieter de Goyer and Jacob Keyser, sent to the emperor of China and Tartary in 1655 to 1657, by way of Canton, Nanjing and Peking. This autograph journal, dated 1659, has 229 pages and 81 coloured pen drawings, made for the company directors before the printed book of 1665. It lets a reader separate what Nieuhof saw from what editors added. The atlas does not hold the text: the corpus knows only a Gallica digitisation of the manuscript.

How they knew
Eyewitness
Who “Tartar” means here
The Manchu (Qing) rulers of China, whom Dutch writers of the time called Tartars. The card notes the framing of the Manchu emperor as Tartar Khan.
Why read it
The unedited eyewitness base of the famous 1665 book, including how the Manchu emperor was framed as Tartar Khan.
Watch out
The catalogue lists the language as French, but the manuscript is Dutch. The atlas quotes nothing from this work.
