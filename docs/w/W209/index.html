Abu'l-Ghazi's Genealogy of the Turkmen

A Khivan khan's history of the Turkmen tribes, gathered from oral tradition in 1659 to 1661.

Full title Genealogy of the Turkmen: Russian translation with critical Chagatai text by A. N. Kononov (Moscow-Leningrad 1958)

شجرهٔ تراکمه

Abu'l-Ghazi Bahadur Khan

Open the original book

Abu'l-Ghazi Bahadur Khan of Khiva wrote this in Chagatai Turkic between 1659 and 1661. It joins the Oghuz legend, copied from Rashid al-Din's Oghuz-nama, to Turkmen tribal traditions that he collected from elders, and lists tribes such as the Salur, Teke, Yomut and Ersari. The atlas does not hold the text: the card records A. N. Kononov's 1958 Moscow and Leningrad Russian translation, known as a web transcription, and nothing is stored.

How they knew
Part eyewitness
Who “Tartar” means here
Not recorded. The card sets this apart from Abu'l-Ghazi's other work (W038) and notes that Europeans were mapping the eastern Caspian as Tartary when it was written.
Why read it
Turkmen tribes describing themselves in the 1650s, just as European maps were labelling the eastern Caspian Tartary.
Watch out
The Oghuz legend is copied from Rashid al-Din, and the tribal lists reflect what seventeenth-century informants said. The atlas quotes nothing from this work.

What it says
