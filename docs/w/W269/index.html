Treatise on the commerce of the Black Sea (2 vols, 1787): trade of the Crimea, Kuban, Circassia, Abkhazia and the Danube ports, with an account of the Crimean Tatar khanate’s economy

Charles de Peyssonnel

How we know

firsthand The author saw the places or people described.

Card wording

firsthand observation and official record: Peyssonnel served as consul to the Crimean khan (1753-1757) and at Smyrna; the treatise is drawn from his consular memoranda

Cautions
  • Published thirty years after the Crimean residence; some data refers to the 1750s. Gallica viewer unreachable from this environment during the pass: technical failure, not restriction.
  • Next action: open e-rara landing page and Gallica ark when reachable; check for tome 2.

Why it matters

The most detailed eighteenth-century account of the economy and administration of the Crimean khanate (Little Tartary) just before Russian annexation; useful for what "Tartary" meant on the Black Sea in commercial practice.
