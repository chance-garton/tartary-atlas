Peyssonnel's Treatise on the commerce of the Black Sea (1787)

A French consul's account of the trade and government of the Crimean khanate in the 1750s.

Full title Treatise on the commerce of the Black Sea (2 vols, 1787): trade of the Crimea, Kuban, Circassia, Abkhazia and the Danube ports, with an account of the Crimean Tatar khanate’s economy

Charles de Peyssonnel

Open the original book

Charles de Peyssonnel served as French consul to the Crimean khan from 1753 to 1757 and later at Smyrna. He drew this two-volume treatise, published in 1787, from his consular memoranda. It covers trade in Crimea, the Kuban, Circassia, Abkhazia and the Danube ports, and the economy and administration of the Crimean Tatar khanate shortly before Russia annexed it. The atlas does not hold the text: the corpus knows a Paris 1787 copy only through a Gallica record and an e-rara copy, and nothing is stored.

Who “Tartar” means here
The Crimean Tatars of the khanate, which the card calls Little Tartary, with the Nogais on its borders.
Why read it
The fullest eighteenth-century account of how the Crimean khanate earned its living and was governed, and of what Tartary meant in Black Sea trade.
Watch out
It was published thirty years after his stay, so some figures belong to the 1750s. The atlas quotes nothing from this work.
