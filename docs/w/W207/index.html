Wonders of Destiny in the Vicissitudes of Timur: Arabic editio princeps by Golius (Leiden 1636) and Arabic-Latin edition by Manger (Leeuwarden 1767-1772)

عجائب المقدور في نوائب تيمور

Ahmad ibn Arabshah (1389-1450)

How we know

mixed Part firsthand observation, part drawn from other sources.

Card wording

mixed: author was carried off from Damascus to Samarkand as a boy in 1401, lived in Timurid Central Asia, Khorezm, the Golden Horde (Sarai, Astrakhan, Crimea) and Edirne; writes from memory and informants, hostile to Timur

Cautions
  • IA AAlexandrina-054610 metadata gives 1842 and publisher 'Dar al-Imara'; the title page as OCR'd names Indian maulawis, consistent with the Calcutta 1818 edition or a reprint; verify against a title-page image. Golius's 1636 text has no translation; Manger's Latin is the first.
  • Further copies: archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.80464; vostlit.info/Texts/rus17/Ibn_Arabshah_2/text1.… (Russian, Tiesenhausen 1884); vostlit.info/Texts/rus17/Ibn_Arabshah/text1.ph… (Russian, Tashkent 2007)

Why it matters

Independent, hostile Arabic witness to Timur and to the Golden Horde in the 1400s-1420s by a resident of Sarai and Crimea; his chapters on the Dasht-i Qipchaq, Sarai and Edigu are extracted in Tiesenhausen I (1884).
