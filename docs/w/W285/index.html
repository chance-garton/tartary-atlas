Steller's voyage from Kamchatka to America with Bering

The naturalist's own journal of Bering's 1741 voyage to Alaska and the shipwreck winter on Bering Island.

Full title G. W. Steller’s Journey from Kamchatka to America with Commander-Captain Bering: a companion to his Description of Kamchatka (ed. Pallas)

Georg Wilhelm Steller

Read the 1 passageOpen the original book

Georg Wilhelm Steller sailed with Vitus Bering from Kamchatka in 1741, went ashore on Kayak Island off Alaska for a few hours, and survived the wreck and winter on what became Bering Island, where Bering died. His journal was edited by P. S. Pallas and published in St Petersburg in 1793 as a companion to his description of Kamchatka. It is a sea and natural history narrative; Tartars appear only once, as a point of comparison for arrows Steller found in an Alaskan storage pit. The corpus copy is the 1793 edition from the Bavarian State Library (its catalogue misreads the author as 'Weller').

What it says: 1 passage

Daily lifeKayak Island, AlaskaJuly 1741

Alaskan arrows like those of the Tungus and Tatars

On Kayak Island Steller uncovered a covered storage pit full of smoked fish, sweet grass, bark and seaweed cord. Among the goods were arrows much bigger than Kamchatka ones, which he says came close to the arrows of the Tungus and Tatars and were so smooth and black that the owners must have had iron knives. It is a small sign of how Siberian travellers used Tatar gear as their measuring stick.

Among these I also found some arrows which in size far exceeded those of Kamchatka and came close to the arrows of the Tungus and Tatars, very smoothly scraped and painted black, so that one would certainly suppose they had iron tools and knives.

Working translation

Original wording

Unter diesen fand ich auch einige Pfeile, so an Größe die Kamtschatkischen weit übertrafen und denen Pfeilen der Tungusen und Tataren nahe kamen, sehr glatt geschabet und schwarz angestrichen, daß man allerdings eiserne Instrumente und Messer bey ihnen vermuthen sollte.

Georg Wilhelm Steller (ed. P. S. Pallas, 1793)
