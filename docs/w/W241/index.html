History of the Most Notable Things, Rites and Customs of the Great Kingdom of China (first edition), with chapters on China’s Tartar frontier and wars

Juan González de Mendoza

How we know

compilation Assembled from earlier sources. The author did not observe it directly.

Card wording

compilation: Mendoza never reached China; he assembled Chinese books brought to Mexico, the reports of the Augustinian (Rada 1575) and Franciscan (1579, 1582) missions, and Portuguese accounts (Gaspar da Cruz, Escalante)

Cautions
  • Wholly derivative; borrows extensively (sometimes verbatim) from Gaspar da Cruz 1569 and Martín de Rada; contains the earliest printed Chinese characters in Europe.
  • Further copies: archive.org/details/itinerarioycompe00gonz (1586 Itinerario)

Why it matters

The best-selling sixteenth-century European book on China (46 editions by 1600), fixing the picture of Tartary as the hostile nomad realm beyond the Great Wall and transmitting Rada's and Cruz's data on Chinese history and the Mongol (Yuan) conquest.
