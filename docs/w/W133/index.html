Zhang Dehui's journey to Mongolia, 1247 to 1248

A Chinese envoy's travel notes on the road from Beijing to the Mongol court, in Palladius's 1867 Russian translation.

Full title Travel notes of the Chinese Zhang Dehui during his journey to Mongolia in the first half of the 13th century (Russian translation, 1867)

Путевые записки китайца Чжан Дэ-хой во время путешествия его в Монголию в первой половине XIII столетия

Zhang Dehui; Archimandrite Palladius (Kafarov)

Open the original book

In 1247 to 1248 the Chinese official Zhang Dehui travelled from Beijing north into Mongolia, as far as the Kerulen and the Karakorum region, and wrote notes on the road and its stations. Archimandrite Palladius (Kafarov) translated them into Russian in 1867 and added his own identifications of places. The card lists a web text of that translation on vostlit.info. The atlas does not hold the text of this edition: no copy was harvested into the corpus, so nothing from it was read for this guide.

How they knew
Eyewitness
Who “Tartar” means here
Not recorded. The card names the Mongols of Mongke's court, along with the Onggut and Kitan remnants met on the way. Whether Palladius's Russian uses Tatars is not recorded.
Why read it
It is a firsthand Chinese itinerary to the Mongol homeland from the same decade as Carpini and Rubruck, giving a route and stations from the Chinese side.
Watch out
Palladius's translation of 1867 predates modern editions such as Bretschneider 1888, and the web text lacks the page numbers of the printed notes.

What it says

No quotes from this one yet. Its text is out of reach for now, or the scan could not be read.
