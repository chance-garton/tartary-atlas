Qadir Ali Beg's Compendium of Chronicles in Tatar

The earliest surviving history written in Volga Tatar, made at the Kasimov court in 1602.

Full title Compendium of Chronicles: Tatar (Nogay-Turkic) translation of parts of Rashid al-Din with an original continuation on the Jochid khans (Urus Khan, Toqtamish, Edigu, Kazan and Kasimov rulers) dedicated to Uraz Muhammad Khan of Kasimov, 1602; manuscript copy dated 20 March 1842

جامع التواریخ (ترجمهٔ ترکی جامع رشیدی)

Qadir Ali Beg Jalayir (fl. 1602), of the Kasimov court

Open the original book

Qadir Ali Beg Jalayir, of the court of Uraz Muhammad Khan at Kasimov, made a Tatar (Nogay-Turkic) version of parts of Rashid al-Din's history in 1602. He added his own continuation on the Jochid houses: Urus Khan, Toqtamish, Edigu and the rulers of Kazan and Kasimov. About two thirds is translation. The atlas does not hold the text: the corpus knows a copy dated 1842 at the Bibliothèque nationale de France only through Gallica catalogue records, and Berezin's 1854 Kazan print was not found online.

Who “Tartar” means here
The author writes in Tatar (Nogay-Turkic), and his subjects are the Tatar khanates that followed the Golden Horde: Kazan, Kasimov and the Nogays.
Why read it
The native record of the post-Horde khanates that Muscovy absorbed, in the earliest surviving Volga Tatar history.
Watch out
The 1842 date is the date of the copy, not of writing, and two thirds of the text translates Rashid al-Din. The atlas quotes nothing from this work.

What it says

No quotes from this one yet. Its text is out of reach for now, or the scan could not be read.

The fine print

How we know

compilation Assembled from earlier sources. The author did not observe it directly.

compilation (translation of Rashid al-Din) plus original dastans on the fifteenth-sixteenth-century Jochid houses from Nogay and Kazakh oral tradition and the author's own experience at Kasimov

Research notes
  • Two-thirds is a translation of Rashid al-Din; the 1842 date is the copy date, not the composition. Catalogue language code 'nog' reflects the BnF cataloguer's identification as Nogay Tatar.
  • Berezin's 1854 edition and the Kazan 1851 lithograph (Sbornik letopisei, tatarskii tekst) are the printed leads; try rusneb.ru or Kazan Federal University digital library from another environment.

Why it matters

The earliest surviving historical work in Volga Tatar, the native record of the post-Horde khanates (Kazan, Kasimov, Nogays) that Muscovy absorbed; the text behind Berezin's Kazan 1854 print (Sbornik letopisei, Biblioteka vostochnykh istorikov II, 1), which was not found online.
