al-Umari's Routes of Insight: a Mamluk encyclopedia of the known world

A Cairo chancery official's world encyclopedia of c.1340 to 1349; this copy is the first volume only, on general geography.

Full title Routes of Insight into the Realms of the Metropolises: chapters on the kingdom of the Tatars (Golden Horde, Chaghadaids, Ilkhanids), Russian translation with Arabic text in Tiesenhausen’s 1884 collection

مسالك الأبصار في ممالك الأمصار

al-Umari (1301-1349), Mamluk chancery official

Read the 4 passagesOpen the original book

Ibn Fadl Allah al-Umari (1301 to 1349) served in the Mamluk chancery in Cairo and Damascus. His vast encyclopedia gathers geography and history, with famous chapters on the Mongol states (the Golden Horde, the Chaghadaids, the Ilkhans) built from merchants and envoys he questioned. The corpus copy is volume 1 of Ahmad Zaki Pasha's Cairo edition (Dar al-Kutub, 1924), which covers the earth, climes, seas, rivers and mountains. The Mongol chapters (in later volumes, and in Tiesenhausen's 1884 Russian extracts) are not in this volume.

How they knew
Compiled from others
Who “Tartar” means here
al-Tatar or al-Tatar (Tatars) means the Mongols and their subjects, especially the Ilkhanid enemies of the Mamluks; 'the land of the Tatars' is a broad region of inner Asia between China and Fergana. The steppe of the Golden Horde is 'the deserts of the Qipchaq'.
Why read it
Brief but telling glimpses: the sultan hailed as conqueror of the Tatars, a mountain chain crossing 'the land of the Tatars', the Volga as the river of Sarai, and old Gog and Magog lore about ruined lands in the far north-east.
Watch out
This volume is general geography, largely copied from earlier Arabic writers; the rich informant-based chapters on the Golden Horde are elsewhere and were not read.

CitiesVolga river and Sarai (Astrakhan oblast)c.1340

The Volga, 'the river on which the city of Sarai stands'

Among them is the river Athil. It is the river on which the city of al-Sarai is set. Its source is a spring welling up at the foot of Mount Qaquna; then it turns south, heading west through the deserts of the Qipchaq, north of the silver mines, until it flows into the Sea of Tabaristan [the Caspian].

Working translation

Original wording

ومن ذلك نهر أثيل . وهو المركب عليه مدينة السراى . ومخرجه من عين تنبع فى ذيل جبل قاقونا ، ثم يقتبل الجنوب آخذا بغرب فى صحارى القبجاق على شمالى معادن الفضة ، حتى يصب فى بحر طبرستان

Editorial context

In his list of rivers al-Umari describes the Athil (Volga) as the river on which the city of al-Sarai is set, flowing through 'the deserts of the Qipchaq' into the Caspian. Sarai was then the capital of the Golden Horde, with which the Mamluks were allied.

al-Umari (Arabic text ed. Ahmad Zaki Pasha, Cairo 1924)

The nameInner Asia, from China to Ferganac.1340

A mountain chain that crosses 'the land of the Tatars'

Its beginning is in the east, in China, at the Encircling Ocean. It cuts across the land of the Tatars, along its mines, until it reaches Farghana.

Working translation

Original wording

فإن أوله بالمشرق من الصين من البحر المحيط . فيقطع بلاد التتر على معادنها إلى أن يأتى فرغانة

Editorial context

Tracing the great mountain chain that ends in Syria, al-Umari starts it in China at the encircling ocean and says it cuts across 'the land of the Tatars' along its mines before reaching Fergana. Here 'land of the Tatars' is simply the Mamluk name for Mongol-ruled inner Asia, much as Europeans used 'Tartary'.

al-Umari (Arabic text ed. Ahmad Zaki Pasha, Cairo 1924)

The nameCairoc.1340

The sultan who 'vanquished the Tatars'

vanquisher of the Rum, the Franks, the Georgians, the Armenians and the Tatars, sultan of the wide earth

Working translation

Original wording

هازم الروم والفرنج والكرج والأرمن والتتار ، سلطان البسيطه

Editorial context

In the dedication al-Umari praises the Mamluk sultan al-Nasir Muhammad ibn Qalawun as king of Arabs, Persians and Turks and 'vanquisher of the Rum, the Franks, the Georgians, the Armenians and the Tatars'. For a Mamluk official the Tatars are one of the great enemy peoples, set beside the Crusaders.

al-Umari (Arabic text ed. Ahmad Zaki Pasha, Cairo 1924)

Strange talesFar north-east of the seventh climec.1340

Lands ruined by the raids of Gog and Magog

Its beginning on the eastern side is a stretch peopled by Gog and Magog, within the Barrier … They say: as for the Rus lands, they are flourishing and inhabited, and so are those of the Ankariyya; but the land of Sisaban was once flourishing, populated and settled, then fell to ruin long ago because of the raids of Gog and Magog upon them.

Working translation

Original wording

فأوله من جهة الشرق قطعة معمورة بيأجوج ومأجوج ، فيما هو داخل السد … قالوا : فأما الروسية ، فعامرة آهلة . وكذلك الأنكرية . وأما بلاد سيسبان ، فقد كانت عامرة آهلة مسكونة ، ثم خربت من قديم ، لإغارات يأجوج ومأجوج عليهم

Editorial context

Describing the far north, al-Umari says the eastern end is a region peopled by Gog and Magog within the Barrier (the wall). He reports that the Rus lands and the land of the Ankariyya (Hungarians) are inhabited, but that the land of Sisaban was once flourishing and populated and fell to ruin long ago because Gog and Magog raided it.

al-Umari (Arabic text ed. Ahmad Zaki Pasha, Cairo 1924)

The fine print

How we know

informant testimony Written down from what informants reported.

informant testimony gathered in Cairo and Damascus from merchants and envoys (e.g. the merchant al-Isfahani on the Chaghadaids, Shaykh Ala al-Din al-Birzali on Sarai); compiled c.1340

Research notes
  • Klaus Lech's 1968 Wiesbaden edition of the Mongol chapters (Das mongolische Weltreich) not open online; the full Abu Dhabi Arabic edition exists on IA (masalik-al-absar-... uploads) but was not verified volume by volume.

Why it matters

The richest fourteenth-century description of the Golden Horde as a state (extent, towns, revenues, Uzbek Khan's court) and of the Chaghadaid ulus; the Arabic basis of later Mamluk encyclopaedias (Nuwayri, Qalqashandi).
