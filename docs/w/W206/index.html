Written source · Russian · Persian, Arabic, Ottoman, Chagatai and Tatar

Routes of Insight into the Realms of the Metropolises: chapters on the kingdom of the Tatars (Golden Horde, Chaghadaids, Ilkhanids), Russian translation with Arabic text in Tiesenhausen’s 1884 collection

مسالك الأبصار في ممالك الأمصار

al-Umari (1301-1349), Mamluk chancery official

How we know

informant testimony Written down from what informants reported.

Card wording

informant testimony gathered in Cairo and Damascus from merchants and envoys (e.g. the merchant al-Isfahani on the Chaghadaids, Shaykh Ala al-Din al-Birzali on Sarai); compiled c.1340

Cautions
  • Klaus Lech's 1968 Wiesbaden edition of the Mongol chapters (Das mongolische Weltreich) not open online; the full Abu Dhabi Arabic edition exists on IA (masalik-al-absar-... uploads) but was not verified volume by volume.

Why it matters

The richest fourteenth-century description of the Golden Horde as a state (extent, towns, revenues, Uzbek Khan's court) and of the Chaghadaid ulus; the Arabic basis of later Mamluk encyclopaedias (Nuwayri, Qalqashandi).
