Herodotus on the Scythians (Histories, Book IV)

The Greek historian's fifth-century BCE portrait of the steppe peoples north of the Black Sea.

Full title Histories, especially Book IV

Herodotus

Open the original book

Herodotus wrote his Histories around 440 BCE. Book IV describes Scythia, the steppe north of the Black Sea, with its peoples, customs, rivers and neighbours. The card calls it travel-informed history plus received reports, so part is his own looking and part is what traders and locals told him. Later writers sometimes linked the Tartars to the older steppe peoples, which is why it appears here as background. The card lists G. C. Macaulay's English translation on Project Gutenberg. The atlas does not hold the text of this edition: no copy was harvested into the corpus, so nothing from it was read for this guide.

How they knew
Part eyewitness
Who “Tartar” means here
The word Tartar is not expected, since Herodotus wrote about Scythians and their neighbours long before the Tartars were known in Europe. The card treats Scythia as background only.
Why read it
It is the classic ancient description of steppe life, so a reader can compare what later travellers reported with what a Greek wrote about the same grasslands.
Watch out
The card warns that Scythians are not proof that later Tartars were the same people. Herodotus mixes what he saw with what he was told.
