Vassaf's History: the Mongol Ilkhans of Iran and their rivals

A Persian court historian's ornate chronicle of the Mongol rulers of Iran and the other Mongol khanates, 1257 to 1328.

Catalogue title History of Vassaf: Ilkhanid history 1257-1328 continuing Juvaini, with sections on the Golden Horde, the Chaghadaids and the Yuan; BULAC manuscript copy dated 1256 AH / 1840 and Persian printed text on Internet Archive

تجزیة الامصار و تزجیة الاعصار (تاریخ وصاف)

Vassaf of Shiraz (1264-c.1330)

Vassaf (Shihab al-Din Abdallah of Shiraz, 1264 to c.1330) was a tax official who presented his history to the Ilkhans Ghazan and Uljaytu. It continues Juvaini from 1257 and covers the Ilkhans' wars with the Golden Horde and the Chaghadaids, the Yuan court in China and India. He knew Iran at first hand and relied on envoys and merchants for the other Mongol states. The corpus holds three BULAC manuscripts: MS.PERS.116 (1670), MS.PERS.64 (1856) and MS.PERS.65 (1840, part only). All are in nastaliq script, and the machine OCR of all three is unreadable.

How they knew
Part eyewitness
Who “Tartar” means here
Vassaf writes of the Mughul (Mongols) and their uluses; European readers later called the same rulers the Tartars of Persia. No passage could be read in this corpus copy to show his own usage.
Why read it
It is the main contemporary Persian narrative for the Ilkhanid court c.1300 and one of the few Persian sources on the Qipchaq steppe wars. Readers should use a printed edition or translation.
Watch out
The OCR of all three manuscripts is illegible and the handwriting cannot be searched, so no passage could be checked or quoted. Vassaf's prose is famously ornate even in good editions; the Bombay 1853 lithograph and the Hammer-Purgstall German translation of volume 1 (1856) are the usual ways in.

Cautions
  • Vassaf's rhetorical prose makes readings difficult; the Bombay lithograph is the only complete printed text and is uncritical. BULAC holds three copies (MS.PERS.64, 65, 116).

Why it matters

Ilkhanid narrative of the wars between Hulegu's line and the Jochids and Chaghadaids; ornate but chronologically valuable for the Qipchaq steppe c.1260-1320.
