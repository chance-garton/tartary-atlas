The Laurentian Chronicle: the Mongol storm over Suzdal Rus

The oldest surviving Russian chronicle codex (copied 1377), with year-by-year entries on the Tatar conquest of Vladimir-Suzdal and life under the Horde to 1305.

Catalogue title Laurentian Chronicle (Complete Collection of Russian Chronicles, vol. 1), including the Primary Chronicle and Suzdal continuation to 1305

Лаврентьевская летопись (Полное собрание русских летописей, т. 1)

Anonymous Rus chroniclers; monk Lavrentii (copyist, 1377); Archaeographic Commission edition

Read the 14 passagesOpen the original book

The monk Lavrentii copied this chronicle in 1377 for Prince Dmitrii of Suzdal-Nizhnii Novgorod. It opens with the Primary Chronicle and continues with the annals of Vladimir-Suzdal, some of them written close to the events. For the Tatars it records the first appearance of 'unknown peoples' in 1223, the fall of the Volga Bulgar capital in 1236, the winter campaign of 1237 to 1238 against Riazan, Moscow, Vladimir and Suzdal, princes' journeys to Batu and the Great Khan, the census of 1257 and revolts against Muslim tax farmers. The corpus copy quoted here is the 1846 first edition (PSRL vol. 1), which also prints a continuation headed 'Troitskaia letopis'; the 1926 to 1928 second edition is in the corpus too.

How they knew
Official record
Who “Tartar” means here
The Mongol armies of Batu and the later Golden Horde: its khans ('tsars'), envoys, census takers and tax officials. The chronicler also calls them godless, pagans, Ishmaelites and 'Taurmens', and calls the Muslim tax farmers who came with them 'besermen'.
Why read it
This is the Suzdal side of the conquest in the words of churchmen who lived through it or wrote soon after: dated sieges, named towns, palisades and catapults, the census and the first protests against Horde tax farming. There is nothing here about a Tartar empire building cities in Rus; the Tatars arrive as outsiders who burn them.
Watch out
The chroniclers read the invasion as God's punishment and use hostile religious language. The 1377 codex has lost leaves (for example 1263 to 1283), so some stories begin or end in mid-sentence. The OCR of the 1846 printing is damaged; quotes have been normalized letter by letter (restoring the old letter yat) against the scan.

CitiesRyazanWinter 1237 (6745)

Riazan: through the forest in winter

The Tatars came 'through the forest' in winter, ravaged the Riazan land as far as Pronsk, burned Riazan and killed its prince. The chronicler describes what they did to captives: some were impaled or stretched, others shot with arrows, others tied with their hands behind their backs. Then the army moved on to Kolomna.

the godless Tatars came from the eastern lands onto the Riazan land, through the forest, and began to make war on the Riazan land, and captured as far as Pronsk; having captured all Riazan they burned it, and killed their prince; of those they seized, some they stretched out, others they shot through with arrows, and others they bound with their hands behind them

CitiesVladimirFebruary 1238

Vladimir besieged: scaffolds, catapults and a palisade

After parading the captured prince Vladimir Iurevich before the Golden Gate so his brothers on the walls could see him, the Tatars camped around the city. On the Saturday before Lent they built scaffolds and set up 'poroki' (stone-throwing engines), and in one night surrounded the whole city with a timber palisade. The next morning they attacked. It is a precise picture of Mongol siege method.

On Meatfare Saturday they began to prepare scaffolds and set up siege engines until evening, and in the night they fenced the whole city of Vladimir round with a palisade; on Meatfare Sunday after matins they advanced on the city, on the 7th of February, the feast of the holy martyr Theodore Stratelates.

Original wording

CitiesVladimir-SuzdalFebruary 1238

Fourteen towns in one February

After Vladimir the army split up: some went to Rostov, some to Iaroslavl, some along the Volga to Gorodets and Galich, others to Pereiaslavl and as far as Torzhok. The chronicler sums up: hardly a village was left untouched, and fourteen towns fell in the single month of February, not counting 'slobodas' (settlements) and 'pogosts' (parish centres).

and from there they captured all that country and many towns, as far as Torzhok, and there is no place, nor village, and few hamlets, where they did not make war in the Suzdal land; and they took 14 towns, besides slobodas and pogosts, in the one month of February

CitiesKozelskSpring 1238

Kozelsk, the 'evil city'

Batu turned back from the road to Novgorod and besieged the small town of Kozelsk, whose prince was a boy. The townsmen held out for seven weeks, sallied out, cut up the siege engines and killed three sons of a 'temnik' (commander of ten thousand). The chronicle says that afterwards the Tatars would not call it Kozelsk but 'the evil city'.

From then on among the Tatars they did not dare to call it Kozelsk, but called it the evil city, because they had fought at that town for seven weeks, and three sons of a temnik were killed; the Tatars searched for them and did not find them among the multitude of dead bodies.

CitiesVolga BulgariaAutumn 1236 (6744)

The Great City of the Volga Bulgars burned

A year before they reached Rus, the Tatars came 'from the eastern lands' and took the 'glorious Great City' of the Volga Bulgars, a Muslim trading state on the middle Volga. The chronicler says they killed everyone, took a great quantity of goods and burned the city. The Great City is usually identified by historians with Bilyar.

That same autumn the godless Tatars came from the eastern lands into the Bulgar land, and took the glorious Great City of the Bulgars, and slew them with weapons from old man to youth to the infant at the breast, and took a great quantity of goods, and burned their city with fire, and captured all their land.

Working translation

Original wording

Тое же осени придоша отъ всточныя страны въ Болгарьскую землю безбожніи Татари, и взяша славный Великый городъ Болгарьскый, и избиша оружьемъ отъ старца и до унаго и до сущаго младенца, и взяша товара множство, а городъ ихъ пожгоша огнемъ, и всю землю ихъ плѣниша.

Laurentian Chronicle, PSRL vol. 1 (1846)

CitiesKursk1284 (6792)

The baskak's two settlements empty overnight

Akhmat had founded two 'slobodas' (tax-free trading settlements) in the Kursk land and left two brothers to guard and fortify them. After a Rus prince ambushed their men, the brothers fled to Kursk and on the next day the whole of both settlements scattered. It is a rare glimpse of settlements founded under Horde protection inside Rus, and of how fragile they were.

That same year, after Easter on St Thomas Sunday, those two besermen brothers fled to Kursk, and on the morrow, Monday, all that sloboda ran away, and the other one too; and so both besermen slobodas dispersed.

Original wording

Того же лѣта, по Велицѣ дни въ недѣлю Фомину, побѣгоста 2 братеника та бесерменина къ Курьску, а на заутрѣи въ понедѣльникъ побѣже вся свобода та, и другая; и тако розоидошася обѣ свободѣ бесерменьскиѣ.

BuildingsVladimir7 February 1238

The stone cathedral that became a furnace

When the new town fell, the bishop, the grand prince's wife, daughters-in-law, grandchildren, boyars and townspeople shut themselves in the stone Cathedral of the Mother of God, built by the Suzdal princes. The Tatars set it on fire. Then they forced the doors, killed the survivors and stripped the wonder-working icon of its gold, silver and gems. The passage shows a Rus-built stone church and who ruined it.

The Tatars forced open the church doors, and saw some who had perished by fire, and others they put to death with weapons; they plundered the holy Mother of God [cathedral], and stripped the wonderful icon adorned with gold and silver and precious stones

Daily lifeLower Volga (Batu's camp)1243 (6751)

A patent from Batu: 'be the eldest of all princes'

Five years after the conquest the grand prince rode 'to the Tatars, to Batu', and sent his son Konstantin on to the Great Khan ('Kan') in Mongolia. Batu received him with honour and named him senior over all Rus princes. The chronicle records the start of the system in which Rus princes held their thrones by the khan's word.

Grand Prince Iaroslav went to the Tatars, to Batu, and sent his son Konstantin to the Kan; Batu honoured Iaroslav with great honour, and his men, and let him go, saying to him: 'Iaroslav! be thou the eldest of all princes in the Russian nation.'

Working translation

Original wording

Великый князь Ярославъ поѣха въ Татары къ Батыеви, а сына своего Костянтина посла къ Канови; Батый же почти Ярослава великою честью, и мужи его, и отпусти й, рекъ ему: «Ярославе! буди ты старѣй всѣмъ княземъ въ Русскомъ языцѣ.»

Daily lifeLower Volga (Batu's camp)1246 (6754)

Bow to the fire and the idols

When Mikhail of Chernigov came to Batu's camp, Batu ordered him to bow to fire and to 'their idols'. Mikhail refused, mocked the idols as deaf, and was killed. The episode matches western reports that visitors had to pass between fires and honour Mongol felt images, and it made Mikhail a Russian saint.

Batu sent to Prince Mikhail, ordering him to bow down to fire and to their idols; but Prince Mikhail did not submit to their command, but reproached him and his deaf idols, and so he was stabbed to death without mercy by the impious ones

Working translation

Original wording

посла Батый къ Михаилу князю, веля ему поклонитися огневи и болваномъ ихъ; Михайло же князь не повинуся велѣнью ихъ, но укори й и глухыя его кумиры, и тако безъ милости отъ нечестивыхъ заколенъ бысть

Laurentian Chronicle, PSRL vol. 1 (1846)

Daily lifeVladimir-SuzdalWinter 1257 to 1258 (6765)

The census: tens, hundreds, thousands and ten-thousands

The Horde sent census takers who counted the Suzdal, Riazan and Murom lands and set up officers over tens, hundreds, thousands and ten-thousands, the Mongol decimal system, before returning to the Horde. Clergy were not counted, in line with the Mongol practice of exempting religious men from tax.

That winter the census takers came, and counted all the Suzdal land, and the Riazan and the Murom, and appointed chiefs of ten, of a hundred, of a thousand and of ten thousand, and went off to the Horde; only they did not count abbots, monks, priests, choir clergy, whoever looks to the holy Mother of God and to the bishop.

Working translation

Original wording

Тое же зимы пріѣхаша численици, исчетоша всю землю Суждальскую, и Рязаньскую, и Мюромьскую, и ставиша десятники и сотники и тысящники и темники, и идоша въ ворду, толико не чтоша игуменовъ, черньцовъ, поповъ, крилошанъ, кто зрить на святую Богородицю и на владыку.

Laurentian Chronicle, PSRL vol. 1 (1846)

Daily lifeRostov1262 (6770)

Tax farmers driven out by the town assemblies

The Horde's tribute had been farmed out to Muslim merchants, who bought the right to collect it and charged interest. In 1262 the towns called their assemblies (veche) and drove them out of Rostov, Vladimir, Suzdal and Iaroslavl. The same entry tells of a renegade monk, Zosima, who became a Muslim under a Tatar official named Kutlubii and was killed in Iaroslavl.

[God] put fury into the hearts of the Christians, who, not bearing the violence of the pagans, summoned the veche and drove them out of the towns, out of Rostov, out of Vladimir, out of Suzdal, out of Iaroslavl; for those accursed besermen had bought up the tribute, and from that they worked great ruin on the people

Working translation

Original wording

вложи ярость въ сердца крестьяномъ, не терпяще насилья поганыхъ, изволиша вѣчь, и выгнаша изъ городовъ, изъ Ростова, изъ Володимеря, изъ Суждаля, изъ Ярославля; окупахуть бо ти оканьніи бесурмене дани, и отъ того велику пагубу людемъ творяхуть

Laurentian Chronicle, PSRL vol. 1 (1846)

Daily lifeKursk1283 to 1284 (6791 to 6792)

The baskak's warning: heads tied to saddle straps

After a quarrel with the local princes, the baskak Akhmat had captured boyars killed, their bodies hung on trees without heads and right hands, and told pilgrims to spread the message that anyone who disputes with his baskak will end the same way. His men then tied the heads to their saddle straps. The Laurentian text starts mid-story because leaves are missing from the manuscript.

'You are guests and pilgrims, you travel through the lands; say this: whoever will hold a dispute with his baskak, so it will be with him.' And whatever common people were seized, with wives and children, he sent all of them away. And he ordered the corpses of those boyars hung up on the trees, cutting off the head and the right hand; and the besermen began to tie the boyars' heads to their saddle straps

Working translation

Original wording

«вы есте гости, а паломници, ходите по землямъ; тако молвите: хто иметь держати споръ съ своимъ баскакомъ, тако ему будеть;» а что изъимано людій черныхъ и съ женами и съ дѣтми, то все попровадилъ прочь. А трупья бояръ тѣхъ повелѣ по деревью извѣшати, отъимая голову и правую руку; и начаша бесурмене вязати головы боярьскыя къ торокомъ

Laurentian Chronicle, PSRL vol. 1 (1846)

The namePontic steppe1223 (chronicle year 6731)

'Unknown peoples': who are these Tatars?

The first Suzdal notice of the Tatars admits that nobody knows who they are, where they came from, what language they speak or what faith they hold. People called them Tatars, Taumens or Pechenegs, while bookish men tied them to a prophecy of Methodius of Patara. The chronicler adds that he writes them down only 'for the memory of the disaster of the Rus princes' at the Kalka.

there appeared peoples whom no one clearly knows: who they are and whence they came out, and what their language is, and of what tribe they are, and what their faith is; and they call them Tatars, and others say Taumens, and others Pechenegs, and others say that these are the ones of whom Methodius, bishop of Patara, bears witness

Working translation

Original wording

явишася языци, ихже никтоже добрѣ ясно не вѣсть, кто суть и отколѣ изидоша, и что языкъ ихъ, и которого племени суть, и что вѣра ихъ; и зовуть я Татары, а инии глаголють Таумены, а друзіи Печенѣзи, ини глаголють, яко се суть, о нихъ же Меѳодій Патомьскый епископъ свѣдѣтельствуеть

Laurentian Chronicle (Suzdal annal), PSRL vol. 1 (1846)

About Tatars (the Mongol army of Jebe and Subutai)

Keep in mind

The editors note that other chronicles read 'Taurmens' (Turkmens). The Methodius link is a book legend, not anything the Mongols said of themselves.

Strange talesPontic steppe1223

The end-of-time peoples of Pseudo-Methodius

Straight after naming the Tatars, the chronicler quotes the prophecy he thinks explains them: peoples driven into the desert by Gideon would come out at the end of time and conquer everything from the east to the Euphrates and from the Tigris to the Black Sea, except Ethiopia. He hedges at once: 'God alone knows who they are.' This is the same book legend that western writers linked to Gog and Magog.

for thus Methodius said: that toward the end of time those whom Gideon drove out will appear, and they will capture all the land from the east to the Euphrates, and from the Tigris to the Pontic Sea, except Ethiopia.

Working translation

Original wording

тако бо Меѳодій рече: яко къ скончанью временъ явитися тѣмъ, яже загна Гедеонъ, и поплѣнять всю землю отъ встока до Ефранта и отъ Тигръ до Понетьскаго моря, кромѣ Еѳіопья.

Laurentian Chronicle, citing the Revelation of Pseudo-Methodius, PSRL vol. 1 (1846)

Why it matters

Earliest surviving Rus chronicle codex; primary Russian-side record of the Mongol conquest, the Tatar census, tribute and Horde politics for 1223-1305, the base text against which New Chronology redatings are argued.
