Book of the Secrets of the Faithful of the Cross (crusade treatise with geography of the Mongol-controlled trade routes and the Tartars), first edition

Marino Sanudo Torsello; Jacques Bongars, editor

How we know

mixed Part firsthand observation, part drawn from other sources.

Card wording

mixed: Sanudo travelled widely in the eastern Mediterranean and used merchant informants; his account of the Tartars (Ilkhanate, Golden Horde, the spice routes via Tabriz and Baghdad) is compilation plus informant testimony, presented to John XXII in 1321

Cautions
  • Bongars printed from a Paris MS; the maps in the 1611 edition are reduced; manuscript maps by Pietro Vesconte c. 1320 are the primary cartographic witnesses (Vat. Pal. lat. 1362A, BL Add. 27376).
  • MAPS TEAM: Vesconte/Sanudo maps; see also L-type lead for manuscript maps.

Why it matters

Fourteenth-century Venetian strategic geography that treats the Mongol khanates as trade partners and potential allies; the accompanying Vesconte world and regional maps (in the manuscripts, partly engraved by Bongars) are an early cartographic record of 'Tartaria' (note for maps team).
