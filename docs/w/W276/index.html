Written source · German · German

The Very Latest State of Siberia, a great and hitherto little-known Muscovite province in Asia: its government, land, mountains, animals, rivers, towns, and the customs of the Samoyeds, Voguls, Kalmyks, Ostyaks, Tunguses, Buryats, Mongols and other Tatar peoples, with a historical report on the Swedish prisoners in this land, the school at Tobolsk and the beginning of the conversion of the heathen

Anonymous (Nuremberg compiler); includes Johann Bernhard Müller

How we know

compilation Assembled from earlier sources. The author did not observe it directly.

Card wording

compilation of recent reports (Swedish captives in Tobolsk, Pietist correspondence) including Müller's firsthand description of the Ostyaks written 1716 at Tobolsk

Cautions
  • Anonymous compilation; J. B. Müller's Ostyak account is the core firsthand piece and was separately printed (Berlin 1720) and translated (Weber 1722-1723 English). Preface signals dependence on Dutch manuscript material (Witsen).
  • Catalogue gives no author; the OCR preface refers to a Dutch work preserved only in manuscript, presumably Witsen.

Why it matters

Earliest German printed ethnography of Siberian peoples, built from Swedish captives' reports; title lists the Tatar peoples of Siberia individually, a useful contemporary usage of Tartarische Völker as a collective label.
