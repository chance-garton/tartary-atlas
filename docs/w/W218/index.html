The Cuman Codex: Latin-Persian-Cuman glossary (c.1294-1330) and German-Cuman religious texts and riddles (c.1330-1340), first complete edition, Budapest 1880

Codex Cumanicus

Anonymous Italian merchants and German Franciscans in the Golden Horde; Count Geza Kuun (editor)

How we know

firsthand The author saw the places or people described.

Card wording

firsthand linguistic record made in the Golden Horde (Crimea or Sarai) by Latin merchants and missionaries; the Cuman (Qipchaq) language of the steppe as spoken c.1300

Cautions
  • Kuun's readings were corrected by Radloff (1887), Bang and Groenbech (1936 facsimile, 1942 dictionary); consult those for the Cuman forms.
  • | Also recorded independently by the russian pass: Open web text retrieved (index with sections I-...) (vostlit.info/Texts/Dokumenty/Zolotoord/XIV/130…).

Why it matters

Primary linguistic evidence for the Turkic language of the 'Tartars' of the Pontic steppe as recorded by Westerners living among them; anchors the identity of the Golden Horde's Qipchaq population.
