Miechowita's Treatise on the Two Sarmatias (1517)

The first systematic Western account of the Tatar hordes, written in Kraków from prisoners' and merchants' reports.

Full title Treatise on the Two Sarmatias, Asian and European, and on what they contain

Maciej Miechowita (Matthias of Miechów), Kraków physician and historian

Open the original book

Maciej Miechowita (Matthias of Miechów), a Kraków physician and historian, never travelled east. He questioned Tatar and Muscovite prisoners and merchants in Kraków and printed this Latin treatise in 1517. Book I describes the Tatar hordes (the Golden Horde beyond the Volga, Perekop, Kazan, the Nogais) and their origins, and rejects the Riphean mountains of classical geography. Herberstein and most later sixteenth-century writers drew on it. The atlas does not hold the text: the corpus knows a 1518 Augsburg copy only as digital image manifests, and nothing is stored.

Who “Tartar” means here
The Tatar hordes of the Volga steppe, Perekop in Crimea, Kazan and the Nogai steppe, as reported to him. The card also lists Kosan and a reported Chagatai.
Why read it
It is the foundation of Renaissance Latin writing on the Tatars and on Asian Sarmatia, and the direct source of many later authors.
Watch out
Everything is second-hand, it argues against Ptolemy, and later authors copied it without saying so. The atlas quotes nothing from it.

  • Second-hand information from informants; polemical against Ptolemy; later authors copy it silently. Modern edition Anninskii 1936 (Latin/Russian).
  • Not found on Internet Archive under Latin spellings; located via Europeana.

Why it matters

The foundational Renaissance Latin description of the Tatar hordes and of 'Asian Sarmatia', the direct source for Herberstein, Guagnini and most sixteenth-century writing on Tartary; translated into German 1518, Polish 1535, Italian (Ramusio vol. II).
