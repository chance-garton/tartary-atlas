History of Sahib Giray Khan

A Crimean court physician's account of the reign of Khan Sahib Giray, 1532 to 1551.

Full title History of Sahib Giray Khan: Ottoman text with French translation, notes and glossary by Ozalp Gokbilgin (Ankara 1973); Russian translation 2018

تاريخ صاحب گرای خان

Remmal Hoca (Nidai Celebi Mehmed Ankaravi, d. 1568), physician-astrologer of the khan

Open the original book

Remmal Hoca (Nidai Çelebi Mehmed Ankaravi, died 1568) was physician, astrologer and confidant to Sahib Giray, khan of Crimea. He wrote this Ottoman Turkish history around 1551 to 1553 from what he saw: the khan's campaigns at Kazan (1532), on the Muscovite frontier, in Circassia and at Astrakhan (1546), and the khan's murder in 1551. The atlas does not hold the text: the corpus knows Gökbilgin's 1973 Ankara edition with French translation, and it keeps full text only for works published before 1929 or openly licensed.

Who “Tartar” means here
The Crimean Tatars of Sahib Giray's khanate, with the neighbouring Kazan and Astrakhan khanates, the Nogays and the Circassians.
Why read it
The earliest Crimean court history, telling the years of Muscovy's conquest of Kazan and Astrakhan as seen from Bakhchysarai.
Watch out
It is a panegyric of the khan, and the scan on offer is a copyrighted 1973 edition uploaded by a third party. The atlas quotes nothing from this work.

Why it matters

Contemporary Crimean Tatar chronicle of the 1530s-1540s, the decades of Muscovy's conquest of Kazan and Astrakhan seen from Bakhchisaray; the earliest Crimean court history.
