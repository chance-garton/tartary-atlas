Veritable Records of the Ming

The Ming court's reign-by-reign record, 1368 to 1627, with dated entries on Mongol, Oirat, Jurchen and Western Region embassies.

Catalogue title Veritable Records of the Ming Dynasty (13 reigns, 1368-1627), containing the dated entries on Mongol, Oirat, Jurchen and Western Region embassies

明實錄

Ming court historiographers

The Ming Veritable Records were compiled reign by reign from court archives by successive teams of historians between 1399 and 1656. They cover thirteen reigns from 1368 to 1627 and hold the dated entries on missions from the Mongols, Oirats and Jurchen and from Central Asian courts such as Hami, Turfan, Samarkand and Herat, and on Tibet. The card lists a set of 43 user-uploaded PDFs on the Internet Archive, source edition not stated. The atlas does not hold the text of this edition: the corpus lists it but stores no text of it, under its rights rule, so nothing from it was read for this guide.

Who “Tartar” means here
Not recorded on the card. In Ming court writing the word Dada (Tatar) is the usual Chinese name for the eastern Mongols, set against the Oirats, but the card does not record particular entries.
Why read it
It is the dated court record of every Ming contact with the steppe and Central Asia, the source behind the Ming frontier chapters.
Watch out
The card says the edition is unidentified and the upload is unverified, so it is an access lead, not a critical edition.

Cautions
  • Edition unidentified; verify against the Academia Sinica 1962-1966 collated edition. Geoff Wade's Southeast Asia extracts (NUS, epress.nus.edu.sg/msl) give an English index for Southeast Asian entries only.
  • Unverified user upload; treat as access lead rather than critical edition.

Why it matters

The dated primary record of every Ming contact with Mongol, Oirat, Jurchen and Central Asian polities; the source of the Ming shi frontier chapters.
