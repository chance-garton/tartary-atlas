Written source · Qarakhanid Turkic · Persian, Arabic, Ottoman, Chagatai and Tatar

Wisdom of Royal Glory: Qarakhanid Turkic mirror for princes, 1069-1070; Vambery’s 1870 partial edition of the Vienna (Herat 1439) Uyghur-script manuscript with German translation, and a facsimile of the Fergana (Namangan) manuscript

قوتادغو بیلیگ

Yusuf Khass Hajib (fl. 1069-1070)

How we know

official or contemporary record An official document or a record made at the time.

Card wording

contemporary literary composition at the Qarakhanid court of Kashgar; not a narrative source

Cautions
  • Vambery's readings are obsolete (Radloff 1891-1910, Arat 1947). The Fergana facsimile item's publisher is not identified in IA metadata.

Why it matters

Together with Kashgari, the Qarakhanid literary evidence that a Turkic Islamic state existed in the Tarim-Semirechye region in the 1070s, the region European maps later filled with 'Tartary'.
