Shah Mahmud Churas's chronicle of Kashgaria

The main native narrative of the Moghul khans of Kashgaria, about 1546 to 1676.

Full title Chronicle: history of the Moghul khans of Kashgaria continuing the Tarikh-i Rashidi, critical Persian text, Russian translation and commentary by O. F. Akimushkin (Moscow 1976; second edition St Petersburg 2010)

تاریخ (انیس الطالبین)

Shah Mahmud Churas (fl. 1670s)

Open the original book

Shah Mahmud Churas wrote this Persian chronicle in the late 1670s as a continuation of Mirza Haidar's Tarikh-i Rashidi. For the sixteenth century he copies earlier sources, and for the mid-seventeenth he speaks from service to Abdallah Khan and Ismail Khan. It tells of Kashgar, Yarkand, Aksu, Turfan and the Ladakh campaigns. The atlas does not hold the text: the corpus knows a 2010 reissue of Akimushkin's Persian text with Russian translation, and it keeps full text only for works published before 1929 or openly licensed.

Who “Tartar” means here
Not recorded. The card ties the polity to what Europeans called Little Bukharia, and lists Moghuls, Khojas, Kirghiz, Kazakhs, Qalmaqs and Uzbeks.
Why read it
The only detailed native story of Kashgaria between Mirza Haidar and the Dzungar conquest.
Watch out
Its early part copies Mirza Haidar, and the scan on offer is a modern copyrighted reissue uploaded by a third party. The atlas quotes nothing from this work.
