Müller's Collection of Russian History, with his history of Siberia

The St Petersburg Academy's historical serial (1732 to 1764), including G. F. Müller's archive-based history of the Tatar khanate of Siberia and its conquest.

Catalogue title Collection of Russian History (Sammlung Russischer Geschichte), 9 volumes in 54 parts, St Petersburg 1732-1764; contains Müller’s Siberian history, the Nachrichten von Seereisen (1758), pieces on the Kalmyks, Samoyeds, Yugra, the Amur and Chinese-Russian relations

Gerhard Friedrich Müller (ed.)

Read the 11 passagesOpen the original book

Gerhard Friedrich Müller (1705-1783), a German historian at the St Petersburg Academy of Sciences, spent 1733 to 1743 in Siberia with the Second Kamchatka Expedition, copying town archives, collecting chronicles and questioning Tatars, Mongols and others. His 'Sammlung Russischer Geschichte' (9 volumes, 1732 to 1764) printed his own and colleagues' studies; volume 6 (1761 to 1762) contains the opening of his 'Description of the Siberian Kingdom', covering the Tatar princes of Tyumen and Isker, Kuchum Khan and Yermak's conquest, with his own descriptions of the ruins. This corpus has the nine volumes of the first edition, a 1777 reprint, and the English 'Conquest of Siberia' derived from it.

How they knew
Part eyewitness
Who “Tartar” means here
Müller's 'Tataren' are chiefly the Turkic Muslim Tatars of Tyumen, Tobolsk and the Baraba, heirs of the Siberian khanate; 'the former Tatars' also means the Mongol empire of Chinggis Khan, which he says united Mongols and Tatars.
Why read it
A careful scholar who both walked the ruins and cross-examined the chronicles: he measured the ramparts of Isker, asked Tatars what they called their own capital, and still relayed the chronicles' omens and miracles, labelled as such.
Watch out
Much of the narrative follows the Siberian chronicles (especially Remezov's), which mix history with legend; Müller flags this but sometimes theorises beyond the evidence. The bundle held only the later English 'Conquest of Siberia', so the German volume 6 is cited directly.

CitiesIsker (Sibir), near Tobolsk1730s

Isker, the khan's capital: 'if one can call it a town'

Müller visited the remains of Isker, the capital Yermak's Cossacks entered in 1582, on a steep height of the Irtysh above Tobolsk. He describes a small round hill protected on the landward side by a triple rampart and ditches, the river and a ravine guarding the other sides, and an interior only a few dozen fathoms across, room for little more than the khan, his family and servants. Part of it had fallen into the river.

The remains of this former capital of the land, if one can indeed call a place such as this seems to have been a town, can still be seen today.

CitiesTyumen1730s

Chimgi-Tura, the old Tatar court at Tyumen

Müller identifies the first seat of the Siberian Tatar princes with Tyumen. The remains of the old Tatar court, a small entrenchment between two ravines of the Tyumenka stream, were still visible, and the local Tatars still called the fort and the Russian town Chimgi or Chimgi-Tura, without knowing why. Müller argues the name comes from 'Chingidin', given by the founder Taibuga in honour of Chinggis Khan.

There one still sees the remains of the former Tatar court, which consisted of a small entrenchment and lay between two ravines of the Tyumenka stream, which flows through the town into the Tura. The Tatars there still call this fortress, and after it the town of Tyumen built beside it, Chimgi or Chimgi-Tura. Why, they do not know.

Working translation

Original wording

Man siehet daselbst noch die Ueberbleibsel des ehmahligen Tatarischen Hoflagers, das in einer kleinen Verschanzung bestanden, und zwischen zwo Klüften des Baches Tümenka, welcher durch die Stadt in die Tura fällt, gelegen gewesen. Die dortigen Tataren nennen diese Festung, und hiernächst auch die dabey erbauete Stadt Tümen, noch heutzutage Tschimgi, oder Zimgitura. Warum? wissen sie nicht.

Gerhard Friedrich Müller (Sammlung Russischer Geschichte, vol. 6, 1761-1762)

BuildingsIsker (Sibir), near Tobolsk1730s

Houses of wood or unburnt brick, leaving no trace

Inside the ramparts of Isker Müller found no remains of houses, only uneven ground and some pits that might have been cellars. He concludes that any buildings must have been of wood in the Siberian Tatar manner, or of unburnt brick in the Bukharan way, since nothing of them survives, and notes that some pits were dug later by treasure seekers, not always in vain.

If so, they must have been built only of wood in the Siberian Tatar manner, or of unburnt bricks after the Bukharan custom, since nothing more is to be seen of them. In some places there are pits which may have served as cellars. But it is also said that some pits were made in recent times, because people searched for treasure there

An einigen Orten sind Gruben, welche zu Kellern gedienet haben können. Man sagt aber auch, daß einige Gruben in neuern Zeiten gemacht seyn, weil man daselbst Schätze gesuchet

BuildingsSouthern Siberia1760s

Gold in the old graves: 'from whom but the former Tatars?'

Arguing that the history of Chinggis Khan belongs to Siberian history, Müller points to the antiquities of southern Siberia: old entrenchments across the steppes, many memorial stones and statues, and graves from which gold and silver had been dug. He asks from whom such treasure could come but the former Tatars, to whom the riches of China, Persia, Russia, Poland, Bohemia and Hungary had fallen as plunder. On the next page he adds that the poorer old graves near Baikal show the earliest owners lived simply.

what treasures of gold and silver have not been brought out of the graves? From whom can they come but from the former Tatars, since it is known that all the treasures of China, Persia, Russia, Poland, Bohemia and Hungary had to fall to them as prey?

Working translation

Original wording

was für Kostbarkeiten an Gold und Silber hat man nicht aus den Gräbern hervorgeholet? von wem können dieselbe anders, als von den ehmahligen Tataren, herstammen, indem bekannt ist, daß alle Schätze von China, Persien, Rußland, Polen, Böhmen, Ungarn, ihnen zum Opfer werden müssen?

Gerhard Friedrich Müller (Sammlung Russischer Geschichte, vol. 6, 1761-1762)

Daily lifeIrtysh River near the Vagai1585

A Tatar funeral feast of thirty oxen for Yermak

In the same chronicle story, the Tatars repented of mistreating Yermak's body, wished they had made him their king, and buried him under a spreading pine at the Begishev burial ground, holding a memorial feast 'after the Tatar custom' at which thirty oxen and ten rams were eaten. They shared out his weapons and clothes, and one coat of mail was offered to a famous Ostyak idol.

They buried him at the Begishev burial ground under a pine with crooked branches, and in his memory held a funeral feast after the Tatar custom, at which 30 oxen and 10 rams were consumed.

Original wording

Man habe ihn an dem Begischewischen Begräbniß-Orte unter einer krauß-astigen Fichte zur Erden bestattet, und zu seinem Gedächtnisse nach Tatarischem Gebrauche ein Todtenmahl angestellet, auf welchem 30 Ochsen und 10 Hammel verzehret worden.

Gerhard Friedrich Müller (Sammlung Russischer Geschichte, vol. 6, 1761-1762), retelling the Siberian (Remezov) chronicle

The nameTyumen and Tobolsk1760s

'Turali': the Tatars who lived in towns

Discussing the word 'tura' (town or fortress in Tatar and Bukharan), Müller reports that the Bashkirs call the Tyumen Tatars, and after them the Tobolsk Tatars, only 'Turali'. On the next page he gives the Bashkirs' explanation: these Tatars had lived since ancient times in towns and fortresses, or at least in fixed places, while the Bashkirs often moved their dwellings about the steppe.

One may easily think here, since Tura in the Tatar and Bukharan language means a town or fortress, that perhaps the river Tura received its name from this old Tatar fortress of Chimgi. The Bashkirs have no other name for the Tyumen Tatars, and after them for the Tobolsk Tatars too, than to call them Turali

Working translation

Original wording

Man kann hier leicht auf die Gedanken kommen, da Tura in der Tatarischen und Bucharischen Sprache eine Stadt, oder Festung, bedeutet, daß vielleicht der Fluß Tura von dieser alten Tatarischen Festung Tschimgi den Nahmen empfangen. Die Baschkiren haben für die Tümenischen Tataren, und nach selbigen auch für die Tobolskischen, keinen andern Nahmen, als daß sie solche Turali nennen

Gerhard Friedrich Müller (Sammlung Russischer Geschichte, vol. 6, 1761-1762)

The nameIsker (Sibir), near Tobolsk1760s

'Sibir' was not the Tatars' name for their capital

Müller corrects a common belief that 'Sibir' is the Tatar word for the capital. When he asked the Tobolsk Tatars, they did not know the word at all; they called the place Isker. He thinks the name Sibir arose in Russia, taken from the Permians or Zyrians who first brought news of the land, and was only later attached to the Tatar capital.

It is commonly believed that Sibir is the Tatar name of the place, meaning a capital or residence. But if one asks the Tobolsk Tatars about this, it is quite unknown to them. They call the place Isker instead

Working translation

Original wording

Man glaubt insgemein, Sibir sey die Tatarische Benennung des Orts, welche soviel, als eine Hauptstadt, oder Residenz, bedeute. Fraget man aber die Tobolskischen Tataren nach diesem Umstande, so ist ihnen solcher ganz unbekannt. Sie nennen vielmehr den Ort Isker

Gerhard Friedrich Müller (Sammlung Russischer Geschichte, vol. 6, 1761-1762)

The nameMongolia1760s

A bird that cried 'Chinggis'

A learned Mongol lama gave Müller a story from Tibetan and Mongol books about the rise of Chinggis Khan. A khan's son and nine nobles fled rather than become monks; the peoples they met joined them and chose the son as khan. As the nine debated his new name, a small bird landed nearby and cried 'Chinggis, Chinggis', and so he was named. Müller calls the story very fabulous but prints it because nothing like it had been published.

At that same time a little bird came flying up, settled near the council, and cried out in a clear voice, 'Chinggis, Chinggis'. At once they unanimously decided to give the khan this name that the bird had sung.

Working translation

Original wording

Zu derselben Zeit sey ein kleiner Vogel angeflogen gekommen, welcher in der Nähe der Rathsversammlung sich niedergesetzet, und mit vernehmlicher Stimme Tschingis, Tschingis, geschrieen. Alsobald habe man einmüthig beschlossen, diesen von dem Vogel angestimmten Nahmen dem Chane beyzulegen.

Gerhard Friedrich Müller (Sammlung Russischer Geschichte, vol. 6, 1761-1762), reporting a Mongol lama's account

Strange talesTobolskbefore 1582

A Christian city seen in the sky above Tobolsk

Müller relays, somewhat apologetically, the omens that the Siberian chronicles say foretold the end of Tatar rule. The Tatars living around the site of later Tobolsk often saw a Christian city with churches and bell towers in the air and heard bells ringing. He says he tells it only to stay faithful to his source.

Will I be forgiven for telling them? I do it for no other reason than that I do not like to depart from my original. The Tatars living round about are often said to have seen, above the place where the town of Tobolsk now stands, a Christian city with churches and bell towers in the air, and to have heard bells ringing too.

Working translation

Original wording

Wird man es mir wohl zu gute halten, wenn ich solche erzähle? Ich thue es aus keiner andern Ursache, als weil ich mich nicht gerne von meinem Originale entferne. Oft sollen die umher wohnenden Tataren über dem Orte, wo jetzt die Stadt Tobolsk stehet, eine Christliche Stadt mit Kirchen und Glockenthürmen in der Luft gesehen, und dabey auch ein Geläute von Glocken gehöret haben.

Gerhard Friedrich Müller (Sammlung Russischer Geschichte, vol. 6, 1761-1762), retelling the Siberian (Remezov) chronicle

Strange talesIrtysh River near the Vagai1585

Yermak's body that bled for six weeks

Following the Remezov chronicle, whose author wanted Yermak made a saint, Müller tells how Tatars found the drowned Cossack leader by his armour. When a murza tried to strip it, blood ran from the corpse's mouth and nose as from a living man, so the Tatars laid the body naked on a scaffold and everyone who passed shot an arrow into it, and each time fresh blood flowed. Birds would not touch it, and after six weeks troubling dreams led the Tatars to bury him.

At once much blood ran from the dead man's mouth and nose, just as from a living person … For this purpose they laid the body quite naked on a scaffold, and everyone who came up or passed by shot an arrow at it, and each time fresh blood ran from the wound.

Working translation

Original wording

Alsobald lief dem Todten aus Mund und Nase häufiges Blut, nicht anders, als von einem lebendigen Menschen … Sie legten zu solchem Ende den Leichnam ganz entkleidet auf ein Gerüste, da denn ein jeder, der herzukam, oder vorüber gieng, einen Pfeil nach demselben abschoß, wovon jedes mahl frisches Blut aus der Wunde lief.

Gerhard Friedrich Müller (Sammlung Russischer Geschichte, vol. 6, 1761-1762), retelling the Siberian (Remezov) chronicle

Strange talesZungharia1760s

A Kalmyk ruler who searched for the legendary valley of Ergene Kon

Discussing Abulghazi's legend that the ancestors of the Mongols lived shut in the valley of Ergene Kon until a whole mountain was melted to let them out, Müller adds a curious report he had been assured of: the Zunghar ruler Galdan Tseren, having heard the story from Bukharans, searched for such a valley as a refuge for his treasure and family, and was said to have found one somewhere in his lands.

It is remarkable that, as I am assured, the Kalmyk ruler Galdan Tseren after long searching is said to have found such a plain among the mountains, as Ergene Kon is described by Abulghazi … within the borders of his territory, only no one knows where

Original wording

Es ist merkwürdig, daß, wie man mich versichert, der Calmückische Beherrscher Galdanzerin nach langem Suchen eine dergleichen Ebene zwischen dem Gebürge, wie Irgene-Kon von Abulgasi beschrieben ist … innerhalb den Gränzen seines Gebiets, man weiß nur nicht wo, soll gefunden haben

Gerhard Friedrich Müller (Sammlung Russischer Geschichte, vol. 6, 1761-1762)

Why it matters

Foundation of Siberian historiography: Müller's history of the conquest of the Siberian khanate from Tobolsk and other archives, the Nachrichten von Seereisen (Bering, Dezhnev), essays on Kalmyk, Samoyed and Yugra questions and on the Amur. Central for how eighteenth-century scholars reconstructed pre-Russian Siberia and Tartary from documents rather than travellers' reports.
