Written source · French · French and Dutch

Memoirs concerning the history, sciences, arts, manners and customs of the Chinese, by the missionaries of Peking, vol. 1 (1776): including Amiot’s Monument de la transmigration des Tourgouths (Torghut migration from the Volga to China, 1771) and Monument de la conquete des Eleuths (Qianlong’s inscription on the Dzungar conquest, c. 1757)

Joseph-Marie Amiot and the Peking Jesuits

How we know

mixed Part firsthand observation, part drawn from other sources.

Card wording

mixed: Amiot in Peking translated Qianlong's stele inscriptions and reported court news of the Torghut arrival (informant testimony and official record), with his own notes

Cautions
  • Amiot translated imperial propaganda inscriptions; his notes distinguish his own knowledge from the emperor's text. Series editorship in Paris altered and cut letters. Amiot's Eloge de la ville de Moukden (1770, Manchu-French) is a separate publication not verified here.
  • Later volumes contain Amiot's Manchu dictionary notices and Cibot on Tartar customs; not itemised in this pass.

Why it matters

Contemporary Jesuit reporting from Peking on the two events that redrew "Tartary" in the 1750s-1770s: the Qing destruction of the Dzungar state and the flight of the Volga Torghuts to China; complements Tulisen (W016) and Zwick (W068) from the Chinese side.
