The Wonders Described (Mirabilia descripta), first edition of the Latin text, in the Société de géographie’s Recueil vol. IV (which also contains d’Avezac’s Carpini and Michel-Wright’s Rubruck)

Jordanus Catalani of Séverac, Dominican, bishop of Quilon; Coquebert de Montbret, editor

How we know

mixed Part firsthand observation, part drawn from other sources.

Card wording

mixed: firsthand observation of Persia and India (c. 1321-1330) with informant testimony on Greater Tartary, Cathay and the Chagatai lands ('quod non vidi eo quod ibi non fui')

Cautions
  • Single manuscript; Coquebert de Montbret's edition is the first and the base of Yule's 1863 Hakluyt translation (IA bub_gb_wQUVAAAAQAAJ, English only). Jordanus's letters (1323-1324) are in Wyngaert-type collections and Yule's Cathay.
  • Tome IV of the Recueil is thus the single most useful 19th-c. volume for the Latin Mongol dossier.
  • Further copies: archive.org/details/RelationsDesVoyagesDeGuill… (Michel-Wright Rubruck offprint)

Why it matters

Fourteenth-century Latin missionary geography whose chapter on the Great Tartar (Cathay) and the three Tartar empires (Cathay, Uzbek's Horde, the Chagatai 'Doa') is an early explicit tripartite description of the Mongol world; Latin original behind Yule's 1863 English.
