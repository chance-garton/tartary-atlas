Broniewski's Description of Tartary (Tartariae descriptio)

A Polish envoy's Latin description of the Crimean khanate, written after his embassy of 1578 to 1579.

Catalogue title Description of Tartary, never before published, with a map of the Tauric Chersonese (Crimea); bound with Reichersdorff’s Transylvania and Moldavia

Marcin Broniewski (Martin Broniovius), twice Polish envoy to the Crimean khan

Marcin Broniewski (Martin Broniovius) went twice as Polish envoy to the Crimean khan, and in 1578 to 1579 he served Stephen Báthory at the court of Mehmed II Giray. He described the khanate's towns, ruins, court, army and economy, from Perekop and Bakhchysarai to Kaffa, Sudak and Kerch, with a map of Crimea. It was written around 1579 and printed at Cologne in 1595. The atlas does not hold the text: the corpus knows the 1595 print only as digital image manifests.

How they knew
Eyewitness
Who “Tartar” means here
The Crimean Tatars of the Giray khanate, with the Nogais of the steppe. Broniewski's title uses Tartary for the khanate and the lands around it.
Why read it
The most detailed sixteenth-century eyewitness description in Latin of the Crimean khanate and its antiquities, with an early map of Crimea.
Watch out
It was printed only in 1595, bound with Reichersdorff's work, and Purchas's 1625 English version is abridged. The atlas quotes nothing from this work.

Why it matters

The most detailed sixteenth-century eyewitness Latin description of the Crimean khanate and its antiquities, explicitly titled 'Tartariae descriptio'; the map of Crimea is an early cartographic source (note for maps team).
