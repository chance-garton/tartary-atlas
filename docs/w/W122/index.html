North and East Tartary, or a concise account of some of those lands and peoples formerly known (Noord en Oost Tartarye), second enlarged edition

Nicolaes Witsen

How we know

compilation Assembled from earlier sources. The author did not observe it directly.

Card wording

compilation of earlier sources and informant testimony: Witsen never visited Siberia; he assembled Russian, Jesuit, Dutch and Muscovy-embassy reports, letters and interviews (his own 1664-65 Moscow journey underlies parts)

Cautions
  • Compilation: each section names its source (Martini atlas, Rougemont, Palafox, Couplet, Montanus, letters from Pekin via Grimaldi, 1686 Dutch embassy diary, Russian informants). First edition 1692 exists but no scan located in this pass; DBNL text is the 1705 second edition. Catalogue publisher string in Delpher gives Halma dates 1699-1711 (publisher activity, not imprint).
  • Resolves L009 (English pass lead). W079 (Philosophical Transactions 1686 notice) refers to the associated Witsen map, recorded separately as a map state. Access checked via KB resolver redirect (HTTP 200) and DBNL colophon.
  • Further copies: books.google.com/books?id=uidnAAAAcAAJ; books.google.com/books?id=seVXAAAAcAAJ

Why it matters

The largest early-modern European compilation devoted explicitly to Tartary: Witsen defines Noord en Oost Tartarye region by region (Niuche/Manchuria, Mongolia, Siberia, Kalmyk and Kirghiz steppe, Crimea) and prints his sources, so it shows exactly what a well-connected 1690s Amsterdam observer meant by the word and what data (Russian route books, Jesuit letters, embassy diaries) lay behind it.
