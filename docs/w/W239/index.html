Voyage round the World, part IV: the most notable things seen in China (with chapters on the origin of the Eastern Tartars and the Manchu conquest, the Lama and Tartary)

Giovanni Francesco Gemelli Careri

How we know

firsthand The author saw the places or people described.

Card wording

firsthand observation (Careri travelled Canton-Beijing in 1695-1696 and was received at the Kangxi court) mixed with compilation from Jesuit informants (Grimaldi) and printed sources; the authenticity of parts of his journey has been questioned but is now generally accepted

Cautions
  • Suspected of borrowing from Jesuit and other printed accounts; portions of the itinerary considered fabricated by some 18th-c. critics.

Why it matters

Late-seventeenth-century Italian lay eyewitness of Qing China and of European beliefs about Tartary and the Lama, independent of the Jesuit relations; frequently cited on the Manchu-Chinese dual administration.
