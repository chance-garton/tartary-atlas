Second Volume of Navigations and Voyages: Marco Polo, Hayton, the Venetian travellers in Persia and Tartary, and the Muscovy texts

Giovanni Battista Ramusio, editor, with Marco Polo, Hayton, Barbaro, Contarini, Angiolello, Zeno, Campense, Herberstein and Interiano

How we know

compilation Assembled from earlier sources. The author did not observe it directly.

Card wording

compilation of earlier firsthand narratives, edited and glossed by Ramusio with his own discourses (Dichiaratione on Marco Polo, Discorso sopra il libro di Haiton Armeno); Ramusio's Polo text derives from a lost Latin recension and is an independent witness

Cautions
  • Ramusio's Polo text (R) contains material absent from other manuscripts and its source is lost, so it is both witness and problem; the 1583 edition adds texts. Overlaps W040 (Polo), W061 (Barbaro, Contarini), W035 (Herberstein) in English translation.
  • Whether the 1574 printing includes the 'Due viaggi in Tartaria' (Carpini/Ascelin via Vincent of Beauvais) was not confirmed by OCR sampling; check table of contents.
  • Further copies: gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/bpt6k131851r (vol. I, 1606)

Why it matters

The Italian Renaissance canon of Tartary texts in one volume; Ramusio's Polo recension (with unique passages) and his Italian Hayton were the versions most Europeans read, and his discourses are the first critical commentary on the Cathay/Tartary geography.
