Records / W268
Written source · French · French and Dutch

Historical and geographical observations on the barbarian peoples who have inhabited the banks of the Danube and the Black Sea (with a map and an inscription plate)

Charles de Peyssonnel

How we know

compilation Assembled from earlier sources. The author did not observe it directly.

Card wording

retrospective history compiled from Byzantine and classical sources, informed by the author's consular residence in the Crimea (Bakhchisarai 1753-1757) and Black Sea posts

Cautions
  • Compiled history; the ethnographic notes on the Crimea belong to Peyssonnel's consular knowledge rather than to the cited sources. Companion Traite sur le commerce de la mer Noire (1787) recorded separately.

Why it matters

An eighteenth-century diplomat's reconstruction of the succession of steppe peoples on the Black Sea from the Huns to the Tatars, written by a man who lived among the Crimean Tatars; shows how the Crimean khanate was fitted into the long "barbarian" sequence.
