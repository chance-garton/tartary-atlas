Wei Yuan's Illustrated Treatise on the Maritime Kingdoms

A Chinese world geography of the 1840s and early 1850s with chapters on Russia and Central Asia.

Catalogue title Illustrated Treatise on the Maritime Kingdoms (50 juan 1844; 60 juan 1847; 100 juan 1852), with chapters on Russia (E’luosi) and maps

海國圖志

Wei Yuan

Open the original book

Wei Yuan compiled this geography in 50 chapters in 1844, then expanded it to 60 in 1847 and 100 in 1852. It draws on Western geographical works in Chinese translation, Chinese travel accounts and official records to describe the world beyond China, including Russia, Siberia, Central Asia and Europe, with maps. The copy is a Peking University Library print in 42 volumes, probably the 100 chapter edition of 1852. The atlas holds only a poor-quality machine reading (OCR) of this scan, and no passages were selected or read for this guide.

Who “Tartar” means here
Not recorded. The card says the work mapped Tartar regions using both Chinese and translated European sources, so it may echo European Tartary geography, but the actual wording is not recorded.
Why read it
It shows how a Chinese scholar of the 1840s combined Chinese and translated Western sources to picture Russia and Inner Asia.
Watch out
If the copy is the 1852 edition it postdates the 1850 limit of this guide by two years, though the Russia chapters draw on the earlier editions. The title page should be checked.

Cautions
  • If the copy is the 1852 edition its composition date exceeds 1850 by two years; the Russia chapters derive largely from the 1844-47 editions and from earlier Chinese sources (Tulisen, Qi Yunshi). Record edition after checking the title page.

Why it matters

Mid-19th-century Chinese synthesis of world geography including the Russian empire in Asia; shows how Qing scholars mapped 'Tartar' regions using both Chinese and translated European sources.
