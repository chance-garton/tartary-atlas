The Qing trilingual Mirror of the Manchu language

A 1780 imperial dictionary matching Manchu, Mongolian and Chinese words with their sounds.

Catalogue title Imperially Produced Manchu-Mongolian-Chinese Trilingual Mirror of Manchu with Phonetic Transcriptions (31 juan, 1780)

御製滿珠蒙古漢字三合切音清文鑑

Qianlong imperial commission

Ordered by the Qianlong emperor, this 31 chapter dictionary of 1780 sets Manchu words beside their Mongolian and Chinese equivalents, with phonetic transcriptions, so that court officials could read Manchu and Mongolian names of offices, peoples and places in a standard form. The card lists a Siku quanshu manuscript copy in 26 volumes, in which the Manchu and Mongolian scripts were hand copied by scribes. The atlas holds only a poor-quality machine reading (OCR) of this scan, and no passages were selected or read for this guide.

How they knew
Official record
Who “Tartar” means here
Not recorded. The card names Manchus, Mongols and Chinese. This is an internal Qing dictionary, not a European account that called the Manchus Tartars.
Why read it
It is a key to reading Manchu and Mongolian terms for peoples, offices and places as the Qing court fixed them.
Watch out
The manuscript is a copy of the 1780 palace print, and the hand-copied Manchu and Mongolian scripts may hold scribal errors.
