Records / W259
Written source · French · French and Dutch

Edifying and curious letters written from the foreign missions, new edition (26 vols, Paris 1780-1783): Memoirs of China volumes containing letters on Tartary by Gerbillon, Jartoux (ginseng in Tartary, 1711), Parrenin, Regis, Gaubil, Amiot and others

Jesuit missionaries; Y. M. M. de Querbeuf, editor

How we know

mixed Part firsthand observation, part drawn from other sources.

Card wording

mixed: firsthand letters from Jesuits who travelled in Manchuria and Mongolia (Jartoux on the 1709-1711 survey, Parrenin at Kangxi's court, Gerbillon) alongside hearsay and edifying narrative; heavily edited in Paris

Cautions
  • Letters were selected, cut and rewritten by the Paris editors; the 1780-83 edition regroups them by mission and drops some. Which volume holds a given letter differs between the original Recueils and later editions: cite by edition and volume.
  • Jartoux's ginseng letter (1711) and Gerbillon's journeys are the Tartary items most cited; Gerbillon's full journals are in Du Halde vol. 4 (recorded separately), not in the Lettres.
  • Further copies: archive.org/details/cihm_41297

Why it matters

The running Jesuit news-service on Qing Tartary for eighteenth-century France: letters describe the Manchu homeland, Mongol princes at court, the Kangxi survey of Tartary and the ginseng country. Much of what Du Halde, Grosier and Deguignes say about Tartary is footnoted to these letters.
