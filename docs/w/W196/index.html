Gardizi's Ornament of Histories (Zayn al-akhbar)

An eleventh-century Persian history whose famous chapter on the Turks is not in this edition.

Catalogue title Ornament of Histories: the section on the Tahirids, Saffarids, Samanids and Ghaznavids, Persian text ed. Muhammad Nazim (E. G. Browne Memorial Fund no. 1, Berlin: Iranschahr, 1928)

زین الاخبار

Gardizi (fl. c.1050)

Gardizi wrote this Persian history around 1050 at Ghazna, drawing on earlier writers rather than on his own travels. The full work has a well-known chapter on the Turks of the steppe (Kimek, Kirghiz, Qarluq, Pechenegs, Khazars, Bulghars), but Muhammad Nazim's 1928 Berlin edition prints only a middle section on the Tahirids, Saffarids, Samanids and Ghaznavids. A scan of that edition sits in the corpus, but this guide has not read it and quotes nothing from it. The profile rests on the catalogue card.

Who “Tartar” means here
Not recorded. Gardizi wrote around 1050, long before the Mongol empire, so Tartar would not mean the Mongols here.
Why read it
The Turks chapter of Gardizi is a core eleventh-century Persian description of the steppe, though it must be read in other editions such as Barthold's.
Watch out
Nazim printed only part of the work for lack of funds, and the Turks chapter is not in this edition. The atlas quotes nothing from it.

Cautions
  • IA date 1050 is the composition date. Nazim printed only ff. 1-101 of the middle of the work for lack of funds (stated in the preface). Barthold's 1897 edition of the Turks chapter (Otchet o poezdke v Sredniuiu Aziiu) and Habibi's full Tehran text were not found scanned.

Why it matters

Gardizi's chapter on the Turks (Kimek, Kirghiz, Qarluq, Yaghma, Pechenegs, Khazars, Bulghars) is a core eleventh-century Persian ethnography of the steppe; this edition gives the adjacent Samanid-Ghaznavid narrative, and the Turks chapter is available in Russian through Barthold (below).
