Zhao Hong's Full Record of the Mong-Tatars

A Southern Song envoy's 1221 report on the Mongols, one of the earliest outside eyewitness descriptions.

Catalogue title Full Record of the Mong-Tatars, annotated (Meng Da beilu, 1221)

蒙韃備錄校注

Zhao Hong (Song envoy), with Wang Guowei's collation and commentary

In 1221 a Southern Song envoy went to meet the Mongol commander Muqali at Yanjing (Beijing) and wrote up what he saw and was told about Mongol origins, customs and rule. The card names the author as Zhao Hong, though older catalogues say Meng Gong, and calls it the earliest surviving outside description of the Mongols, earlier than Carpini. The copy is Wang Guowei's annotated edition, first issued in 1926, in Chinese. The atlas holds only a poor-quality machine reading (OCR) of this scan, and no passages were selected or read for this guide.

How they knew
Part eyewitness
Who “Tartar” means here
Da-da (Tatar) as Chinese writers of 1221 used it for the Mongols and their neighbours. The card says the work divides the Tatars into Black, White and Wild Tatars and explains the name as the Chinese used it.
Why read it
It is the earliest outside eyewitness look at the Mongols, and it shows the Chinese meaning of Tatar before European travellers arrived. That makes it central to the question of where the name Tartary comes from.
Watch out
The author was long given as Meng Gong, but Wang Guowei showed it was Zhao Hong, and the catalogue creator field still repeats the old name. The text passed through the Shuofu anthology, and the Chinese commentary is Wang's, not the envoy's.

Card wording

firsthand observation by a Southern Song envoy to the Mongol commander Muqali at Yanjing (Beijing) in 1221, with informant testimony on Mongol origins, customs and administration

Cautions
  • Author traditionally given as Meng Gong (as in this catalogue) but shown by Wang Guowei to be Zhao Hong. Transmitted through the Shuofu anthology; Wang Guowei's edition collates the Shuofu texts. Catalogue creator field repeats the erroneous attribution: record both.
  • Russian annotated translation by N. Ts. Munkuev (1975) verified on vostlit (separate record).

Why it matters

Earliest extant outside eyewitness description of the Mongols (1221), earlier than Carpini; explains the ethnonym Tatar / Dada as used by the Chinese, and the three-fold division of Tatars, central to any discussion of the name Tartary.
