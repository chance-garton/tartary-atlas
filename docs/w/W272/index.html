Memoir of M. d’Anville, first geographer to the king, on China (defence of his China and Chinese Tartary maps and their sources, 1776; 47 pp.)

Jean-Baptiste Bourguignon d'Anville

How we know

compilation Assembled from earlier sources. The author did not observe it directly.

Card wording

compilation and methodological statement: d'Anville explains how the Jesuit survey data for China, Tartary and Tibet were reduced to his maps (see M013, M016, M022)

Cautions
  • Gallica dc:date 1776 and imprint "Pekin" (a false imprint) should both be recorded. The Eclaircissemens geographiques sur la carte de l'Inde (1753) and the Nouvel atlas de la Chine (1737, M022) are related d'Anville items.
  • The Gallica SRU also lists manuscript and printed states of d'Anville's 1733 Tibet-Kashgar-Hami map (btv1b53008987t manuscript; btv1b5963120b, btv1b5963119p, btv1b530275540, btv1b84929416 printed; btv1b53027528j 1750 manuscript): map leads related to M016/M022, not separately verified.

Why it matters

D'Anville's own statement of the sources behind the standard eighteenth-century maps of Chinese Tartary and Tibet (M013, M016, M022): needed to judge what on those maps is survey, what is Jesuit report, and what is inference.
