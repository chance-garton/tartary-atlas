Sorrowful Lament on the Destruction of the Kingdom of Hungary by the Tartars (Carmen miserabile), in Bongars’s Hungarian historians collection

Master Roger of Torre Maggiore, archdeacon of Várad (Oradea); Jacques Bongars, editor

How we know

firsthand The author saw the places or people described.

Card wording

firsthand observation: Rogerius was captured by the Mongols in 1241 and lived among them in occupied Hungary until 1242; written c. 1243-1244 and addressed to Bishop James of Palestrina

Cautions
  • Textual transmission entirely via the 1488 Thuroczy prints (no MS); modern critical edition Juhász in Scriptores rerum Hungaricarum II (1938) and Bak/Rady CEMT 5 (2010) not open access. The Bongars 1600 volume also contains Thuroczy and other Hungarian chronicles with Tartar notices.

Why it matters

The most detailed contemporary eyewitness account of Mongol administration in a conquered European kingdom (census, appointed officials, harvest under occupation), and of the withdrawal of 1242; central for what 'Tartar' rule looked like on the ground.
