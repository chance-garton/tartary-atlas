On the Customs of the Tartars, Lithuanians and Muscovites: Ten Fragments

Michalo the Lithuanian (pseudonymous Lithuanian nobleman and envoy); J. J. Grasser, editor

How we know

firsthand The author saw the places or people described.

Card wording

firsthand observation: the author served as Lithuanian envoy to the Crimean khanate (c. 1538-1540) and describes Tatar society at Perekop, Kaffa and the Dnieper steppe from experience; written c. 1550 for Sigismund Augustus, printed in fragments 1615

Cautions
  • Pseudonymous author; text survives only as ten fragments selected by the 1615 editor; idealises the Tatars to criticise Lithuanian mores.

Why it matters

Rare insider Latin description of the Crimean khanate's society, law and economy in the 1540s, framed as a moral comparison; a primary source for the 'Tartary' of the northern Black Sea.
