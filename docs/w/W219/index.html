Mahmud al-Kashgari's Compendium of the Turkic Dialects

An eleventh-century Turkic dictionary and world map by a Qarakhanid prince, made at Baghdad, 1072 to 1077.

Full title Compendium of the Turkic Dialects: Arabic-Turkic dictionary with ethnographic notes and a circular world map centred on Balasaghun, editio princeps from the unique Istanbul manuscript (Millet Kutuphanesi Ali Emiri Arabi 4189), printed Matbaa-i Amire 1915-1917

ديوان لغات الترك

Mahmud al-Kashgari (fl. 1072-1077)

Open the original book

Mahmud al-Kashgari, a Qarakhanid prince, wrote this Arabic dictionary of Turkic words at Baghdad in 1072 to 1077, saying he had travelled among the Turkic tribes to record their speech. It adds notes on the tribes and a circular world map centred on Balasaghun, with Gog and Magog beyond a wall. It is known from one manuscript copied in 1266 and found in 1914. The corpus holds scans of the 1915 Istanbul print, but this guide has not read them and quotes nothing from them.

How they knew
Eyewitness
Who “Tartar” means here
The card lists Tatars as one of the Turkic peoples he records, with the Oghuz, Qipchaq, Kirghiz and Yaghma. He wrote well before the Mongol empire.
Why read it
The eleventh-century Turkic view of the steppe from Kashgar to the Volga, and the earliest Turkic world map.
Watch out
It survives in a single manuscript, and Kilisli's print has errors corrected in later editions. The atlas quotes nothing from this work.

Research notes
  • Single manuscript; Kilisli's print has errors corrected in the 1941 Ankara facsimile, Atalay's Turkish (1939-43, IA divan-1939) and Dankoff and Kelly's English (1982-85, not open).
  • Map record recommended: 'World map of Mahmud al-Kashgari, 1072-1077, in the 1266 manuscript; reproduced in the 1915 print and the 1941 facsimile'.

Why it matters

The eleventh-century Turkic self-description of the steppe from Kashgar to the Volga, listing the tribes in geographical order from Rum to China, and the earliest Turkic world map (should be recorded in the map inventory: the map shows the Turkic lands with Balasaghun at the centre and Gog and Magog beyond a wall).
