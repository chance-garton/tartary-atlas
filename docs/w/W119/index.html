Compendium of Chronicles (Jami al-tawarikh), Persian critical edition in four volumes

جامع التواریخ

Rashid al-Din Fadl Allah Hamadani (d. 1318)

How we know

compilation Assembled from earlier sources. The author did not observe it directly.

Card wording

compilation of earlier sources plus informant testimony: Ilkhanid vizier writing c.1300-1310 from Mongol court informants (Bolad Chingsang), Mongol records (Altan Debter tradition) and earlier Persian histories (Juvaini); retrospective for pre-1250 events

Cautions
  • IA item metadata carries the composition date (1307) and a Persian description that actually describes a different, facsimile publication (Jami al-tawarikh with Hafiz-i Abru's Majma al-tawarikh, typeset introduction only); the scanned object itself is the Rawshan and Musavi typeset edition, Alburz 1373 SH. Rashid al-Din's Mongol material depends on Bolad Chingsang and lost Mongol records; his Turkic tribal classification is a court synthesis, not fieldwork.
  • Resolves L002 (open text of Rashid al-Din) with the Persian original rather than the Boyle English translation. The English pass lead L002 pointed at a lending-only Boyle 1971 copy; Thackston's 1998-99 English translation was not located open-access.

Why it matters

The fullest Persian critical text of the principal Mongol-era history of the Turkic and Mongol peoples: vol. 1 opens with the classified account of the Turkic tribes ('aqwam-i atrak') and their subdivision into those 'now called Mongols'. Foundational for any reading of steppe ethnonyms behind the later European word 'Tartary'.
