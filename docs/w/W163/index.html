The Hypatian Chronicle: Kiev, Galicia and the Horde

The southern Rus chronicle, whose Galician-Volhynian part describes the fall of Kiev in 1240, Prince Daniil's visit to Batu and a century of living beside the Tatars.

Catalogue title Hypatian Chronicle (Complete Collection of Russian Chronicles, vol. 2): Primary Chronicle, Kievan Chronicle, Galician-Volhynian Chronicle

Ипатьевская летопись (Полное собрание русских летописей, т. 2)

Anonymous Kievan and Galician-Volhynian chroniclers; Archaeographic Commission edition

The Hypatian codex (about 1420s) joins the Primary Chronicle, the Kievan Chronicle and the Galician-Volhynian Chronicle, a court history of Prince Daniil of Galicia and his heirs written in the later 13th century. Its authors knew Daniil's household and sometimes cite eyewitnesses. For the Tatars it gives the Kalka campaign, the siege of Kiev with the names of Batu's generals, the storming of Volhynian towns, Daniil's humiliating visit to Batu on the Volga in 1245 to 1246, the Tatar general Burundai forcing the princes to tear down their town walls, and the disastrous campaigns of Nogai and Telebuga. This corpus copy is the 1843 first edition (PSRL vol. 2), checked against the 1908 Shakhmatov edition.

How they knew
Part eyewitness
Who “Tartar” means here
The Mongols of Batu's army and afterwards the Golden Horde, its khans, generals (Kuremsa, Burundai, Nogai, Telebuga) and their subjects. The chronicler also calls them godless Moabites, Hagarenes and pagans.
Why read it
Here the Tatars are seen from a princely court that dealt with them for decades: the sounds of Batu's camp outside Kiev, drinking kumiss in Batu's tent, and a Tatar general ordering Rus towns to demolish their own walls. It also shows the Rus prince Daniil building a new stone and timber town, Kholm, which the Tatars could not take.
Watch out
The Galician-Volhynian text was written without dates, and a later copyist added years that are often wrong by a year or more. It is a court history, hostile to the Tatars and flattering to Daniil. The 1843 OCR is damaged; quotes are normalized and checked against the 1908 edition where doubtful.

CitiesKyivLate 1240

Kiev besieged: you could not hear for the carts and camels

Batu came to Kiev 'in heavy strength' and ringed the city. The chronicler's famous line: inside the walls people could not hear each other for the creaking of Batu's carts, the bellowing of his camels and the neighing of his herds of horses. Batu then set up siege engines by the Liadskie (Polish) Gate, where the ravines came close, and battered the walls day and night.

Batu came to Kiev in heavy strength, with a great multitude of his forces, and surrounded the city, and the Tatar force hemmed it in, and the city was under great siege. And Batu was at the city and his men besieged the city, and one could not hear for the noise of the creaking of his carts, the multitude of the bellowing of his camels, and the neighing of the herds of his horses

CitiesChelm (Kholm), Poland1230s to 1250s

Kholm: a new town that craftsmen fled to from the Tatars

Daniil founded Kholm on a wooded hill he spotted while hunting, and the chronicler notes that the Tatars could not take it when Batu conquered Rus. Daniil invited Germans, Rus, Poles and other foreigners, and young men and master craftsmen of every kind 'fled from the Tatars' to it: saddlers, bowyers, quiver makers, and smiths of iron, copper and silver. Here the arrow runs the other way: craftsmen leave Tatar-held lands to build a Rus prince's town.

he began to invite newcomers, Germans and Rus, foreigners and Poles; they came day after day, and young men and masters of every kind fled from the Tatars, saddlers and bowyers and quiver makers and smiths of iron and copper and silver, and there was life, and they filled the courtyards around the town, the fields and the villages

CitiesVolodymyr (Vladimir-Volynskii)1259 to 1260 (6768 to 6769)

Burundai: 'tear down all your town walls'

The Tatar general Burundai arrived in Volhynia during a princely wedding and told the princes: if you are at peace with me, raze your towns. Lev dismantled Danilov and Stozhek and then Lviv; Vasilko razed Kremenets and Lutsk. Vladimir-in-Volhynia was too big to pull down quickly, so Vasilko had it burned overnight, and next day Burundai came to see it burned 'with his own eyes' and dined with the prince.

'If you are at peace with me, then tear down all your towns.' Lev tore down Danilov and Stozhek, and from there he sent and tore down Lviv, and Vasilko sent and tore down Kremenets and Lutsk.

CitiesVolhynia1240 to 1241

Twelve catapults at Kolodiazhin, and a town Batu could not take

After Kiev, Batu marched into Volhynia. At Kolodiazhin he set up twelve siege engines but could not break the wall, so he talked the defenders into surrendering and then killed them. Kremenets, Daniil's town, he judged impossible to take and left alone. Vladimir-in-Volhynia was stormed and its people slaughtered. The passage shows that walls and position still mattered against the Mongols.

he came to the town of Kolodiazhin, and set up 12 siege engines and could not break the wall, and began to talk the people over; they, heeding his evil counsel, surrendered and were themselves slaughtered. … and seeing Daniil's town of Kremenets, that it was impossible for him to take it, he went away from them

BuildingsKyivDecember 1240

The church vaults that collapsed under the refugees

When the Tatars broke through, the townspeople threw up a last ring of defences around the Church of the Mother of God (the Tithe Church, Kiev's first stone church, built by Prince Vladimir about 990). Next day, as people crowded onto the church and its vaults with their goods, the walls gave way under the weight, and the city fell. The chronicler credits the building to Rus princes and its ruin to the siege.

Next morning they came against them, and there was a great battle between them; and as the people ran up onto the church and onto the church vaults, with their goods, from the weight the church walls fell down with them, and so the city was taken by the warriors.

BuildingsChelm (Kholm), Poland1250s

Kholm's church: carved heads, Roman glass, a floor like a mirror

The chronicler describes Daniil's Church of St John in Kholm in unusual detail: vaults resting on four carved human heads, windows glazed with 'Roman' glass, a floor cast of copper and pure tin that shone like a mirror, and doors trimmed with white Galician and green Kholm stone, carved by 'a certain craftsman Avdei'. Nearby stood a tower on a stone base 15 ells high, built of hewn timber and whitewashed. The builders are Rus, and the materials are named.

vaults, with an arch from each corner, and their supports on four human heads carved by a certain craftsman; the windows adorned with Roman glass … its floor inside was cast of copper and pure tin, so that it shone like a mirror; its two doors were adorned with hewn stone, white Galician and green Kholm stone

Daily lifePereiaslav and the Volga (Kuremsa's and Batu's camps)1245 to 1246 (entered under 6758)

Sorcery, Chinggis Khan's dreams and bowing to a bush

On his way to Batu, Daniil passed through the camp of the commander Kuremsa and was horrified by Tatar religion as a Christian saw it: shamans' 'kudesy' (sorcery), the 'dreams' of 'Chigizakon' (Chinggis Khan), and visiting kings and princes being led around a bush to bow to the sun, the moon, the earth and the souls of their ancestors. At Batu's a Rus servant told him to bow to the bush as his brother had done.

for he saw them possessed by the devil: their foul sorcerers' ravings and the dreams of Chigizakon, his foul blood-drinkings, his many witchcrafts; the kings and princes and nobles who came were led around a bush to bow to the sun and moon and earth, to the devil and to their fathers and grandfathers and mothers who have died and are in hell

Original wording

Daily lifeLower Volga (Batu's camp)1245 to 1246

'Drink our black milk, mare's kumiss'

Inside Batu's tent Batu asked Daniil whether he drank 'black milk, our drink, mare's kumiss'. Daniil said he never had, but would if ordered. 'You are now one of us, a Tatar: drink our drink,' said Batu. Then Daniil went to pay respects to Batu's senior wife Barakchin, who sent him a cup of wine because, she said, he was not used to milk.

'Do you drink black milk, our drink, mare's kumiss?' He said: 'Until now I have not drunk it; now, since you order it, I drink.' And he said: 'You are already one of us, a Tatar; drink our drink.' And he drank and bowed, according to their custom

Daily lifeLower Volga (Batu's camp)1245 to 1246

'Worse than evil is Tatar honour'

The chronicler breaks off to lament what homage meant: a great prince who ruled Kiev, Vladimir and Galich now knelt and was called a slave, and paid tribute in fear for his life. He adds that the Tatars had poisoned Grand Prince Iaroslav of Suzdal and knifed Mikhail of Chernigov for not bowing to the bush. Daniil stayed 25 days before he was sent home with his lands confirmed.

O, worse than evil is Tatar honour! Daniil Romanovich was a great prince, who ruled the Russian land, Kiev and Vladimir and Galich, with his brother, and other lands: now he sits on his knees and is called a slave, and they want tribute, he does not expect to live, and threats come.

Original wording

О злѣе зла честь Татарьская! Данилови Романовичю князю бывшу велику, обладавшу Рускою землею, Кыевомъ и Володимеромъ и Галичемъ, со братомъ си инѣми странами: нынѣ сѣдить на колѣну и холопомъ называеться, и дани хотять, живота не чаеть и грозы приходять.

The nameKyiv1240

A prisoner names Batu's generals, and Guyuk

During the siege of Kiev the defenders captured a Tatar named Tovrul, who told them who was in Batu's army. The list matches Batu's Chinggisid relatives and generals known from Persian and Mongol sources. The chronicler adds that Guyuk turned back on hearing of the Great Khan's death and became khan himself, though (he believed) not of the khan's family but his first general.

They captured among them a Tatar named Tovrul, and he told them all their strength; these were his brothers, strong commanders: Urdiui, Baidar, Biriui, Kaidan, Bechak and Mengu and Kiuiuk (who returned on learning of the khan's death and became khan, though not of his family, but he was his first commander), Sebediai the Bogatur and Burundai the Bagatyr

Working translation

Original wording

Яша же въ нихъ Татарина, именемъ Товрулъ, и тъ исповѣда имъ всю силу ихъ; се бяху братья его силный воеводы: Урдюй, Байдаръ, Бирюй, Кайданъ, Бечакъ и Меньгу и Кююкъ (иже вратися увѣдавъ смерть канову и бысть каномъ, не отъ роду же его, но бѣ воевода его перьвый), Себѣдяй Богатуръ и Бурунъдай Багатырь

Who Batu's commanders: Urdiui (Orda), Baidar, Biriui, Kaidan, Bechak, Mengu (Mongke), Kiuiuk (Guyuk), Subutai, Burundai

The namePontic steppe1223 (entered under 6732)

'Godless Moabites, called Tatars'

The Galician chronicler opens the Kalka story with an 'unheard-of army': godless Moabites 'called Tatars' who fell on the Polovtsian land. Even the greatest Cuman chief could not stand against them. After the rout the Tatars 'went back to their vezhi', the steppe word for nomad tent camps. The biblical name Moabites shows how the chronicler fitted a new enemy into scripture.

There came an unheard-of army: the godless Moabites, called Tatars, came onto the Polovtsian land. The Polovtsy made a stand, but Iurii Konchakovich, who was greatest of all the Polovtsy, could not stand against their face; he fled, and many were killed, as far as the river Dnieper, and the Tatars turned back and went to their tent camps.

Working translation

Original wording

Приде неслыханая рать: безбожніи Моавитяне, рекомыи Татарове, придоша на землю Половецькую. Половцемь же ставшимъ, Юрьгій Кончаковичь бѣ болійше всихъ Половець, не може стати противу лицо ихъ; бѣгающи же ему, и мнози избьени быша, до рѣкы Днѣпра, Татарове же возвратишася, идоша въ вѣжа своя.

Strange talesCarpathian Mountains1285 to 1286 (entered under 6790)

Telebuga lost in the mountains: a hundred thousand dead

Returning from a raid on Hungary, Nogai went by Brasov, while Telebuga tried to cross the mountains. A three-day crossing became fifty days of wandering. Famine set in, the soldiers began to eat people and then to die, and 'eyewitnesses said' a hundred thousand perished. Telebuga came out on foot with his wife and a single mare. The chronicler presents it as God's punishment.

Telebuga went across the Mountain; what should have been crossed in three days, he wandered for 50 days lost in the mountains, led by the wrath of God. And there was a great famine among them; and they began to eat people, and then began themselves to die, and a countless multitude of them died; eyewitnesses said this: the dead were a hundred thousand.

Original wording

Strange talesLutskAbout 1255 to 1259

The wind that turned the catapult stones back

When Kuremsa's Tatars attacked the unfortified town of Lutsk in a winter flood, the townsmen cut the bridge. Kuremsa set up siege engines, but, says the chronicler, God, St John and St Nicholas worked a miracle: the wind blew the stones back onto the Tatars, and then the sling of the engine broke. The Tatars went back to their camp in the steppe.

But God worked a miracle, and St John and St Nicholas: there was such a wind that when the engine threw, the wind turned the stone back upon them; and again, as they hurled hard, by the power of God their sling broke, and having achieved nothing they went back to their camps, that is, to the steppe.

Original wording

Богъ же чюдо створи, и святый Иванъ, и святый Никола, вѣтру же таку бывшу, яко порокомъ вергшу вѣтръ же обращаше камень на нѣ, пакы же мечющимъ на нѣ крѣпко изломися, Божіею силою, пракъ ихъ, и не успѣвше ничтоже вратишася во станы своя, рекше въ поле.
