Kharosthi documents from Niya

Third and fourth century local records, mostly on wood, from an oasis on the southern edge of the Tarim Basin.

Catalogue title Kharosthi Documents from Ancient Chinese Turkestan

Local administrators and correspondents

These are administrative and private documents in the Kharosthi script, found at the ruined oasis settlement of Niya on the southern rim of the Taklamakan. They date from about 235 to 325 CE and deal with orders from officials, households, disputes, property and daily affairs. They are the ordinary paperwork of a local kingdom, not a history or a travel book. The card lists the translations of Boyer, Rapson and Senart and selections held at the University of Washington. The atlas does not hold the text of this edition: no copy was harvested into the corpus, so nothing from it was read for this guide.

Who “Tartar” means here
Not recorded, and the word is not expected here. These local papers of the third and fourth centuries CE come from a Kharosthi-writing oasis kingdom, not from the steppe. Turkestan in the title is a modern name for the region.
Why read it
They are a rare local counterweight, written by residents rather than visitors, and show how an oasis community ran its affairs and held property.
Watch out
The card stresses that these are supplementary documents. Each has its own date and place of finding, and together they do not tell a continuous story.
