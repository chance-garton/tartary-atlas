Travels through Muscovy, Persia and India, enriched with three hundred plates (Archangel, Moscow, Volga, Astrakhan, Caspian, Persia 1701-1708)

Cornelis de Bruyn

How we know

firsthand The author saw the places or people described.

Card wording

firsthand observation by a painter-traveller (Volga voyage and Astrakhan 1703, Caspian crossing; Kalmyk and Tatar encounters drawn from life)

Cautions
  • Witsen sponsored and annotated de Bruyn; the French version (Le Brun, 1718) is a translation with some abridgement.
  • DBNL has only the 1996 modern abridgement; the 1711 original is via Google Books (KB copy).
  • Further copies: resolver.kb.nl/resolve?urn=dbnl:bruy004reiz01 (1996 abridged edition, DBNL)

Why it matters

Eyewitness of the lower Volga and Kalmyk steppe in 1703 by a trained observer who drew what he saw; used by Witsen (his friend) and by later French compilers. Documents Astrakhan Tatars and Kalmyks a decade after Witsen published.
