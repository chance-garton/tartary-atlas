BuildingsCrimea and the Black Sea steppec. 1550

No houses, only portable huts of wicker and reeds on carts

Michalo describes the Tatars' pastoral life: they follow their herds across the plains, have no enclosures and no houses except portable tents made of wicker and reeds covered with felt and rush mats, which they carry on carts full of wives and children. They do not farm even rich land, live on milk without bread or beer, and own nothing fixed except wells shared by the clan.

They have no enclosures and no houses, only portable tents of wicker and reeds joined together, covered with felts and mats and made fast with rush matting, which, filled with wives and children, they carry with them on carts.

Working translation

Original wording

Septa nulla habent, nec domos, praeter tabernacula gestatoria, ex viminibus & arundinibus compactis, cilicijs velata storeis, & mattis scirpeis munita, quae uxoribus & pueris referta, vectant secum in plaustris.

Michalo the Lithuanian, De moribus Tartarorum, Lituanorum et Moschorum, fragment 1 (c. 1550; Basel, 1615)

About Crimean Tatars

Keep in mind

Michalo generalises; the Crimean khanate also had towns such as Bakhchysarai and Kaffa (under the Ottomans), which he mentions elsewhere.

Daily lifeBlack Sea (voyage to Odessa)1839

Hajj pilgrims on a Black Sea steamer

On the steamer to Odessa's quarantine, Jesse shared the deck with forty passengers including Tatars returning from the pilgrimage to Mecca. He sneers at their lack of washing but notes that they prostrated themselves constantly towards the Kaaba.

The Tartars were returning from a pilgrimage which they had made to Mecca; and though Mohammedans, did any thing but observe the ablutions recommended by their religion; but they unceasingly prostrated themselves in the direction of the Caaba.

Captain William Jesse

About Tatar pilgrims returning from Mecca

Keep in mind

Openly contemptuous; the conditions were those of a crowded quarantine deck.
