What the sources say here: 1 passage

CitiesIben river, northern Mongolia (near the Selenga)about 1710

A city made of tents around a living Buddha

The Khalkha Mongols revered a lama called the Hutuktu, a 'living Fo' (Buddha), brother of one of their khans, who had built a costly temple of yellow glazed bricks on the Tula with workmen from Beijing; the Oirat ruler Galdan destroyed it in 1688. Now the Hutuktu lived in tents, and his camp drew Russian traders, monks from India, Pegu, Tibet and China, and lamas of every rank: 'a large City made of Tents'.

One may call it a large City made of Tents, the Hurry being much greater there than any where else in this Part of Tartary. The Russians of Selinghinskoy, which is not far distant, trade thither; there you meet also with Bonzas from Indostan, Pegu, Tibet, and China
