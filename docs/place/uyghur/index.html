Daily lifeUighur lands (Hayton's 'Tarse')early 13th century

Tartars learned letters, and idols, from the Uighurs

Hayton says that before their expansion the Tartars had no letters at all, which is why he cannot give dates for their early history. When their armies reached the kingdom of 'Tarse' (the Uighurs), they learned writing there, and because the people were idolaters they also began to worship idols, while always confessing one immortal God above the others.

In those parts the Tartars learned letters, for before they had no letters at all. And because the inhabitants of those lands were all idolaters, the Tartars began to worship idols; but they always confessed the immortal God, greater than the others.

CitiesUyghur (Toghuzghuz) lands, Mongolia and Xinjiang9th century

The Toghuzghuz khan's city of twelve iron gates

In his own route list, Ibn Khurradadhbih says the Toghuzghuz khan's capital lay three months' march beyond Upper Nushajan, through pasture land with large villages. The king lived in a great city with twelve iron gates and had a golden tent on top of his palace, visible five parasangs away, while the Kimek king camped among pastures in tents. Steppe Turks here have both cities and tents.

From Upper Nushajan to the capital of the khaqan of the Toghuzghuz is three months' march, through pasture country covered with large villages … Their king lives in a great city closed by twelve iron gates … The king of the Toghuzghuz owns a golden tent set on the top of his palace; it can shelter a hundred people

Daily lifeBishbalik (near Urumqi)14th to 15th century

Nomads of Bishbalik in felt tents

Klaproth translates the Ming dynasty's geography on the land of Bishbalik, north of the Tian Shan. It says the people had no houses, lived in felt tents, kept cattle, sheep, camels and horses, farmed little, lived on meat and milk, and spoke a Turkic dialect called Uighur. The same book notes that under the Mongols there had been a town of Bishbalik with a viceroy and military farming colonies.

The inhabitants are nomads, have no houses, and live under felt tents. They occupy themselves almost entirely with keeping oxen, sheep, camels and horses. They have little agriculture and live on the meat and milk of their herds, which also supply the clothing they need. Their language is a dialect of Turkish
