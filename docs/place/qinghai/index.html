Qinghai (Kokonor)

What the sources say here: 3 passages

CitiesKokonor steppe (Qinghai)1661

Hordes that deserve to be called portable cities

This desert, however squalid it may be, is nevertheless inhabited at fixed times of the year by the Tartars whom they call Kalmuk, along the banks of the rivers where there is more pasture, in hordes which not unfittingly deserve to be called portable cities; the Tartars roam the desert far and wide here and there for the sake of robbery, so a caravan must be equipped with a strong force to repel the violence of the attacking Tartars.

Working translation

Original wording

Hoc desertum quamtumvis squalidum sit, à Tartaris tamen, quos Kalmuk vocant, constitutis anni temporibus, ubi ad ripas fluminum major pascuorum copia est, per hordas quæ portatiles non incongruè civitates dici merentur, habitatur; Tartaris desertum hinc indè longè latéque latrocinandi causâ divagantibus; undè ad Tartarorum insultantium violentiam propulsandam valida manu Caravanam instructam esse oportet.

Editorial context

Crossing the desert beyond the Great Wall at Xining on the way to Lhasa, Grueber and Dorville found it inhabited at set seasons by 'Tartars whom they call Kalmuk', camped along rivers where pasture was best, in hordes 'which not unfittingly deserve to be called portable cities'. The same Tartars roamed the desert as raiders, so caravans needed a strong armed escort.

Athanasius Kircher, S.J. (China illustrata, Amsterdam 1667), from the oral report and drawings of Johann Grueber and Albert Dorville

About Kalmyk (Oirat, Khoshut) Mongols

Keep in mind

Kircher never travelled; he writes up what Grueber told him and drew. 'Kalmuk' here means the Oirat Mongols then dominant around Kokonor.

BuildingsKokonor steppe (Qinghai)1661

The Kalmyk tent: folding sticks inside, rough wool outside

Figure A shows their dwelling: it is a Tartar tent made on the inside of small folding rods, and covered on the outside with a rough material of a kind of wool, bound tight with ropes.

Working translation

Original wording

Habitationem eorum Figura A. exprimit, estque tentorium Tartaricum intrinsecùs ex parvis plicatilibus bacillis confectum, exterius verò rudi ex certæ lanæ materia funibusque constricta contectum.

Editorial context

Describing Grueber's engraving of a Kalmyk dwelling (Figure A), Kircher explains the felt tent: a framework of small folding rods inside, covered outside with a coarse woollen material bound with ropes. This is the yurt, with its collapsible lattice wall, as reported by an eyewitness.

Athanasius Kircher, S.J. (China illustrata, Amsterdam 1667), from the oral report and drawings of Johann Grueber and Albert Dorville

BuildingsKokonor region (Qinghai)1661

Kalmuk Tatars with 'portable cities'

they immediately entered that vast and barren desert of Kalmuk, inhabited by the Kalmuk Tatars (the Eluths or Tatars of Koko-nor), who rove up and down it to rob the caravans, and at certain seasons settle with their portable cities on the banks of the rivers. … Their habitations are tents made with small sticks twisted or plaited together, and covered with a coarse woollen stuff bound together with cords.

Editorial context

The appendix summarises the letters of the Jesuit Johann Grueber, who crossed from China to Lhasa in 1661. Past the Yellow River his party entered the Kalmuk desert, whose people raided caravans and 'at certain seasons settle with their portable cities' on riverbanks. Their houses were tents of plaited sticks covered with coarse woollen cloth tied with cords, and the Jesuits sketched the people they met.

Account of Johann Grueber's travels, from Astley's Collection of Voyages (reprinted in Markham's appendix)
