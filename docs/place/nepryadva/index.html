The nameDon and Dnieper, Kulikovo field1380

Not grey wolves but the pagan Tatars: the 'Khinova' comes to Rus

The Khinova is marching on the Russian land. Grey wolves are howling; by the river Mecha they mean to cross into the Russian land. Those were not grey wolves: the pagan Tatars have come, they mean to pass through making war and take the whole Russian land.

Working translation

Original wording

Идетъ Хинова на Рускую землю. Сѣріе волци воютъ, на рѣкѣ хотятъ на Мечи поступити въ Рускую землю. То ти были не сѣріе волци, придоша поганые Татарове, хотятъ проити воюючи, взяти всю землю Рускую.

Editorial context

The poet builds the Horde's approach from omens: creaking carts between the Don and the Dnieper, the 'Khinova' marching on the Russian land, grey wolves howling by the river Mecha. Then he turns the image: those were not grey wolves but the pagan Tatars, come to take the whole Russian land. Sreznevsky's commentary suggests 'Khinova', an old poetic name for steppe enemies also used in the Lay of Igor, may hide the name of the Huns.

Zadonshchina (Kirillo-Belozersk text), ed. I. I. Sreznevsky, 1858
See the original page (p. 21)

About Golden Horde army of Mamai (Tatars)

Keep in mind

Poetic imagery. The manuscript reads 'хинела'; 'Хинова' is Sreznevsky's correction (footnote on the same page), and the Huns etymology is his guess (commentary, n54 p.49).

The nameKulikovo Field (Tula region)1380

Kulikovo: 'Ishmaelites' ruled by Mamai behind a puppet khan

the pagan Ishmaelite race rises against the Christians: for a certain feeble man reigns among them, and Prince Mamai does everything among them … and Grand Prince Dmitry, with his brother Vladimir, arrayed his regiments against the pagan Polovtsy

Working translation

Original wording

въздвизается на крестьяны Измаилтескии родъ поганыи: нѣкоему убо у нихъ худу царствующу, а все дѣющу у нихъ князю Мамаю … великии же князь Дмитрии, съ братомъ своимъ съ Володимеромъ, изрядивъ полкы противу поганыхъ Половець

Editorial context

The younger Novgorod text on the battle of Kulikovo explains the politics of the Horde: a weak khan reigned while Mamai did everything. It calls Mamai's army the pagan 'Ishmaelite race' and 'sons of Hagar', and when Dmitry arrays his troops it is 'against the pagan Polovtsy'. Old names for steppe enemies were simply reused for the Tatars.

Novgorod First Chronicle, younger redaction (PSRL vol. 3, 1841)

Daily lifeKulikovo field (Tula region)1380

The Tatars' lament: no more tribute, and their camels and sugar carried off

Now, brothers, we shall not be in our own land nor see our children, nor march in war on Rus, nor ask tribute of the Russian people. … for the sons of Rus have plundered the Tatar treasures, and armour, and horses, and oxen, and camels, and wine, and sugar, and costly finery.

Working translation

Original wording

Уже намъ, братіе, въ земли своей не бывать и дѣтей своихъ не видать, а въ Русь ратью намъ не хаживать, и выхода намъ у Рускихъ людей не прашивать. … уже бо Рускіе сынове разграбиша Татарскія узорочья, и доспѣхи, и кони, и волы, и верблуды, и вино, и сахаръ, и дорогое узорочіе.

Editorial context

The poet gives the defeated Tatars a lament as they flee toward the sea: they will never again see their land or children, never again raid Rus or collect the 'vykhod', the tribute Russian princes paid the Horde. The Russians have plundered their armour, horses, oxen, camels, wine, sugar and treasures. The list shows what a Horde army carried with it: herds, camels and luxury goods from trade.

Zadonshchina (Kirillo-Belozersk text), ed. I. I. Sreznevsky, 1858
See the original page (p. 26)

About Golden Horde Tatars (as imagined by the Russian poet)

Keep in mind

A speech invented by the poet for the enemy; the 'vykhod' was in fact paid to the Horde again after 1382.
