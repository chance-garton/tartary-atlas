Strange talesNorthern Asia1559 (Ramusio's commentary)

Prester John between Gog and Magog on the old sea charts

Ramusio notes that Marco Polo called the northern lord Uncan 'Prester John' and put his seat in two regions, Og and Magog. He adds that on all the sea charts made 200 and 300 years earlier, Prester John is placed in the north above India, between the Ganges and the Indus, with no mention of an Ethiopian Prester John; on the next page he calls these 'the provinces of Gog and Magog, that is, of the Tartars'.

where Messer Marco Polo says that in the north there was a great lord called Uncan, whose name some take to mean Prester John in our language, and that his principal seat was in two regions, Og and Magog: it should be known that on all those sea charts one sees today, made 200 and 300 years ago, this Prester John is placed in the north and above India

Strange talesNorthern and central Asia1711 to 1730

Gog and Magog in Tartar speech

Strahlenberg says the Tobolsk Tartars told him they call peoples on this side of the mountains 'Ma-Tschudi' and those beyond 'Ja-gougi and Ma-gougi', and he concludes that these words are the same as the biblical Gog and Magog. Elsewhere he suggests that Gog was the Tartars' legendary ancestor Oghuz Khan. It is a learned attempt to find the Bible's northern nations among the Tartars.

The same Tartars have also told me, that they use, in the like Manner, the Name Ma-Tschudi, … And when they, at the same Time, would denote the Heathen roving Scythian Nations, who inhabited the other Side of that Mountain, they call them Gaed-Tschudi, Ma-schudi or Ja-gougi and Ma-gougi; And these Words then signify the very same as Gog and Magog,

Who Tartars of Tobolsk (his informants)

Heard itPhilipp Johann von Strahlenberg (English translation, 1738)

Keep in mind. The informants' words are filtered through his own etymologies, which are speculative.

The nameEurope; northern Asia1730 (translator's preface)

'We are no other than a Colony of Tatars'

The anonymous English translator argues that Europeans should despise the Tatars less because Europeans descend from them: the Cimbrians, Celts, Gauls, Goths, Huns, Vandals and Franks were all 'swarms from the same hive' out of Tatary. On the next page he even toys with the idea that the Saxons were akin to the Uzbeks. This is 18th-century speculation about 'Scythian' origins, using 'Tatar' for any northern Asian people.

Our Contempt for the Tatars would still lessen perhaps, did we consider how nearly we stand related to them: that our Ancestors came originally from the North of Asia, and that our Customs, Laws, and way of Living were formerly the same with theirs: In short, that we are no other than a Colony of Tatars.

Who Europeans and 'Tatars' in the translator's broad sense

Read itThe anonymous English translator's preface (1730), not Abu'l Ghazi

Keep in mind. The translator's own theory, offered without evidence; nothing in Abu'l Ghazi's text says this.

BuildingsNorthern and central Asia1730

Tartar towns of wood and sun-dried brick

Explaining why old place names in Marco Polo and Rubruck cannot be found on the ground, Strahlenberg says that most Tartar peoples roamed, their khans never stayed long in one place, and their towns were built of wood or sun-dried brick. Centuries of wars, he says, destroyed them so thoroughly that not even ruins remain. This is his general judgement, not a report of a particular site.

…because most of the Tartarian Nations roving about, and the Residences of their Chams being never fix'd long in one Place, their Cities and Towns being also compos'd of Houses or Huts, built either of Wood or Bricks, slightly dry'd in the Sun, it is, therefore, no Wonder that … their Cities and Towns have been so destroy'd, that even the Ruins of them are no more to be seen.

Who nomadic 'Tartarian nations' in general

Read itPhilipp Johann von Strahlenberg (English translation, 1738)

The nameSiberia and northern Asia1751

'All the land was Tartary, and all the peoples Tartars'

In his preface Gmelin explains why Peter the Great sent the expedition. Before it, he says, European geographers knew nothing of the far north: every land there was simply 'Tartary' and every people 'Tartars'. On the next page he adds that in Russia itself people talk far less about 'Tartary' than elsewhere, the way real experts talk less about hidden qualities than those still in the dark.

In the northernmost lands the geographers had nothing but darkness. All the land was Tartary, and all the peoples of those regions Tartars.

Original wording

In den Nordlichsten Ländern war bey den Erdbeschreibern nichts als Dunkelheit. Alles Land war Tatarey, und alle Völker selbiger Gegenden Tataren.

Who Everyone in northern Asia, as European maps saw them

Read itJohann Georg Gmelin, preface (German original, Göttingen, 1751)
