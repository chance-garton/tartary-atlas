A wall of brushwood built by captives at Esztergom

Esztergom, the richest city in Hungary, had fortified itself with ditches, walls and wooden towers. Roger says the Tartars had their captives carry bundles of branches and pile them into a high wall along the edge of the ditch in a single effort, then set thirty stone-throwing machines behind it and pounded the city and its wooden towers day and night. The builders of this siege work, he notes, were the prisoners.

Meanwhile the people of Esztergom had fortified themselves very strongly with ditches, walls and wooden towers. … Then one day the Tartars surrounded the city, and the captives who were with them carried so many bundles of branches that on one side of the city, on the brink of the ditch, they built a high wall of the bundles all at once, and immediately behind that wall they set the thirty machines already mentioned

CitiesEsztergom1242

Wooden houses burned, stone palaces stormed

Seeing they could not hold the town, the Hungarian, French and Lombard citizens who ran Esztergom burned the suburbs and the many wooden houses right up to the stone palaces, destroyed their cloth and goods, buried their gold and retreated into the palaces. Enraged at losing the plunder, the Tartars stormed the palaces; Roger doubts fifteen people survived. The castle itself, held by a Spanish count with crossbowmen, was not taken.

But the Hungarians, the French and the Lombards, who were as it were the masters of the city, seeing that they could not defend themselves, burned the suburbs and the wooden houses, of which there were many, right up to the palaces of the city. … Afterwards they began to storm the palaces. These were quickly taken, and I do not believe, to tell the truth, that fifteen people of the whole city survived
