The nameIsker (Sibir), near Tobolsk1760s

'Sibir' was not the Tatars' name for their capital

Müller corrects a common belief that 'Sibir' is the Tatar word for the capital. When he asked the Tobolsk Tatars, they did not know the word at all; they called the place Isker. He thinks the name Sibir arose in Russia, taken from the Permians or Zyrians who first brought news of the land, and was only later attached to the Tatar capital.

It is commonly believed that Sibir is the Tatar name of the place, meaning a capital or residence. But if one asks the Tobolsk Tatars about this, it is quite unknown to them. They call the place Isker instead

Working translation

Original wording

Man glaubt insgemein, Sibir sey die Tatarische Benennung des Orts, welche soviel, als eine Hauptstadt, oder Residenz, bedeute. Fraget man aber die Tobolskischen Tataren nach diesem Umstande, so ist ihnen solcher ganz unbekannt. Sie nennen vielmehr den Ort Isker

Heard itGerhard Friedrich Müller (Sammlung Russischer Geschichte, vol. 6, 1761-1762)

BuildingsIsker (Sibir), near Tobolsk1730s

Houses of wood or unburnt brick, leaving no trace

Inside the ramparts of Isker Müller found no remains of houses, only uneven ground and some pits that might have been cellars. He concludes that any buildings must have been of wood in the Siberian Tatar manner, or of unburnt brick in the Bukharan way, since nothing of them survives, and notes that some pits were dug later by treasure seekers, not always in vain.

If so, they must have been built only of wood in the Siberian Tatar manner, or of unburnt bricks after the Bukharan custom, since nothing more is to be seen of them. In some places there are pits which may have served as cellars. But it is also said that some pits were made in recent times, because people searched for treasure there

Working translation

Original wording

Wenn solches ist, so müssen sie nach Sibirisch-Tatarischer Art nur von Holze, oder nach Bucharischer Gewohnheit von ungebrannten Ziegeln, gebauet gewesen seyn, weil nichts davon mehr bemerket wird. An einigen Orten sind Gruben, welche zu Kellern gedienet haben können. Man sagt aber auch, daß einige Gruben in neuern Zeiten gemacht seyn, weil man daselbst Schätze gesuchet

Saw itGerhard Friedrich Müller (Sammlung Russischer Geschichte, vol. 6, 1761-1762)

CitiesIsker (Kashlyk), near Tobolskbefore 1580 (narrated c. 1700)

Who built Kashlyk, the Tatar capital of Sibir

The chronicle credits a Tatar ruler, Mamet, with founding the town of Kashlyk at the mouth of the little Sibirka river after defeating a khan of Kazan. From this town, it says, the whole land took the name Sibir. The author says he took this from a 'Muslim history', meaning Tatar tradition.

Tsar Mamet defeated Alim, tsar of Kazan, and built the town of Kashlyk at the mouth of the little river Sibirka, spreading his realm in Siberia and making subjects; and from then Siberia and its tsars began to be more famous, as the Muslim history tells.

Working translation

Original wording

Маметь царь казанскаго царя Алима побѣдилъ, и на усть рѣчки Сибирки градъ Кашлыкъ учинилъ, царство въ Сибири распространивъ и поддани учинилъ; и оттолѣ нача славнѣе зватися Сибирь и цари, ихъ же бусурманская исторія повѣдаетъ.

Read itKungur (Remezov) chronicle, article 17 (1880 printed text)

Keep in mind

CitiesSibir (Isker), near Tobolsk1581 to 1582

Yermak enters an empty capital

After routing Kuchum's army on the Irtysh, Yermak marched on Sibir, 'the residence of the Tartar princes', expecting a hard fight. Instead the news of the defeat had emptied it; he rode in and sat on the Khan's throne unopposed, and neighbouring peoples came in to swear allegiance and pay tribute.

delay to Sibir, the residence of the Tartar princes … But the news of the late defeat had diffused universal consternation, and Sibir was entirely deserted … he himself soon after made his triumphant entry, and seated himself upon the throne without the least opposition.

Read itWilliam Coxe (compiling G. F. Müller and J. E. Fischer)

CitiesIsker (Kashlyk), near Tobolsk15th to 16th century (written 1740s)

Isker, the real Tatar name of the town of Sibir

Müller says Khan Makhmet moved the princely seat from the Tura to a high headland on the Irtysh and fortified it, because the old capital Chimgi (Tyumen), though protected by ravines, a rampart and ditches, lay on flat ground. The ruins could still be seen above Tobolsk. He rejects the idea that 'Sibir' is a Tatar word for 'capital': the Tatars call the fortress Isker, and the name Kashlyk used in the Tobolsk chronicle was, he says, used by no people he knew.

This place is the so-called old Tatar town and fortress of Sibir, whose remains can still be seen upriver of Tobolsk … The true Tatar name of this old fortress is Isker, by which name the Tatars call it to this day.

Working translation

Original wording

Сіе мѣсто есть такъ именуемое старинной Татарской городъ и крѣпость Сибирь, котораго остатки повыше Тобольска … еще и теперь видны. … Подлинное Татарское имя сей старинной крѣпости есть Искеръ, которымъ именемъ Татаре еще и понынѣ оную называютъ.

Saw itGerhard Friedrich Müller (Russian edition, 1787 issue)

BuildingsIsker (Kashlyk), near Tobolsk1675

Kuchum's empty capital and the mosque the Tatars still kept

Just up the Irtysh from Tobolsk, by a stream called Sibirka that he thought gave all Siberia its name, Spafarii notes the empty little town and earthworks of Kuchum, the Tatar khan who ruled when Yermak's Cossacks came. The site was naturally strong, an island, but deserted. Only the Tobolsk Tatars still renewed a mosque there and came to worship. The editor adds that the historian Müller later described these ruins.

near the stream lies the empty little town and earthworks of Kuchum, tsar of Siberia, who ruled at the time when Yermak came into Siberia; and the place is very strong: it is an island, yet now it lies empty, only the Tobolsk Tatars, in remembrance of their unclean Mahometan faith, renewed a mosque before this and renew it now, in which they come and perform their rites according to their unclean faith.

Working translation

Original wording

близъ рѣчки лежитъ пустой городокъ и шанцы Кучюма царя Сибирского, который владѣлъ въ то время, какъ приходилъ Ермакъ въ Сибирь; и мѣсто то самое крѣпкое: островъ есть, однакожъ нынѣ лежитъ пусто, толко Тоболскія Татары для воспоминанія нечистые ихъ вѣры магмецкіе обновили прежъ сего и нынѣ обновляютъ мечеть, въ которой по ихъ нечистой вѣрѣ приходяще дѣйствуютъ.

Heard itNikolai Spafarii (ed. Yu. V. Arsenyev, 1882)

CitiesIsker (Sibir), near Tobolsk1730s

Isker, the khan's capital: 'if one can call it a town'

Müller visited the remains of Isker, the capital Yermak's Cossacks entered in 1582, on a steep height of the Irtysh above Tobolsk. He describes a small round hill protected on the landward side by a triple rampart and ditches, the river and a ravine guarding the other sides, and an interior only a few dozen fathoms across, room for little more than the khan, his family and servants. Part of it had fallen into the river.

The remains of this former capital of the land, if one can indeed call a place such as this seems to have been a town, can still be seen today.

Working translation

Original wording

Die Ueberbleibsel dieser ehmahligen Haupt-Stadt des Landes, wenn man anders einen Ort, so wie dieser gewesen zu seyn scheinet, eine Stadt nennen kann, sind noch heut zu Tage zu sehen.

Saw itGerhard Friedrich Müller (Sammlung Russischer Geschichte, vol. 6, 1761-1762)

CitiesIsker (Sibir), near TobolskAbout 1495 (legendary retrospect)

Sibir, 'the chief town', built on the Irtysh

Esipov continues: Taibuga's descendant Mamet killed Upak (Ibak, khan of Tyumen, whom Esipov calls 'the Kazan tsar'), destroyed his own town of Chingiden, went deeper into Siberia and built a town on the Irtysh, naming it Sibir, 'that is, the chief one'. This is the Tatar capital also called Isker or Kashlyk, which Yermak took in 1582. The Russian name for the whole region comes from this Tatar town.

Mamet killed Upak, the Kazan tsar, and destroyed his own town Chingiden and went away from there into the interior of the Siberian land and built himself a town on the river Irtysh and named it the town of Sibir, that is, the chief one; and the tsar lived in it many years

Working translation

Original wording

Маметъ Казанского царя уби Упака и градъ свой Чингиденъ разруши и отиде оттуду внутрь Сибирския земли и постави себѣ градъ на рѣке Иртише и назва его градъ Сибирь, сиречь началный; и живяше в немъ царь лѣта многа

Legend or rumourSavva Esipov, Siberian Chronicle (1636), in Sibirskie letopisi (1907)

About Mamet (Muhammad), Taibugid prince; Upak (Ibak), 'the Kazan tsar'

Keep in mind

Esipov's 'Kazan tsar' Upak is Ibak of the Shibanid dynasty; the meaning 'chief' for Sibir is Esipov's gloss, not a proven etymology.

CitiesIsker (Sibir), near TobolskMay 1734

Old Sibir: a rampart and a name

Gmelin and Müller rode 18 versts up the Irtysh from Tobolsk to where, by common report, the old town of Sibir had stood, seat of Siberia's former rulers. Apart from traces of a rampart they saw nothing at all. Gmelin suggests that both the little Sibirka stream and the whole province of Siberia took their names from this vanished town.

where, according to common report, the old town of Sibir stood, which is said to have been the seat of the former rulers of Siberia... Apart from some traces of a rampart that had been thrown up there, we could see nothing.

Working translation

Original wording

wo der gemeinen Sage nach die alte Stadt Sibir gestanden hat, welche der Sitz der ehemahligen Sibirischen Beherrscher gewesen seyn soll. … Außer einigen Spuren von einem Walle, der daselbst aufgeworfen war, konnten wir nichts sehen.

Saw itJohann Georg Gmelin (German original, Göttingen, 1751)

CitiesTobolsk (Isker, the old Sibir)1580s

Sibir, 'the capital of the Tartars'

Cochrane retells the Cossack Yermak's conquest: after taking Tyumen he marched on Sibir, 'the capital of the Tartars', twelve miles from where Tobolsk now stands, entered it in triumph on 26 October and 'from the head of a banditti, became a sovereign prince'. He notes that the town's name 'seems to have given name' to all Asiatic Russia. He admits the history is translated from a note printed on a map given him by the governor of Tobolsk.

Yermak instantly directed his march against Sibir, the capital of the Tartars, distant twelve miles, and on the 26th October entered it in triumph, received the oath of allegiance of his new subjects, and, from the head of a banditti, became a sovereign prince.

Read itJohn Dundas Cochrane (1824), translating a biographical note on a map
