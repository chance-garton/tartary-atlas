BuildingsTash Rabat, Kyrgyzstanearly 15th century

Tash Rabat: a Moghul khan's stone caravanserai

Mirza Haidar credits Muhammad Khan, the khan who pushed the Moghuls into Islam, with building a rabat (travellers' lodge) of huge stones north of the Chadir Kul pass, with a tall entrance hall, a dome, cells and a mosque. He had seen it himself. The editor's note matches it with the ruin now called Tash Rabat and rejects a later claim that it was a converted Hindu temple, since no manuscript says so.

In the construction of this building he employed stones of great size, the like of which are only to be seen in the temples … of Kashmir. The Rabat contains an entrance hall 20 gaz in height. … The whole building is of stone, and over the doors there are huge solid blocks of stone, which I thought very wonderful, before I had seen the temples in Kashmir.

Saw itMirza Haidar Dughlat (translated by E. Denison Ross, 1895)

Daily lifeTian Shan, west of Kashgar1813

Kirghiz snow-treaders keep the pass open

In winter the snow in the passes west of Kashgar lay a man's height deep or more. Izzet Ullah describes Kirghiz posted along the road whose job was to trample the snow hard so caravans could cross. Even so, several horses died, including his own.

The Kirghizes tread the snow down so as to make it hard, and then the caravans pass; a certain number of Kirghizes are appointed to this duty,

Saw itMir Izzet Ullah (translated by H. H. Wilson)

BuildingsTian Shan, between Kashgar and Osh1813

Felt tents pitched beside a domed tomb

Crossing the mountains west of Kashgar in deep winter, Izzet Ullah notes Kirghiz felt tents lining the road (p. 321). At the stage called Mizar Gumbaz he found a domed building with Kirghiz tombs and the living Kirghiz camped in tents beside it: permanent structures for the dead, portable ones for the living.

Mizar Gumbuz, … west by north, eight hours. A building with a cupola and Kirghiz tombs; their tents were also pitched near the building; supplies plentiful;

Saw itMir Izzet Ullah (translated by H. H. Wilson)
