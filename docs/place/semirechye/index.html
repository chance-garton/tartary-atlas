Turkistan after the Tatars: nothing but ruins

The editor quotes the fourteenth-century Arab geographer Shihab al-Din (al-Umari), who was told by a traveller that the land had been ruined 'by the arms of the Tatars'. From a distance a village looks well built, but up close its houses are empty, and the people are now all herders. Elias adds that the towns had been built by earlier settled peoples such as the Uighurs and Kara-Khitai, before the Mongol nomads made it their home.

Since the region has been devastated by the arms of the Tatars," he writes, "it is inhabited only by a scanty population. According to what I have been assured by a man who has travelled through the country, there is nothing to be seen in Turkistan but ruins, and more or less obliterated remains.
