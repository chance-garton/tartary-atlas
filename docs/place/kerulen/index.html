What the sources say here: 1 passage

BuildingsKerulen River, Mongolia1247

Old walls of 'the former dynasty' and earth-roofed huts on the Kerulen

Zhang passed the ruined base of a long wall stretching out of sight, which he credits to 'the former dynasty', meaning the Jurchen Jin, as their outer rampart. On the Kerulen he found a mixed population of 'barbarians' and Chinese living in a few houses roofed with earth and growing a little hemp and wheat.

After four stages there is the ruined base of a long wall, stretching on endlessly as far as one can see; this too is an outer rampart built by the former dynasty... The people along the river are a mix of fan (steppe peoples) and Han Chinese. There are a few houses, all roofed with earth, and they also do some farming, of hemp and wheat, and that is all.

Working translation

Original wording

四驛有長城䫋址望之綿延不盡亦前朝所築之外堡也…瀕河之民雜以蕃漢稍有屋室皆以土冐之亦頗有種藝麻麥而巳

Zhang Dehui, Record of a Journey (Qiujian ji juan 100 = Yutang jiahua juan 8; Sibu congkan text, Kanseki Repository transcription)

About Mongols and Chinese living together

Keep in mind

'Former dynasty' (前朝) here means the Jin, whose border walls ran across Inner Mongolia.
