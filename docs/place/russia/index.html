Raiding the Land of Darkness on mares with foals

Far to the north, Polo says, lies a Land of Darkness where no sun, moon or stars are seen. Tartars raid it riding mares whose foals they leave at the border, trusting the mares to find the way back to their young. The pale inhabitants hunt sables, ermine and black fox for the fur trade. Yule suggests the story garbles the Arctic winter night.

The Tartars however sometimes visit the country, and they do it in this way. They enter the region riding mares that have foals, and these foals they leave behind. After taking all the plunder that they can get, they find their way back by help of the mares, which are all eager to get back to their foals

Who Tartars (Golden Horde raiders) and northern fur hunters

Legend or rumourMarco Polo (translated by Henry Yule, 1871)

Keep in mind. Pure hearsay; the mare-and-foal trick is an old travellers' tale also told of Alexander the Great.

Old maps of Tartary drawn before even the Russians knew it

In his preface Gerritsz attacks a German doctor, Helisaeus Röslin, who claimed ships could sail round Nova Zembla to Cathay. Röslin's 'absurdities', he says, rest on maps of Tartary drawn in very old times, when the Russians themselves did not know the country. A sharp early warning that the big 'Tartary' of old maps was guesswork.

This was also done by a certain Heliseus Röslin, a doctor of Hanau … in which (after many absurdities which he grounds on the maps drawn of Tartary from very old times, when the Russians themselves were unacquainted with it) he says: that the nearer the pole the warmer it is in summer time

Olla Billa: the Tartar war cry

Fletcher describes Tartar raiders setting dummy riders on spare horses to look more numerous, and charging with the cry 'Olla Billa' (Allah, bi-llah), which he translates 'God help us'. He contrasts their refusal to surrender with the behaviour of Russian and Turkish soldiers.

When they make any onset their manner is to make a great shoote, crying all out together, Olla Billa, Olla Billa, God helpe us, God helpe us. They contemne death so much as that they chuse rather to die then to yeeld to their enimie, and are seene when they are slaine to bite the very weapon

Pigs as a border defence

Russian borderers, Fletcher says, kept almost nothing but pigs, because Muslim Tartar raiders would not touch or drive them off. He also notes that the Crimean khan was now largely picked by the Ottoman sultan and paid him a tenth of his war booty.

The Russe borders (being used to their invasions lightly every yeere in the sommer) keepe fewe other cattel on the border parts save swine onely, which the Tartar will not touch nor drive away with him ; for that he is of the Turkish religion, and will eate no swines flesh.

Sober Tatars who still enjoy a drink

Commenting on Ibn Fadlan's horror at Rus drinking, Frähn notes that not all Muslims keep the ban on wine. Among the Tatars of Russia, he says, 'sober as this people is', he met many who did not refuse wine and strong drink, though he never saw them drink too much. It is a small firsthand note on real Tatars by a scholar who knew them.

And so among the Tatars of Russia too, sober as this people is, I have found not a few who did not scorn wine and such strong drinks, though I never saw them exceed moderation.

Strange talesSouthern frontier of Russia1570s

'Away with those new devils and their thundering puffs'

Horsey claims he persuaded Ivan IV to arm captured Scottish mercenaries and send them against the Crimean Tartars. Twelve hundred Scots with guns, he boasts, did better than 12,000 Russian archers, and the Tartars, unused to firearms, fled crying out against the 'new devils'.

Twelve hundred of them did better service against the Tartor then 12 thowsand Russes, with their shortte bowe and arowes. The Crim, not knowinge then the use of peece and pistolls, stroken dead of their horses with shott they sawe not, cried : " Awaye with those new divells that com with their thunderinge puffs ;"

Who Crimean Tartars

Heard itSir Jerome Horsey (memoir; ed. E. A. Bond, 1856)

Keep in mind. A boastful anecdote; the Crimean khanate had firearms and Ottoman gunners by this time.
