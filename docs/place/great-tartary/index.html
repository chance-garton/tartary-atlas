Great Tartary

Label for Asiatic Tartary on 17th-18th century maps

A name, not a spot on the map. Its edges moved from map to map. See the many Tartarys.

The nameSiberia and Great Tartary1726

Thirty 'Tartarian' languages from the Black Sea to Japan

The first, then, is a Specimen of the Harmony of the Tartarian Languages, consisting of 30 languages, which begin at the Black Sea and end at the Sea of Japan. … (since various peoples live in Siberia and Tartary who, besides the main languages in use among them, have their own particular ones as well)

Working translation

Original wording

Das erste nun, ist ein Specimen Harmoniae linguarum Tartaricarum, bestehend in 30 Sprachen, die sich anheben von dem Schwartzen und sich endigen bey dem Japanischen Meer. … (indem unterschiedliche Völckerschaften in Siberien und der Tartarey vorhanden sind, welche nebst denen Haupt-Sprachen, so unter ihnen gebräuchlich sind, ihre eigene oder particuliere daneben haben)

Editorial context

Strahlenberg announces the first part of his planned work: a comparative table of thirty languages spoken between the Black Sea and the Sea of Japan, which he calls the 'Tartarian' languages. He explains that the many peoples of Siberia and Tartary each have their own language besides the main languages used among them, and a footnote adds that they therefore cannot all properly go under the name of Tartars.

Philipp Johann von Strahlenberg (Vorbericht, Stockholm 1726)

The nameGreat Tartary1734

Where the name 'Tartary' came from: a river, a province or a Syriac word

Tartary, which got its name perhaps from the river Tartar …, or according to Hayton's account from a province so called, or from the Syriac word Tartaroye, which means a numerous nation, is divided by some into old and new Tartary, likewise into little and great, or into that lying on this side and beyond Mount Imaus

Working translation

Original wording

Die Tartarey, welche etwan von dem Fluß Tartar …, oder nach Haithony Bericht von einer also genannten Provintz, oder von dem syrischen Wort Tartaroye, welches eine zahlreiche Nation bedeutet, seinen Nahmen bekommen, wird von einigen in die alte und neue Tartarey, item in die kleine und grosse, oder in dieß- und jenseits des Bergs Imaus gelegene, eingetheilet

Editorial context

Before describing China, the enlarged 1734 edition inserts a compiled chapter on Tartary 'from the most approved historians'. It offers three origins for the name: a river called Tartar, a province of that name mentioned by Hayton, or a Syriac word 'Tartaroye' meaning a numerous nation. It then divides Tartary into old and new, little and great, stretching from Jesso (Hokkaido) to the Sea of Azov, Poland and Russia.

Compiled chapter in the 1734 enlarged edition of Brand's journey (from earlier historians)
