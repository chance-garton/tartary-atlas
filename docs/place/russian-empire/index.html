Daily lifeRussian frontierc. 1238

Conquered men made to fight, and the rest 'called Tartars'

Julian explains how the Tartar army grows: they kill the rulers of conquered lands at once, drive the strong men into battle in front of them, leave the weaker men to farm, give each of them ten or more women of the dead, and order that 'from now on they be called Tartars'. Soldiers who retreat are killed by the Tartars, so they prefer to die fighting. Instead of storming strong castles they strip the land and force the local people to attack the castle.

assigning ten or more to each, and they order them that from now on they be called Tartars. … They do not storm fortified castles, but first lay waste the land and plunder the people, and compel the gathered people of that same land to fight to take the castle.

Friar Julian: the Tartars' first land was 'Gothia'

Friar Julian, a Hungarian Dominican who had travelled to the edge of Rus' looking for the pagan 'Greater Hungarians', tells the papal legate what he learned 'from many': that the Tartars once lived where the Cumans now live, are 'in truth' called Ishmaelites but wish to be called Tartars, and came out of a land called 'Gothia'. He then tells a tangled origin story of a duke named Gurgutha whose vengeance war launched their conquests.

For it is reported by many that the Tartars, who formerly lived in the land the Cumans now inhabit, are in truth called Ishmaelites, but now wish to be called Tartars. The land from which they first came out is called Gothia

Keep in mind

Hearsay gathered on the Russian frontier; the 'Ishmaelite' label is biblical classification, and the Gurgutha story has no basis in Mongol history. The OCR is badly garbled and the wording is reconstructed from it.

The nameRussian Empire1807

A name Tatars called dishonourable

In the written instructions the Academy gave Klaproth, a scholar remarks that peoples sometimes adopt names their neighbours use: just as the Tartar nations in Russia call themselves 'Tartars' for outsiders, though at home they consider the name dishonourable.

They perhaps assume it for the purpose of accommodating their neighbours, as the Tartar nations in Russia give themselves the name of Tartars, which in their own countries they consider as dishonourable.

Read itInstructions for Klaproth from the St Petersburg Academy of Sciences (section II, probably by A. C. Lehrberg or Ph. Krug), printed in Klaproth's Travels

Daily lifeRussian frontierc. 1238

They seem to rain arrows rather than shoot them

Julian's report on how the Tartars fight: they shoot farther than other nations, and in the first clash 'do not seem to shoot but to rain arrows', while swords and lances are said to be less suited to them. Their army is organised so that one Tartar commands ten men and a centurion commands a hundred, so that spies cannot hide among them.

it is said that they shoot farther than other nations are accustomed to. In the first clash of battle, it is said, they do not shoot, but seem as it were to rain arrows.
