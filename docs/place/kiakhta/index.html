CitiesKyakhta (Altanbulag, Mongolia)1770s

Maimachin: a wooden frontier town guarded by Mongols

Editorial contextFollowing Pallas, Coxe describes the Chinese trading town of Maimachin facing Russian Kiakhta, with frontier posts inscribed in Russian and Manchu. Its only defence was a wooden wall and a small ditch dug in 1756 during the Qing wars with the Kalmyks (Dzungars), and its gates were watched by a garrison of Mongols in tattered clothes armed with clubs.

Maimatschin has no other fortification than a wooden wall, and a small ditch of about three feet broad; the latter was dug in the year 1756, during the war between the Chinese and the Calmucs … over each of these gates there is a wooden guard-house for the Chinese garrison, which consists of Mongols in tattered clothes, and armed with clubs.

William Coxe (from P. S. Pallas's travels)

The nameKyakhta, Buryatia1823

'Mongul Tartary' beyond Lake Baikal

Editorial contextHolman calls the whole country between Lake Baikal and the trading towns of Kiakhta (Russian) and Maimachin (Chinese) 'Mongul Tartary', wild and rugged and full of wild animals. Here the old map name Tartary survives as a regional label. A page earlier he describes the Buryats as 'of Mongul origin'.

The whole country between these points and the Baikal, which has the name of Mongul Tartary, is wild and rugged in the extreme, and often dangerous to the traveller in consequence of the great number of wild animals that inhabit it.

James Holman (1825)
