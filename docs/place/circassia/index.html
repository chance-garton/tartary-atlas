Anapa: mud cabins and a medley of peoples

Anapa, the first Russian fortress on the Circassian coast, is described from notes by the expedition's historiographer: most houses were cabins of wood and mud thatched with maize leaves or reeds, and its 2,000 people included Circassians, Nogai Tatars, Kalmyks, Kumyks, Kabardians, 'Kazannes', Dagestanis and Bukharans. Spencer admits he did not land there himself.

The greater number of the houses of the town are mere cabins, constructed of wood and mud, thatched with the leaves of Indian corn or reeds. The inhabitants, consisting of Circassians, Nogay Tartars, Kalmucks, Komouks, Kabartis, Kazannes, Demikarponis, Daghistanes, Boukhares, with a few Greeks, Armenians, and Russians, speaking a medley of tongues

Heard itEdmund Spencer, from the expedition historiographer's notes

The nameCircassia (northwest Caucasus)1836

Crimean and Nogai refugees in the Caucasus

Explaining why his book includes a Tatar vocabulary, Spencer says many Circassians knew the language and that Crimean and Nogai Tatars who had fled Russian rule were settled throughout the Circassian provinces. He adds that the word lists should disprove the common idea that Circassians and Tatars shared an origin.

many of the Circassians are not only acquainted with it, but we every where find, throughout the whole of these provinces, Krim and Nogay Tartars domiciliated, who, on the subjection of their country to the rule of Russia, fled into the Caucasus.

Heard itEdmund Spencer
