BuildingsChusovaya River, Perm Krai1692

Log houses with windows of ice

The Vogul 'Tartars' lived in square log rooms like Russian peasant huts, with an open hearth instead of an oven. Once the fire burned down to coals, they sealed the smoke-hole with a sheet of ice, which kept the heat in and let daylight through. Wide benches around the walls served as seats by day and beds by night.

Their Dwellings are quadrangular wooden Rooms, like those of the Russian Peasants; only instead of Ovens they use Hearths, on which they burn Wood, and dress their Victuals. The Smoak-hole which is in the Roof, is covered with Ice, as soon as the Wood is burnt to a Coal, which keeps the warmth in the Room, and the light strikes through the bright and clear Ice.

Who Voguls (Mansi), called Siberian Tartars

Saw itEvert Ysbrants Ides (English translation, 1706)

Daily lifeChusovaya River, Perm Krai1692

Praying once a year is enough

Ides lodged a night with the Voguls and asked about their religion. Once a year they go into the woods, kill one animal of each kind, hang the carcass on a tree and bow to it, then feast; and they saw no reason to pray more often. On the same page he notes that a good hunting dog was laid to rest in its own little wooden hut raised on four props of earth.

Their whole Religious Worship consists in making an Offering according to their Custom once each Year: When they go in crouds into the Woods, and kill one of each Species of Beasts … they flea off the Skins, hang up the Carcass on a Tree, and fall prostrate to the Earth, and in their way, Pray to it … What need of Praying any oftner than once a Year, say they?

Who Voguls (Mansi), called Siberian Tartars

Saw itEvert Ysbrants Ides (English translation, 1706)

Keep in mind. Based on one night's conversation through interpreters.

BuildingsKungur, Perm Krai1823

Kungur's Tatar caves, now ice-cellars

On his way home Cochrane stopped at Kungur, a town of 1,100 houses. Before it became a provincial capital, he says, it was a favourite place of the Tartars, and the caves where they lived were still shown to visitors, now used as ice-cellars and storehouses.

Formerly it was the capital of a province, and previous to that period, a favourite place of the Tartars. The caves where they resided, are still shewn in the light of useful curiosities, inasmuch as they are converted into ice-cellars, and storehouses.

Who Tatars (former inhabitants)

Heard itJohn Dundas Cochrane (1824)

Keep in mind. Local tradition. Kungur is famous for its natural ice cave; the idea that Tatars lived in it is folklore, not evidence of Tatar cave cities.

The nameChusovaya River, Perm Krai1692

The 'first Siberian Tartars' are Voguls

As soon as he crosses from Europe into Asia, Ides meets his first 'Siberian Tartars' on the Chusovaya river and names them Wogulski. These are the Voguls, today's Mansi, who speak an Ugric language and are not Turkic Tatars at all. The passage shows how 'Tartar' worked for travellers: it meant almost any native, non-Russian people east of the Urals.

we came too, and touched at a pleasant Shoar on the 25th of May, amongst the first Siberian Tartars, called Wogulski. … But the Wogulskian Tartars to which this River led us, are stupid Heathens

Who Voguls (Mansi), called Siberian Tartars

Saw itEvert Ysbrants Ides (English translation, 1706)

Keep in mind. Ides's scorn ('stupid Heathens') is his own; he spent one night among them.
