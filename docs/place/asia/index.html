The nameAsia1768

'When a European speaks of Tatars, no one knows who is meant'

Fischer complains that Europeans mispronounce the name as 'Tartar', perhaps imagining it comes from Tartarus, an allusion he says is common in old and new histories. Worse, they give it to every Asian people beyond Persia and India up to the Pacific, however different in looks, way of life, religion and language. He even doubts whether any single people ever properly bore the name, noting that Batu's army that invaded Hungary were Mongols, not Tatars.

Perhaps they imagined that the name comes from Tartarus. … So it happens that when a European speaks of Tatars, one does not know whom he means. Because this name is so very general, I have often hesitated whether there was ever a particular people on earth that bore the proper name Tatar.

Working translation

Original wording

Vielleicht haben sie sich eingebildet, daß der name von Tartarus herkomt. … Solcher gestalt geschiehet es, daß wenn ein Europäer von Tataren redet, man nicht weiß von wem die rede ist. Weil dieser name so gar allgemein ist, so habe ofters bei mir angestanden, ob auch jemals ein besonder volk auf dem erdboden gewesen sei, das den eigentümlichen namen Tatar geführet.

Strange talesAsia (unspecified)c. 1580

A Tartar emperor in Asia who keeps Christian rites

The same informant adds that the 'Cham', emperor of the Tartars in Asia, lives not far from the Circassians and still keeps many Christian rites because his people were once converted. This is an echo of the medieval Prester John and Christian khan legends, repeated as current news.

He added, moreover, that the Cham, Emperor of the Tartars in Asia, is not far from that people, and still keeps much of the Christian rites, since they once came over to our faith.

Working translation

Original wording

Adiecit autem, Cham, Tartarorum in Asia Imperatorem, non procul ab ea gente abesse, qui multum adhuc rituum Christianorum retineat, cũ olim ad fidem nostram accesserint.
