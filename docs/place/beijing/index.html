It was first walled by the Mongols in 1267 with eleven gates; the Ming cut it down to nine gates, which is why its governor was called governor of the nine gates. So the 'Tartar' city of Beijing was a Mongol Yuan foundation reshaped by the Chinese Ming.

the Mongols, in 1267; it was then sixty li in circumference, and had eleven gates. It is for this reason that the governor of the Tartar town bears the title of governor of the nine gates.

So he caused the present city to be built close beside the old one, with only a river between them. Marco Polo (translated by Henry Yule, 1871)

Born in Beijing, but still Tartars

Editorial contextStaunton explains that the Qianlong emperor, though four generations removed from the conquest and born in Beijing, was seen by everyone, and saw himself, as a Tartar. Peking: the Tartar kings' city becomes the Northern Court

Editorial contextRicci explains why China had two capitals. The Ming founder ruled from Nanjing, but his son Yongle, who had guarded the northern frontier against the recently expelled Tartars, seized the throne and moved his seat north, fearing the Tartars would break out again to win back the empire. He chose the very city where the Tartar kings had lived and renamed it Peking, 'Northern Court'. The Mongol capital (Dadu, Khanbaliq) thus became Ming Beijing.

And since he had more strength and loyalty in the northern provinces, and it was likely that the Tartars would break out from there to recover their tyranny over the kingdom, he decided to settle in those parts, and indeed in the very city where the Tartar kings lived when they ruled the Chinese. He named that city Pequim, that is, Northern Court, on the model of the southern one called Nanquim.
