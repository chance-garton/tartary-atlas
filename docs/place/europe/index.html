The nameEurope; northern Asia1730 (translator's preface)

'We are no other than a Colony of Tatars'

The anonymous English translator argues that Europeans should despise the Tatars less because Europeans descend from them: the Cimbrians, Celts, Gauls, Goths, Huns, Vandals and Franks were all 'swarms from the same hive' out of Tatary. On the next page he even toys with the idea that the Saxons were akin to the Uzbeks. This is 18th-century speculation about 'Scythian' origins, using 'Tatar' for any northern Asian people.

Our Contempt for the Tatars would still lessen perhaps, did we consider how nearly we stand related to them: that our Ancestors came originally from the North of Asia, and that our Customs, Laws, and way of Living were formerly the same with theirs: In short, that we are no other than a Colony of Tatars.

Who Europeans and 'Tatars' in the translator's broad sense

Read itThe anonymous English translator's preface (1730), not Abu'l Ghazi

Keep in mind. The translator's own theory, offered without evidence; nothing in Abu'l Ghazi's text says this.

Strange talesEurope (Hungary and Poland)13th century poem, quoted 1839

A 13th-century poem calls the Tartars cannibals

In his notes d'Avezac prints lines, copied by Thomas Wright from a Cotton manuscript, of John of Garland's poem On the Triumphs of the Church. It paints the Tartars as Satan's household and the Church's plague who eat human flesh: no lion eats a lion and no wolf tears a wolf, but these men roast human limbs on spits. D'Avezac traces the poet's 'reliable' information to atrocity letters from priests in central Europe.

A people exceedingly savage, of Satan's household, a plague to the Church, at odds with the faith, in love with slaughter. … Hungry, man gnaws man; no lion fills its slack belly with lion's flesh; no wolf tears a wolf with its claw; this people turns limbs spitted on hazel skewers over the fire.

Strange talesEurope1241

Scholars decide they are the peoples before Antichrist

After the Hungarian disaster, Thomas says, the whole world trembled. Even the emperor Frederick II was said to be thinking not of resistance but of a hiding place. Learned men searched old writings and concluded, above all from the prophecies attributed to Methodius, that these were the peoples who must come before Antichrist, and cities began fortifying themselves in case the Tartars marched on Rome.

Even Frederick, the Roman emperor, is said to have thought not of resisting but of a hiding place. Then many learned men, searching the old writings, conjectured, above all from the sayings of Methodius the Martyr, that these were those peoples who must come before the advent of Antichrist.

Who Tartars as seen by Latin scholars

Keep in mind. The claim about Frederick is gossip ('is said'); the Methodius prophecy is the Pseudo-Methodius apocalypse, which linked enclosed northern nations with the end of the world.

Strange talesTibet and Mongolia (and Europe's scholars)1801

Scholars who moved Atlantis to Spitsbergen

Pallas rejects the theory, pushed by the Augustinian friar Georgi, that Tibetan and Mongol Buddhism grew out of Nestorian Christianity or Manichaeism, calling it a heap of rubble glued together in a polemical spirit. He lumps it with other learned fantasies: the Chinese and Indians as an Egyptian colony, the Lapps as Israelites, pagan gods as biblical patriarchs, and the recent placing of Atlantis and the first seat of all learning as far north as Spitsbergen.

With such learned bombast the Chinese and Indians have before now been made into an Egyptian colony, the Lapps into Israelites, the idols of heathendom into patriarchs of the Bible, and only recently the Atlanteans, and with them the first seat of all learning, have been moved as far as Spitsbergen

Original wording

Mit dergleichen gelehrtem Schwall sind wohl eher die Chineser und Indianer zu einer ägyptischen Colonie, die Lappländer zu Israeliten, die Götzen des Heydenthums zu Patriarchen der Bibel gemacht, und noch neulich die Atlanten und mit ihnen, der erste Sitz aller Wissenschaften bis nach Spitzbergen verlegt worden

Who Mongol and Tibetan Buddhists; European theorists

Read itPeter Simon Pallas (Sammlungen historischer Nachrichten über die mongolischen Völkerschaften, part 2, 1801)

Keep in mind. Pallas does not name the Atlantis author; it fits J.-S. Bailly's northern Atlantis letters of 1779.

The nameEurope and China1730

Blame Marco Polo and the friars

Strahlenberg blames Marco Polo and the thirteenth-century friars (Carpini, Rubruck) for spreading the 'Chinese conceit' that every steppe people is a Tartar. It is an early and explicit statement that 'Tartary' was an outsiders' label, not a nation's own name.

And because Marcus Paulus Venetus, when he was in China, was not rightly inform'd of the State of Things, he, as well as the Monks sent into Tartary, viz. Plan, Carpin, Rubriquis, &c. have themselves taken up, and misled us Europeans, with the Chinese Conceit, that all those People, whether Kalmucks or Mungals, must go under the Denomination of Tartars:

Who Mongols and Kalmyks

Read itPhilipp Johann von Strahlenberg (English translation, 1738)
