Gog and Magog on the Onon River

An anonymous description of the Mongols printed next in Weber sets out their lands, from Lake Khövsgöl east to the sand desert, to the Argun, and north-west to the rivers Onon and Sikoi, a country which, it says, has been said since ancient times to be inhabited by Gog and Magog. The same description goes on to the lamas, the Dalai Lama and whether he could be Prester John.

further to the land of Argun, and from there again north-west to the rivers Onon and Sikoi, which, as has been said from ancient times, is inhabited by Gog and Magog.

Mongol chiefs made Knights of Malta

Near Irkutsk, Holman heard of a settlement of 'Mongul Tartars' at Tunka near the Chinese frontier, whose chiefs had been made Knights of Malta by Emperor Paul (Paul was Grand Master of the Order from 1798 to 1801). Holman doubts they could have understood the honour beyond the star and ribbon.

The Emperor Paul made some of the chiefs of these Mongul Tartars Knights of Malta! It must be difficult to conceive how these people would contemplate the nature or duties of such a creation, or appreciate the honour conferred upon them

Who Buryats and Mongols ('Mongul Tartars')

Heard itJames Holman (1825)

Keep in mind. Reported to Holman, not seen; his dismissive comment is his own. Holman puts 'Toonga' about sixty miles from Irkutsk; the identification with Tunka is ours.

Raw rhubarb stalks that grip the throat

In an appendix on rhubarb, Coxe (credited to Pallas) says the Tartars ate the leaf stalks of wild rhubarb raw, and that outsiders who tried them got a spasm of the throat at every meal until they grew used to it. A small, odd detail of steppe diet.

The stalks of the leaves are eaten raw by the Tartars: they produce upon most persons, who are unaccustomed to them, a kind of spasmodic contraction of the throat, which goes off in a few hours; it returns however at every meal, until they become habituated to this kind of diet.

Who 'Tartars' of the Baikal region (probably Buryats or Mongols)

Read itWilliam Coxe (from information supplied by P. S. Pallas)

Keep in mind. Which people Coxe means by 'Tartars' here is not stated.

Remains of large 'Tartar ovens' near Chinggis's birthplace

In the Onon valley east of Lake Baikal, Cochrane passed Kondou, 'an ancient place' held to be the birthplace of the 'conqueror of China' (Chinggis Khan). There he saw 'many remains of large Tartar ovens', but gives no more detail, being more interested in a lively 93-year-old woman who cooked him dinner.

I saw many remains of large Tartar ovens, but nothing that excited my curiosity so much as an old lady of ninety-three years of age.

Who Mongols (the 'conqueror of China')

Saw itJohn Dundas Cochrane (1824)

Keep in mind. Cochrane does not describe the 'ovens' or say how he knew they were Tartar. The Onon valley is indeed linked with Chinggis Khan's birth in Mongol tradition.

The ruined city of Taiminzin

Discussing old silver mines on the Argun, Strahlenberg quotes the Dutch envoy Isbrand Ides that the Jurchens and Mongols once mined silver there. He then identifies the 'large and ancient city' of Alackzin in Abulghazi's Tartar history with the ruined city of Taiminzin that Ides described, once the seat of 'Chan Utay'.

But that large and ancient City, which this Tartarian Author calls Alackzin, is the now ruin'd City of Taiminzin; … See the Description which Isbrand Ides gives of the ancient City of Taiminzin, (p. 75.) formerly the Residence of Chan Utay:

Who Jurchens and Mongols ('Niuchaeans and Mungals')

Read itPhilipp Johann von Strahlenberg, author's footnote (English translation, 1738)

Keep in mind. An identification made from books (Ides and Abulghazi), not from a visit.
