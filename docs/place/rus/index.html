CitiesKozelskSpring 1238

Kozelsk, the 'evil city'

Batu turned back from the road to Novgorod and besieged the small town of Kozelsk, whose prince was a boy. The townsmen held out for seven weeks, sallied out, cut up the siege engines and killed three sons of a 'temnik' (commander of ten thousand). The chronicle says that afterwards the Tatars would not call it Kozelsk but 'the evil city'.

From then on among the Tatars they did not dare to call it Kozelsk, but called it the evil city, because they had fought at that town for seven weeks, and three sons of a temnik were killed; the Tatars searched for them and did not find them among the multitude of dead bodies.

The nameTorzhok, Tver Oblast1828

'Tatar' should mean Kazan

At Torzhok, famous for embroidered Russia-leather boots, Erman notes that the Russians learned the craft from the Tatars and still call the boots 'Kasan boots'. For Russians, he says, Kazan was the land of the Tatars, and he complains that Western European scholars too often call peoples 'Tatars' who had nothing to do with Kazan.

The Russians learned this art from the Tatars, whom they soon surpassed, and the name "Kasan boots," now usually given to the boots and shoes made in Torjok, points to a Tatar origin; for among the Russians, Kasan was originally the land of the Tatars; and the learned men of Western Europe too often give the name of Tatars erroneously to tribes who had no connexion with Kasan.

Adolph Erman (translated by W. D. Cooley, 1848)

Daily lifeRostov1262 (6770)

Tax farmers driven out by the town assemblies

The Horde's tribute had been farmed out to Muslim merchants, who bought the right to collect it and charged interest. In 1262 the towns called their assemblies (veche) and drove them out of Rostov, Vladimir, Suzdal and Iaroslavl. The same entry tells of a renegade monk, Zosima, who became a Muslim under a Tatar official named Kutlubii and was killed in Iaroslavl.

[God] put fury into the hearts of the Christians, who, not bearing the violence of the pagans, summoned the veche and drove them out of the towns, out of Rostov, out of Vladimir, out of Suzdal, out of Iaroslavl; for those accursed besermen had bought up the tribute, and from that they worked great ruin on the people

Daily lifeRus (reported)1244 to 1245

A Russian archbishop's report: food, prayer and women archers

Peter, an archbishop who had fled Russia before the Tartars, was questioned in the West, and Matthew records his answers. The Tartars, he said, eat horse and dog meat and in need even human flesh, but cooked, not raw; they drink blood, water and milk. Each morning they raise their hands to heaven to worship the Creator, toss the first morsel into the air and pour the first drink on the ground, and claim John the Baptist as their leader. Their women fight, and are excellent archers.

They eat the flesh of horses and dogs and other abominable things, even human flesh in necessity, yet not raw but cooked. They drink blood, water and milk. … Every morning they raise their hands to heaven, worshipping the Creator. When eating, they throw the first morsel into the air; when drinking, they first pour part onto the ground, in veneration of the Creator. And they say they have Saint John the Baptist as their leader. … The women are warriors, and especially excellent archers.

CitiesVolodymyr (Vladimir-Volynskii)1259 to 1260 (6768 to 6769)

Burundai: 'tear down all your town walls'

The Tatar general Burundai arrived in Volhynia during a princely wedding and told the princes: if you are at peace with me, raze your towns. Lev dismantled Danilov and Stozhek and then Lviv; Vasilko razed Kremenets and Lutsk. Vladimir-in-Volhynia was too big to pull down quickly, so Vasilko had it burned overnight, and next day Burundai came to see it burned 'with his own eyes' and dined with the prince.

'If you are at peace with me, then tear down all your towns.' Lev tore down Danilov and Stozhek, and from there he sent and tore down Lviv, and Vasilko sent and tore down Kremenets and Lutsk.

Original wording

«оже есте мои мирници, розмечѣте же городы своѣ всѣ.» Левъ розмета Даниловъ, и Стожекъ, оттолѣ же пославъ Лвовъ розмета, а Василко пославъ Кремянець розмета и Луческъ.

Daily lifeVladimir-SuzdalWinter 1257 to 1258 (6765)

The census: tens, hundreds, thousands and ten-thousands

The Horde sent census takers who counted the Suzdal, Riazan and Murom lands and set up officers over tens, hundreds, thousands and ten-thousands, the Mongol decimal system, before returning to the Horde. Clergy were not counted, in line with the Mongol practice of exempting religious men from tax.

That winter the census takers came, and counted all the Suzdal land, and the Riazan and the Murom, and appointed chiefs of ten, of a hundred, of a thousand and of ten thousand, and went off to the Horde; only they did not count abbots, monks, priests, choir clergy, whoever looks to the holy Mother of God and to the bishop.

The nameMongolia and Rus13th century (argued 1828)

The Russians named the Mongols 'Tatars', and Europe copied them

Bichurin says the invaders of 1237 to 1240 were Mongols of the 'Tatan' confederation, among whom the Tatar tribe near the Argun was only one conquered clan. In his view the Russians made the first mistake by calling the whole army 'Tatars', Carpini copied it through poor interpreters, and geographers without further inquiry called the whole belt from the Caspian to the Pacific 'Great Tartary'.

And so, in my opinion, the Russians, calling the Tatans 'Tatars' at their first invasion, were the first to err and passed this error on to the western peoples. … and geographers, without further investigation, called the whole belt from the Caspian Sea to the Eastern Sea Great Tartary.

Working translation

Original wording

И такъ, по моему мнѣнію, Россіяне при первомъ нашествіи Татаньцевъ, назвавъ ихъ Татарами, первые погрѣшили и сообщили сію погрѣшность западнымъ народамъ. … а Географы, безъ дальняго изслѣдованія, всю полосу отъ Каспійскаго моря до Восточнаго назвали Великою Татаріею.

Iakinf (N. Ya. Bichurin), 1828

About Mongols of the 13th century

Keep in mind

Bichurin's own argument; he is disputing Klaproth, who held that the Mongols were rightly called Tatars. 'Tatan' is his rendering of Chinese Dada.

Fourteen towns in one February

and from there they captured all that country and many towns, as far as Torzhok, and there is no place, nor village, and few hamlets, where they did not make war in the Suzdal land; and they took 14 towns, besides slobodas and pogosts, in the one month of February

The stone cathedral that became a furnace

When the new town fell, the bishop, the grand prince's wife, daughters-in-law, grandchildren, boyars and townspeople shut themselves in the stone Cathedral of the Mother of God, built by the Suzdal princes. The Tatars set it on fire. Then they forced the doors, killed the survivors and stripped the wonder-working icon of its gold, silver and gems. The passage shows a Rus-built stone church and who ruined it.

The Tatars forced open the church doors, and saw some who had perished by fire, and others they put to death with weapons; they plundered the holy Mother of God [cathedral], and stripped the wonderful icon adorned with gold and silver and precious stones

CitiesTorzhokMarch 1238

Torzhok's two-week siege and the road to Novgorod

After Moscow, Pereyaslavl, Yuryev, Dmitrov, Volok and Tver, the Tatars besieged Torzhok. As at other towns, they fenced it with a palisade and battered it with siege engines for two weeks while Novgorod sent no help. They took it on 5 March and chased the fugitives along the Seliger road 'cutting down people like grass' as far as Ignach Krest, 100 versts from Novgorod. There they turned back, which the chronicler credits to God and St Sophia.

the lawless ones came and surrounded Torzhok, on the Sunday of Orthodoxy, and fenced it all round with a palisade, as they took other cities, and the accursed ones battered it there with siege engines for two weeks … Then the accursed godless ones pursued from Torzhok along the Seliger road as far as Ignach Krest, cutting down all the people like grass, to within 100 versts of Novgorod

CitiesVladimirFebruary 1238

The fall of Vladimir: palisade, siege engines, a burning church

The chronicle describes the Tatar method at Vladimir. They surrounded the city with a palisade (tyn), set up siege engines (poroki), took the city and set it on fire. The prince's family and the bishop, who had taken monastic vows as the end approached, shut themselves in the gallery of the cathedral. The Tatars broke down the doors, piled up timber and burned the church, suffocating everyone inside.

And the lawless Ishmaelites drew near the city, and surrounded the city in force, and fenced it all round with a palisade … having set up siege engines they took the city and set it on fire … they ran into the church of the Holy Mother of God and shut themselves in the gallery; and the pagans, breaking down the doors, set fire to the church, having piled up timber, and suffocated them all.

Original wording

CitiesVladimirFebruary 1238

Vladimir besieged: scaffolds, catapults and a palisade

After parading the captured prince Vladimir Iurevich before the Golden Gate so his brothers on the walls could see him, the Tatars camped around the city. On the Saturday before Lent they built scaffolds and set up 'poroki' (stone-throwing engines), and in one night surrounded the whole city with a timber palisade. The next morning they attacked. It is a precise picture of Mongol siege method.

On Meatfare Saturday they began to prepare scaffolds and set up siege engines until evening, and in the night they fenced the whole city of Vladimir round with a palisade; on Meatfare Sunday after matins they advanced on the city, on the 7th of February, the feast of the holy martyr Theodore Stratelates.

Original wording

And whatever common people were seized, with wives and children, he sent all of them away. CitiesTver1327

Tver rises against the khan's cousin

Uzbek Khan's nephew Shchelkan came to Tver as envoy. The Moscow chronicle says he meant to kill the Tver princes, rule Tver himself, put Tatar princes into the Rus towns and bring the Christians to the 'Tatar faith' (Islam). Tver rose, and Shchelkan was burned in the prince's palace; the Horde merchants in the town were killed too. Uzbek's revenge helped Moscow's rise.

There came to Tver a very powerful envoy, the tsarevich Shchelkan, from the Horde, from Tsar Uzbek; he was Uzbek's nephew, and he wanted to kill the Tver princes and himself sit as prince in Tver, and to set his own Tatar princes over the Russian towns, and he wanted to bring the Christians into the Tatar faith.

Original wording

Пріиде во Тверь посолъ силенъ зѣло царевичь Щелканъ изъ Орды, отъ царя Азбяка; бѣ же сей братаничь царю Азбяку, хотя князей Тверскихъ избити, а самъ сѣсти на княженіи во Твери, а своихъ князей Татарскихъ хотя посажати по Рускимъ градомъ, а христіанъ хотяше привести въ Татарскую вѣру.

Stone figures 'in Tatar shape' on the Don mounds

Near the Cossack settlement of Babskaya on the Don, Gmelin saw two stone statues standing on burial mounds, one of a man and one of a woman 'in Tatar shape', so weathered that little more could be made out than that they were carved from gypsum stone. Daily lifeTver1327

Tver kills the Horde's envoy, and a Tatar army lays the land waste

In 1327 Prince Alexander of Tver killed many Tatars in Tver and other towns, after the mighty envoy Shevkal had come from the Horde with a large Tatar retinue. That winter a huge Tatar army took Tver, Kashin and the Torzhok district and 'laid the whole Russian land waste'. Novgorod escaped by paying the Tatar envoys 2000 in silver and sending gifts to the commanders.

Prince Alexander Mikhailovich killed many Tatars in Tver and in other towns … for a mighty envoy had come from the Horde, named Shevkal, with a multitude of Tatars. … That same winter a Tatar army came, a very great multitude, and took Tver, and Kashin, and the Torzhok district, and, to put it simply, laid the whole Russian land waste

A Chinese picture of the far north-west: white nights and blue-eyed horsemen

The Chinese annal describing the Mongol attack on the 'tribes of the state of Kincha' (Kipchak) says the land lay 30,000 li from China, that its summer nights were so short the sun barely set before rising, that it bred fine horses, and that its people were brave, with blue eyes and reddish hair. Bichurin notes on the facing page that this is the Mongol invasion of Russia in 1237.

In summer the nights are extremely short. The sun barely sets and at once rises again. This country produces excellent horses … They are courageous and brave … Their eyes are blue, their hair reddish.

The baskak's two settlements empty overnight

Akhmat had founded two 'slobodas' (tax-free trading settlements) in the Kursk land and left two brothers to guard and fortify them. After a Rus prince ambushed their men, the brothers fled to Kursk and on the next day the whole of both settlements scattered. It is a rare glimpse of settlements founded under Horde protection inside Rus, and of how fragile they were.

That same year, after Easter on St Thomas Sunday, those two besermen brothers fled to Kursk, and on the morrow, Monday, all that sloboda ran away, and the other one too; and so both besermen slobodas dispersed.

Working translation

Original wording

Того же лѣта, по Велицѣ дни въ недѣлю Фомину, побѣгоста 2 братеника та бесерменина къ Курьску, а на заутрѣи въ понедѣльникъ побѣже вся свобода та, и другая; и тако розоидошася обѣ свободѣ бесерменьскиѣ.

Laurentian Chronicle, PSRL vol. 1 (1846)

About Akhmat's 'besermen' (Muslim) settlers; Rus princes of Rylsk and Lipovichsk

Keep in mind

The manuscript spells sloboda as 'svoboda'.
