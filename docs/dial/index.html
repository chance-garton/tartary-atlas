1253 to 1255 · William of Rubruck · No map: a journey

A people, not yet a place

William of Rubruck rides from the Black Sea to the Mongol court at Karakorum and back, and writes of the Tartars as a people on the move, with their tents, carts and herds. The line is his road. On the world maps of his century in this atlas, the word is not yet lettered as a country.

about 1300 · Hereford Cathedral · The Hereford world map

The great wall maps do not know the name

The largest medieval world map to survive was drawn while the first reports from the Mongol courts were already in Europe, yet it carries none of them. Its northern Asia is still Scythia, with Gog and Magog walled in by Alexander. It is a round picture of the world with east at the top, so it cannot be laid on the real earth. Open it to see.

1516 · Martin Waldseemüller · Carta marina, sheet 3

Three Tartarys around the Caspian

[Carta marina navigatoria Portugallen navigationes ...] sheet 3

This sheet letters TARTARIA three times. The largest stands on the steppe north of the Caspian Sea, over the tent of the khan, and two more east of the Caspian are tied to older country names: TARTARIA CORASINE (Khwarezm) and TARTARIA TORQVESTEN (Turkestan). North of the Sea of Azov the plain is called CAMPESTRIA TARTAR, and note after note across the sheet says that Russians, Armenians and Persians live under the Tartars.

The whole sheet at the Library of CongressThis map in the atlas

Laid on the earth by 39 matched places. Left out one at a time, a place lands about 182 km from where it belongs; the worst lands 596 km off. That is the mapmaker's error, kept on purpose.

What is uncertain (6 notes)
  • The heading TERRA MONGAL ET QUE VERA TARTARIA DICITUR is not on this sheet; it must stand on the neighbouring sheet to the east (sheet 4), which is a different picture. The title given here is only the running head printed over this sheet, and the map's main title stands on another sheet.
  • The western two thirds can be laid on the earth honestly: the Black Sea, the Levant coast, Russia and the west and north of the Caspian come from sea charts and reports and fit to within a few hundred km. The sheet sits turned about 40 degrees, which is only the projection at this far western longitude, not a fault.
  • East of the Caspian not one place can be matched: there is no Samarkand, Bukhara or Urgench, and the country is drawn from Ptolemy with rivers and old province names. The eastern third, where TARTARIA CORASINE and TARTARIA TORQVESTEN stand, is carried along by the western fit, so its position on the earth is only a rough guide.
  • The Caspian is drawn as an oval lying east to west, as on the old sea charts, so its true north to south shape cannot be matched. The places around it (Volga delta, Kura mouth, Ural mouth, south shore) pull against each other.
  • Several place readings are not sure: Sarai is read from the town lettered Sala ciuit. on the Volga, the river name dayr fl. is read as the Yaik (Ural), and Grassan is read as Kashan. Derbent could not be found by name, Astrakhan is lettered only as agitarcam in a list of names at the Volga delta, and CASANA (Kazan) is lettered as a country near the Don with no town sign.
  • The second line of CAMPESTRIA TARTAR reads TAR TAR and seems cut short (for Tartarorum or Tartarie). A small name east of the Sea of Azov, Tacaria over Tarmacho, may be a slip for Tartaria but is lettered with a c, so it was not marked; nor were the many written notes that name the Tartars, such as sub mandato Tartarorum and sub tributo tartarorum.

1562 · Anthony Jenkinson · Russia, Muscovy and Tartary

One word, letter by letter, across the steppe

RVSSIAE, MOSCOVIAE ET TARTARIAE DESCRIPTIO. Auctore Antonio Ienkensono Anglo, edita Londini Anno 1562 & dedicata illustriss. D. Henrico Sydneo Wallie…

The word TARTARIA is lettered once, in big letters spread one by one across the open steppe from the Don and lower Volga eastward past the Yaik toward the country above Tashkent. It is not divided into parts; smaller names such as Nagaia, Cassacki, Turkmen and Kirges sit inside and around it, and the far north is given to the Samoyeds instead. A note beside Samarkand calls that city the former capital of all Tartary.

The whole sheet at the Library of CongressThis map in the atlas

Laid on the earth by 47 matched places. Left out one at a time, a place lands about 127 km from where it belongs; the worst lands 676 km off. That is the mapmaker's error, kept on purpose.

What is uncertain (4 notes)
  • The picture used is a 1914 black and white Russian reproduction that follows Ortelius's 1570 reduced plate, not the 1562 London sheet. Fine lettering is blurred in places.
  • The word also appears in the title and inside two written notes, the one at Samarkand and one saying Little Khorasan was taken 'adiuvantibus Tartaris 1558'. These are blocks of text and were not marked.
  • The Ob, Sibir, Tashkent and Kashgar came from hearsay and sit far from their true places. The Caspian is drawn wider east to west than north to south, so its shore towns fit badly.
  • Vardo and Kola are read from a dark, crowded coast, so their spots may be slightly off.

1570 · Abraham Ortelius · Tartary, or the kingdom of the Great Khan

The Great Khan's whole realm under one word

TARTARIAE SIVE MAGNI CHAMI REGNI typus

Ortelius letters TARTARIA once, in large capitals across the steppe north-east of the Caspian, and adds in small script that it takes in Asiatic Sarmatia and both Scythias of the ancient writers. He does not divide it into Great, Little or Chinese Tartary; the rest of the country is filled with the names of hordes and with Marco Polo's kingdoms such as Tangut, Camul, Tenduc and Cathay, all of them, by the title and the note in the lower cartouche, under the Great Khan.

The whole sheet at the Library of CongressThis map in the atlas

Laid on the earth by 35 matched places. Left out one at a time, a place lands about 247 km from where it belongs; the worst lands 1,502 km off. That is the mapmaker's error, kept on purpose.

What is uncertain (6 notes)
  • The word Tartary also occurs inside blocks of text that were not marked: the title cartouche (TARTARIAE SIVE MAGNI CHAMI REGNI typus), the lower left cartouche ('Continet hec tabula oem Tartariam'), the note by the Great Khan's tent ('lingua Tartarorum') and the Altai note ('oes Tartarie imperatores').
  • A town on the China coast is lettered Tartaho. It is a town name, not the word Tartary, and was not marked.
  • The interior east of Samarkand and the whole north-east coast (Scythicum prom., Tabin, Arsareth, the Strait of Anian) are legend, so nothing fixes the sheet there. The fit rests on the Black Sea, the Caspian, the Russian north coast to the Ob, the China coast and Japan.
  • No fitting place could be found in the south-west and south, where Persia and India are covered by a cartouche and blank lettering, so that side is held only by Tabriz, Merv and Termez. Moscow and Bukhara are not on this sheet, Canton lies off the bottom edge, and Korea and Novaya Zemlya are not drawn.
  • The Caspian is drawn as an east to west oval and Hangzhou (Quinsai) far north of Ningbo, so those places land far off however the sheet is laid.
  • Merv, Termez and Margilan are read from Gastaldi-style names (Maru, Terment, Merglan) and are not sure.

1626 · John Speed · Asia with the islands adjoining

The name reaches the Arctic sea

ASIA with the Islands adioyning described, the atire of the people, & Townes of importance, all of them newly augmented by I

Speed letters TARTARIA in large spaced capitals across the country between the Volga and the Ob, running up from north of the Caspian Sea. The word comes back three more times: as a tribe (Kaski Tartari) in the steppe below it, as a town called Tartar in the empty far north-east, and on the water as The Tartarian Sea along the whole Arctic coast. A note beside the Great Wall says it was built against sudden inroads of the Tartars, and one of the costume figures in the border is labelled Tartarian.

The whole sheet at the Library of Congress

Laid on the earth by 39 matched places. Left out one at a time, a place lands about 223 km from where it belongs; the worst lands 1,010 km off. That is the mapmaker's error, kept on purpose.

What is uncertain (6 notes)
  • The tribe name under TARTARIA has a meridian line through its third letter. It is read here as 'Kaski Tartari' (long s, probably the Kazakhs); earlier notes gave 'Kalbi Tartari', and 'Kalki' is also possible.
  • The note on the Great Wall and the border figure lettered TARTARIAN hold the word, but they are a text note and a border decoration, so they were not marked.
  • North-east Asia is drawn by guess (Mongul, Tenduc, Tartar, a coast running to a strait with America), so nothing fixes the fit there beyond Beijing and northern Japan.
  • The Caspian is the old east to west oval, and Samarkand is set far east. Both are true places that the mapmaker misplaced.
  • The picture is a photograph of an open book, with a gutter and some page curve, and the plate edge is slightly out of square.
  • The town marks for Mogadishu and Macao are slightly uncertain.

about 1680 · Frederick de Wit · Great Tartary, the Mogul empire, Japan and China

Great Tartary, from the Ob to the Pacific

MAGNÆ TARTARIÆ, MAGNI MOGOLIS IMPERII, IAPONIÆ et CHINÆ, Nova Descriptio

The words TARTARIA MAGNA, Great Tartary, are spread one letter at a time across the whole of inner Asia, from the river Ob to the Pacific coast and from the Arctic shore down to the Great Wall of China. Inside that space the sheet names many separate peoples and kingdoms, among them the Kazakh Tartars, Chagatai, Turkestan, the Mongols, Cathay and Tangut, and the Arctic sea above is called the Tartar Sea. Siberia is only a small name by the Ob, and the far north-east is filled with names from Marco Polo and from legend, such as Magog and a town and river called Tartar.

The whole sheet at the Library of CongressThis map in the atlas

Laid on the earth by 49 matched places. Left out one at a time, a place lands about 163 km from where it belongs; the worst lands 980 km off. That is the mapmaker's error, kept on purpose.

What is uncertain (6 notes)
  • Short Latin notes on the map body also use the word and were not marked: 'Castrum contra irruptionem Tartarorum', 'eunt ac redeunt Tartari' and 'Regnum Tartariae Sepulchra sunt', besides a note on the trumpeters.
  • 'Tart.' after ZACATAY is cut short on the sheet, so it may mean Tartaria or Tartari.
  • North of China and east of the Ob almost nothing on the sheet is real: the Arctic and north-east coasts are guesses, there is no Baikal, Aral or Amur, and the towns come from Marco Polo. The fit there rests on the Ob and Yenisei mouths and on the Dutch survey of Yezo and Sakhalin at the east edge, so the place of the TARTARIA lettering on the earth is loose.
  • The Volga mouth and Astrakhan fall off the left edge, so the west side is held by the Ural river mouth, Samara, Saratov and Bolgar.
  • Tashkent is lettered far to the east of Samarkand and was kept out of the fit.
  • Two places stay far off because the sheet itself is wrong there: Verkhoturye (about 1000 km off) and the south-east corner of the Caspian (about 500 km off).

1706 · Guillaume Delisle · Map of Tartary

Split in three by who rules it

Carte de Tartarie, dressée sur les relations de plusieurs voyageurs de differentes nations et sur quelques observations qui ont été faites dans ce…

Delisle uses Tartary as the name for nearly all of northern and central Asia and splits it by who rules it: Muscovite Tartary across the whole of Siberia, Independent Tartary over the Kalmyk and Uzbek country east of the Caspian, and Chinese Tartary from the Mongol lands to the Pacific, with Western and Eastern Tartary lettered inside the Chinese part. Little Tartary sits apart on the steppe north of the Crimea. Within these broad names the sheet letters kingdoms and peoples of its own, such as the Kingdom of Siberia, the Kalmyks and the Mongols, and it names three smaller groups as Tartars: those of Tyumen, of the Kuban and of Dagestan.

The whole sheet at the Library of CongressThis map in the atlas

Laid on the earth by 40 matched places. Left out one at a time, a place lands about 276 km from where it belongs; the worst lands 1,103 km off. That is the mapmaker's error, kept on purpose.

What is uncertain (5 notes)
  • North-east Siberia is drawn from hearsay: the Lena, Yakutsk and the Amur mouth sit six to seven degrees too far south and the coast beyond the Lena is guesswork. The fit is poor there, with errors of 800 to 1100 km, though the places themselves are certain.
  • Lake Baikal is drawn very small and the Angara towns are crowded against it, so Irkutsk lands about 750 km off.
  • Novaya Zemlya is joined to the mainland and no Kamchatka is drawn, so there are no sound fitting points on the far northern and north-eastern edges beyond the Yenisei mouth and the Tugur mouth.
  • The first T of TARTARIE MOSCOVITE, just west of the Urals near Solikamsk, is half hidden by the border colour and reads almost like an I. Its position is taken from the spacing of the letters.
  • The lower left corner is taken up by the title cartouche, so the south-west has no fitting points below Derbent and west of Khiva.

1739 · Johann Matthias Hase, for the Homann Heirs · The Russian Empire and Tartary, great and little

Promised in the title, scarce on the map

Carte generale d'Empire de Russie et de Tartarie grande et petite en Europe et Asie.

The titles promise all Tartary, great and little, yet on the map itself the word is used sparingly and is spelled Tataria, without the first r. It is lettered large only twice: Little Tartary north of the Black Sea, and a shortened Tataric. laid over Manchuria under China. Everywhere else it appears only in small notes naming single peoples as Tartars, while the land is lettered Siberia, Yakutia, Turkestan and the Kalmyks.

The whole sheet at the Library of CongressThis map in the atlas

Laid on the earth by 42 matched places. Left out one at a time, a place lands about 123 km from where it belongs; the worst lands 424 km off. That is the mapmaker's error, kept on purpose.

What is uncertain (6 notes)
  • The Library of Congress scan is dark and small, so the lettering was read on a sharper copy of the same plate at the BnF (Gallica btv1b5963132j).
  • The two copies are different issues: the Gallica copy has a dedication at upper left and the imprint Norib. A. 1739, while this sheet has the French title there and an imprint Norimbergae without a year. A change made to the lettering between the issues would not have been seen, though none was noticed where the two were compared.
  • A search of the whole map body found no Great, Russian, Muscovite, Asiatic or Independent Tartary lettered across Siberia or the steppe. 'Grande' and 'majoris et Asiaticae' appear only in the titles.
  • The large Manchurian word is TATARIC, cut short on the sheet, with no second large word. Whether a full stop follows the C could not be seen.
  • Two readings are still partial: the small Tarta.. by the Volga, whose second line is hidden, and the first letter of PORVTI.
  • The far north-east (Chukchi cape, Kamchatka) and the Tarim towns sit 250 to 430 km off, because the sheet itself is wrong there.

1796 · John Wilkes · Modern Asia

Russia's share is now Siberia

MODERN ASIA.

This small sheet writes Tartary only twice. Western Tartary is the pink country between the Caspian and the Gobi, holding Turkestan, Bucharia, the Dzungars and Kashgar, with Tibet coloured as part of the same block. Chinese Tartary is the green country of the Mongols and Manchus north of China, while everything Russian is called Siberia and split into Tobolsk, Yakutsk and other governments with no Tartary name at all.

The whole sheet at the Library of CongressThis map in the atlas

Laid on the earth by 75 matched places. Left out one at a time, a place lands about 119 km from where it belongs; the worst lands 532 km off. That is the mapmaker's error, kept on purpose.

What is uncertain (6 notes)
  • The scan is small, so towns can only be placed to within 40 km or so on the ground.
  • Each name is set on two lines: WESTERN over TARTARY, and CHINESE north of the Amur with TARTARY far to the south of it. No Independent or Eastern Tartary is lettered.
  • Little Tibet as well as Tibet shares the pink colour of Western Tartary, so the colour block is wider than the name strictly says.
  • Arabia and the Red Sea fit worst, with Mecca and Bab el Mandeb 500 to 600 km off: the sheet draws the Red Sea too far west and sets Mecca deep inland.
  • The Arctic coast from Novaya Zemlya to the Lena is loosely drawn, so the Gulf of Ob, Taimyr and Lena mouth are placed only approximately.
  • Okhotsk has no town symbol and the head of Penzhina Bay is unnamed; both are read from the shape of the coast.

1818 · John Pinkerton · Asia

Two Tatarys left, and a new spelling

ASIA. Drawn under the direction of Mr. Pinkerton by L. Hebert. Neele sculpt. 352, Strand. Published by Dobson Philada.

Pinkerton spells the word Tatary, without the first R, and uses it three times. Independent Tatary lies over the Kirghiz steppe and Bukhara, Chinese Tatary runs in one long line from Khotan through Mongolia and Mandshuria to the Amur, and the strait beside Sakhalin is the Channel of Tatary. North of them there is no Russian Tatary at all: the whole north is Siberia, divided into Russian governments.

The whole sheet at the Library of Congress

Laid on the earth by 68 matched places. Left out one at a time, a place lands about 64 km from where it belongs; the worst lands 523 km off. That is the mapmaker's error, kept on purpose.

What is uncertain (4 notes)
  • Earlier notes on this map gave the spelling as TARTARY. On the sheet each of the three names has six letters, T A T A R Y.
  • The last word of CHANNEL OF TATARY is small and lies over sea shading. It is read as TATARY by its letter count and by the map's own spelling elsewhere.
  • SIBERIA and MANDSHURIA are followed letter by letter among other spaced government names, so one or two of their letters may belong to a neighbouring name.
  • East Cape, the Lena delta, the centre of the Aral Sea and the southern tip of Novaya Zemlya are placed by eye on drawn coasts, so they are only approximate.

Independent Tartary, already shared out

PERSIA, ARABIA, TARTARY, AFGHANISTAN

The word stands twice on this sheet. 1849 · S. Augustus Mitchell · Asia

One modest line by the Aral Sea

ASIA.

By 1849 the word Tartary has shrunk to one modest line of spaced capitals in central Asia, running from just west of the Sea of Aral across Khokan to the edge of Soongaria, with no border or colour of its own. It is threaded among the names that have taken its place: Turcomania, Khiva, Great Bucharia, Khokan, Kirguis and Turkestan. Farther east the sheet letters Soongaria, Mongolia, Little Bucharia, Mandshuria and Chinese Empire with no Chinese Tartary at all, and the only other survival is the small Channel of Tartary between Saghalin and the mainland.

The whole sheet at the Library of Congress

Laid on the earth by 61 matched places. Left out one at a time, a place lands about 96 km from where it belongs; the worst lands 452 km off. That is the mapmaker's error, kept on purpose.

What is uncertain (6 notes)
  • The third letter of TARTARY (the first R) falls inside the wave shading of the Sea of Aral and can hardly be seen. The word is read from the other six letters, which are evenly spaced along one line.
  • TARTARY is lettered no larger than TURCOMANIA beside it, and much smaller than RUSSIA, SIBERIA or CHINESE EMPIRE.
  • No imprint, date or publisher line is lettered on this sheet, only the title ASIA. and the plate number 63 in the lower right margin. The atlas title and date come from the catalogue.
  • Kashgar is the worst place in the fit, about 450 km off: the sheet draws it too far north-west, against Khokan. Samarcand is also set too near Bokhara.
  • The south-east corner (Guam, Palau, Yap) and the south-west corner (Aden, Cape Guardafui) fit less well, 250 to 400 km off, mostly because few places hold the sheet there. No places were matched south of about 5 degrees north or in the Australia inset, so the fit is not to be trusted there.
  • A search of the whole map body found only the two Tartary letterings recorded. Very small italic names in the hatching of the seas could still hide a word.

1862 · Johnson and Ward · Johnson's Asia

The country is gone; a people and a gulf keep the word

JOHNSON'S ASIA BY JOHNSON AND WARD.

On this American family atlas sheet of 1862 the country called Tartary is gone. Where older maps spread that name, this one letters TOORKISTAN with Khiva, Bokhara and Khokand, and Siberia, Outer and Inner Mongolia and Manchooria, all inside the Russian Empire and the Chinese Empire. The word survives in only two small places: KIRGHIZ TARTARS, the name of a people on the steppe north of the Aral Sea, and the Gulf of Tartary, the water between Saghalin and the mainland.

The whole sheet at the Library of Congress

Laid on the earth by 80 matched places. Left out one at a time, a place lands about 46 km from where it belongs; the worst lands 590 km off. That is the mapmaker's error, kept on purpose.

What is uncertain (5 notes)
  • The whole map body was searched from the Arctic coast to the bottom edge, with a second closer look at the Kirghiz steppe, Toorkistan, Mongolia, Manchooria, the Amoor and Saghalin. Nothing else turned up: no Tartary, Chinese Tartary, Independent Tartary or Crim Tartary anywhere, on land or sea.
  • No imprint or copyright line is lettered on this sheet: the title block reads JOHNSON'S ASIA BY JOHNSON AND WARD and the corner carries the plate number 82. The place and date (New York, 1862) come from the library record, not from the sheet.
  • Most places land within 100 km of their true positions. The poorest are at the edges (East Cape and North East Cape on the Arctic rim, the north tip of Nova Zembla, the Kolyma mouth, and Aden in the south-west corner), while Lake Balkhash and Khokand are simply misplaced by the mapmaker.
  • The sheet runs south to about 5 degrees south (Sumatra, Borneo, New Guinea), but no places were matched south of Ceylon, so the fit is not held there.
  • The KIRGHIZ TARTARS lettering is very small, and Gulf of Tartary is in small italic.

1894 · R. Grossmann, Berlin · Asia

Only the water remembers

ASIEN.

The word Tartary is gone from the land on this German atlas map. The only trace is a small name in the water between the mainland and Sakhalin, Tatar'scher Golf, the Tartar Gulf. Where older maps wrote Tartary, this one writes Russisches Reich and Chinesisches Reich in big spaced letters, with West-Sibirien, Ost-Sibirien, Turkestan, Ost-Turkestan, Mongolei and Mandschurei beneath them.

The whole sheet at the Library of Congress

Laid on the earth by 45 matched places. Left out one at a time, a place lands about 67 km from where it belongs; the worst lands 472 km off. That is the mapmaker's error, kept on purpose.

What is uncertain (6 notes)
  • No year is printed anywhere on the sheet. It carries 'Verlag R. Grossmann, Berlin S.W. 29.' and 'Red. O. Brunckow, Berlin.', and the 1894 date rests on the Library of Congress catalogue entry for the atlas.
  • The content agrees with a date around then: the colour key has 'Deutsche Schutzherrschaft', Kaiser Wilhelms Land is on New Guinea, Burma is in the British colour, Wladiwostok is lettered, and no Siberian railway is drawn.
  • A search of the whole map body found no Tartarei, Tatarei or Tataren on land anywhere, including the Crimea, the Volga and Kazan country, and the steppe.
  • The small inset map of peoples ('Völkerkarte von ASIEN') was read as well. There is no Tataren in its key or on the inset itself.
  • 'Tatar'scher Golf' is small italic lettering crossed by a meridian line and crowded by 'Constantinowsk'. The ending before Golf reads as r (Tatar'scher) but could be n.
  • A fold runs down the middle of the sheet and the two halves do not meet perfectly there. The fit is worst at the far corners (East Cape at Bering Strait, Aden, Manila, the Arctic capes), and Arkhangelsk, Obdorsk, Fu-san, the south tip of Kyushu and Manila were placed from the coast beside the name because no clear town ring could be made out.

How the dial was made

Each sheet is a Library of Congress picture of a printed map. On every one, between 35 and 80 places were matched by an AI reader to where they really are: river mouths, capes, lakes and towns the mapmaker could have known from report. The sheet was then bent, stiffly, so those places fall as near their true spots as the sheet allows. A stiff bend keeps the map looking like itself, so its mistakes stay visible: an oval Caspian, a Siberia squeezed short, towns from Marco Polo set down by guess.

The coloured outlines mark where the word is lettered, traced on each sheet and carried across with it. Where a sheet rests on hearsay, the outline is only as good as the sheet. Between two stops the dial fades one sheet into the next; nothing is known about the years in between. No person has checked the matched places or the readings yet. The many Tartarys counts every map and book in the atlas that uses each name, and How it was made explains the rest.
